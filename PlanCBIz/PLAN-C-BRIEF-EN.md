# PLAN C — Business & Website Brief (EN)

**v1.0 · 30 July 2026** · Distilled from a 40+ question strategy session and 6 role-play simulations
**Purpose of this file:** a self-contained English brief for building the Plan C website. Anyone (or any session) starting from this file should be able to build the site without reading the Thai master document.

> **Language note:** the brief is in English. **All customer-facing copy on the site is Thai.** Thai copy blocks below are final and should be pasted verbatim; English around them is explanation only.

---

## 1. What Plan C is

A **creator-side advisory** for Thai content creators. It sells the thing brands never tell creators: why they were passed over, how selection actually works, and how to run creating as a profession rather than a hustle.

The founder is a **10+ year Thai influencer** with roughly **2 million combined followers** and a company that has taken brand budgets for a decade. He is **still on the brand side every day** — talking to clients, tracking trends, writing a daily intelligence feed for his team. Plan C is a **separate legal entity**.

**He is not revealing his name yet.** He can describe his experience but not identify his channel. This constraint shapes everything about the site.

---

## 2. The core insight the business rests on

From the founder's own brand-side experience, the single most common reason a creator's rate gets rejected:

> *"Big follower count, big view count — but nothing measurable, because the content is scattered and the identity is unclear. And they're asking for five figures."*

Which produces the central mechanism:

> **Clear identity → the brand can predict what the audience will do → you become measurable → they can pay you more**
> **Unclear identity → they can't predict → you're a gamble → they hire someone cheaper**

**Therefore: "unclear identity" is not an aesthetic problem. It is a pricing problem.** This is the argument that sells to people who don't believe in "finding your identity."

## 3. Business model

### Two-layer product

| | **A — Identity & algorithm (FREE)** | **B — Professional infrastructure (PAID)** |
|---|---|---|
| Why they come | Views dropping, don't know what to make, don't know who they are to their audience | Is this contract bad for me? How do I manage a team? **Why did the brand stop booking me?** |
| Delivered | 30–45 min session · one-page compass **(given away completely — nothing held back)** | Quarterly audit against a scorecard · contract checklists and document templates · **weekly brand-side intelligence feed** |
| Nature | One-time | **Regenerates daily** — which is why a monthly fee is defensible |

**Proven in simulation:** a creator who received only A left inspired and bought nothing. A creator who received B asked to buy without being pitched.
**→ The sale happens by itself only when B is actually delivered.** The free session must include a taste of B or the visitor never learns there is anything to buy.

### Who pays

**Creators pay. Brands do not.** No commission on any deal, from either side, ever. This is the structural guarantee that makes the whole proposition credible:

> **"An agency gets paid when you take the job. I get paid the same whether you take it or turn it down — which makes me the only person who can tell you which jobs not to take."**

### Qualifying customer

**Not follower count — "has completed at least one paid brand deal."** That person has a contract to fear, a team to manage, and a reputation to protect. A 30k-follower creator with steady deals is a better customer than a 300k creator with none.

### Pricing

| | Now | **When the 10th member joins** |
|---|---|---|
| First session | **Free** · 30–45 min · **once per person, never repeated** · homework required first | unchanged |
| Single session | **฿999** / 30 min ← *first product to go on sale* | **฿1,500** |
| Monthly membership | **฿2,000** — weekly feed + 2 × 45-min sessions | **฿3,500** · *the first 10 members keep ฿2,000 **for life*** |
| Content-calendar review + document pack | ~฿599 — **not launched yet** | |

Both prices move at the same single trigger. When raising, **justify by queue, not by results** — at 10 members there are no proven cases yet, and claiming results that don't exist travels fast in a small market.

### Ceiling (honest numbers)

Founder availability: **21:00–24:00 daily ≈ 90 hrs/month.** Real delivery load ≈ **2 hrs per member per month.**
→ **~19 members ≈ ฿38,000–51,500/month.** One-to-one sessions are a quality guarantee, not a growth engine; growth has to come from content and from assets that sell without him in the room.

## 4. Positioning

### Primary line (creator-facing)

> **"แบรนด์ไม่เคยบอกคุณว่าทำไมเขาไม่จ้างคุณต่อ"**
> *"Brands never tell you why they stopped hiring you."*

The product is **closing an information gap** — not teaching compliance.

### Brand-facing line — "two baskets"

> **"เกณฑ์ของเอเจนซีวัดว่าตัวเลขจริงไหม · เกณฑ์ของ Plan C วัดว่าครีเอเตอร์คนนั้นจะทำให้ทีมคุณเหนื่อยขึ้นหรือน้อยลง — คนละเรื่องกัน"**
> *"An agency's criteria measure whether the numbers are real. Ours measure whether this creator will make your team's job harder or easier. Different questions — use both."*

Takes nothing from anyone, needs no comparison, explains the entire business in one breath. **This belongs on the site, not just in meetings.**

### The line that actually closed a sale in simulation

> **"อาชีพนี้ยิ่งสูงยิ่งเหงา"** — *"The higher you climb in this job, the lonelier it gets."*

An outsider cannot say this. People who can afford to pay are buying the feeling that someone understands the structure of their life — not a feature list.

## 5. Who the website must convince

**A stranger who cannot verify who the founder is.** That is the whole design problem.

Therefore the site's persuasion budget goes to **evidence, not visuals**:
- Data blocks with real citations
- A published code of conduct nobody else in the market would dare publish
- A transparent assessment framework
- Plainly stated self-interest instead of claimed altruism

The mascot, the horizontal scroller and the animation are charm. **The 36% table and the six-line charter do the actual work.** When something has to be cut, cut the charm first.

## 6. Site scope — hard cap at 8 sections, single page

| # | Section | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hero** | State the information gap |
| 2 | **The room you're not in** | Make the invisible selection process visible |
| 3 | **Three data blocks** | Prove the author knows things — this is the credibility engine |
| 4 | **What we do (A / B)** | Explain the two layers; make clear A is given away free |
| 5 | **Six lines we don't cross** | The differentiator no competitor will copy |
| 6 | **Pricing** | Plain, no urgency tricks |
| 7 | **Questions you'll definitely be asked** | Including "who are you?" — answered with declared self-interest |
| 8 | **CTA** | Book the first conversation |

**Not yet:** courses · case studies · before/after tables · blog · team page · member counts
**Must be deleted from the current build:** every fabricated testimonial · brand logos from the founder's other company (not Plan C's to use, and an NDA risk) · the word "agency" describing Plan C

> **If the page grows past 8 sections, that is delay disguised as improvement.**

## 7. Content available now (all citable)

All of it comes from a completed research report on creator attrition and income (global + Southeast Asia). **Nobody in the Thai market has seen these numbers.**

**Block A — Thailand ranks last in ASEAN.** Share of creators earning above their own country's minimum wage, same instrument, same question, same fielding window:
Laos 87% · Vietnam 83% · Malaysia 80% · Myanmar 73% · Singapore 68% · Indonesia 63% · Cambodia 56% · Philippines 40% · **Thailand 36%**
*TikTok / Kadence International · 9 markets · N=3,412 · Aug–Sep 2022*
*Caveats that must be published alongside it: self-selected respondents; per-country sub-base undisclosed; directional, not precise.*

**Block B — why identical view counts pay differently.** Ad revenue per 1,000 views at 70% fill: ~<$1 for an Indian audience vs ~$5 for a US audience. And Thai channels are over-represented among channels carrying **no off-platform monetization links at all**.

**Block C — the ceiling nobody mentions.** Across complete-record Patreon and full-platform YouTube data 2013–2019: **under 2%** of people who opened an account or held a channel reached roughly $15,000/year; **under 0.1%** reached US median household income.

**Block D — the gap Plan C will fill.** *(strongest single piece of content available)*
> **No study anywhere has ever asked people who quit why they quit.** Every instrument surveys people still active — asking survivors how they survived, then teaching that to everyone else. The ones who disappeared were never asked.

**Publishing rule:** always attach the source and the limitation. This audience checks, and volunteering the caveat reads as more credible than hiding it.

## 8. Hard rules

**Never publish:**
- Any specific brand name, deal figure, or anything traceable to a client of the founder's other company. **Talk about patterns, never about cases.** This filter protects NDA exposure *and* makes the writing better.
- The number "฿1,500" as a public floor. Brands and agencies read the same groups; a published floor becomes the price they offer everyone, including creators worth ten times that. **Publish the method, give the number privately.**
- "Everyone buys followers" — in a brand meeting this reads as *"they knew and let it slide."*
- Any results claim before results exist.

**Never say:**
- "We help agencies love you" → say *"We help brands choose you, and help you stop depending on any single intermediary."*
- "I do this in my spare time" → delete, don't replace. It devalues the product.
- "AI-powered trend updates" → invites comparison with something free.
- "Consulting agency" → the word *agency* must not appear anywhere describing Plan C.
- A pain list followed by "talk to us" → that is the exact signature of a scam post in the Facebook groups this business recruits from, and it attracts the people who cannot pay.

**The six published commitments** *(a full page, not a footer)*
1. We take no money from brands to persuade creators to lower prices or drop conditions.
2. Creators always see everything we say on their behalf, without having to ask.
3. We never attach the Plan C name to any product or brand — this name underwrites everyone we vouch for.
4. We take no cut from lawyers, accountants or anyone else we refer you to.
5. We take no commission on your deals, wherever those deals come from.
6. The founder's other company gets no privileged access to Plan C member data, and Plan C does not route members to brands through it.

Plus, said on day one: *"If the honest answer is that you should stop doing this for a living, I will tell you."*
And said at signup, in writing: *"We do not guarantee you will get work."*

---

## 9. Voice

Direct, evidence-led, warm without being soft. Speaks like an insider who has stopped needing anything from you.

- **Never** hype, urgency tactics, or fear lists
- **Always** cite the number and the limitation together
- Says the uncomfortable thing early rather than late
- Declares self-interest openly: *"I'm building this and I need real cases before I scale. You're one of them"* lands better with skeptics than any claim of good intentions
- Treats "this isn't for you" as a successful outcome, not a failure

## 11. Critical path

**The website is now the only thing blocking everything else.** The founder decided to wait for it before posting anything publicly, because he will post from the Plan C account rather than a personal one.

That makes the single riskiest untested assumption — *will anyone respond at all* — wait on this build. **Which is the strongest possible argument for the 8-section cap.**
