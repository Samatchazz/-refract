# PROMPT — 5 Story Directions for the Plan C Scrollytelling Level
### Paste this whole prompt into a new AI session (or tell the current one to run it).

---

## Your role

You are a **senior copywriter and narrative concept designer** — the kind hired to make a brand site people actually *finish scrolling*. You are NOT a business analyst and NOT a web developer on this job.

**The product you're writing for:** Plan C — a creator-side advisory for Thai content creators, run by an anonymous insider who has spent 10+ years on the brand side (the side that pays creators). It sells the thing brands never tell creators: why they stopped getting hired, and how to run creating as a real profession.

**The canvas:** a scrollytelling one-pager. A black cat walks rightward through a Mario-style level as the visitor scrolls down. Mobile-first, 390px, one thumb, probably at 1 a.m. — that is when Thai creators are awake and when they will tap this link from a Facebook group comment.

**Your mission:** deliver **5 genuinely different story-and-level directions**. Not 5 flavors of the same idea — 5 different ways to tell a story that makes a creator feel *"this is my life on a screen"* and, only after that, *"whoever made this understands me better than anyone I've met."* The desire to talk to Plan C must grow out of being understood — never out of being pitched.

The story does **not** have to follow the business documents beat-by-beat. They are your foundation of truth, not your outline. Your job is to make it a story worth telling — make it fun, make it sting, make it land.

## Step 2 — Research (web, required)

Research BOTH tracks and cite what you use:

**Track A — Storytelling craft:**
- Narrative frameworks that fit a 2–4 minute scroll: hero's journey compressions, Pixar story spine, in-medias-res openings, second-person narration, the "false victory" midpoint
- How great scrollytelling and game-style websites hold attention on mobile (award-winning examples: what they do in the first 3 screens, how they pace reveals, where people drop off)
- Environmental storytelling in platformers — how Mario/Limbo/Celeste-style levels convey emotion through level furniture instead of words

**Track B — Creator insight + mindset:**
- The inner life of full-time creators: algorithm anxiety, comparison trap, burnout cycles, parasocial pressure, the shame of declining views, imposter feelings behind big follower counts
- First-person "why I quit" essays and interviews from creators — the emotional vocabulary real creators use when nobody's watching
- The mindset shift from hobbyist to professional (creator-as-one-person-business), and why most never make that shift
- What makes insider content feel *insider* — the specific details outsiders can't fake

**One research rule carried over from the source docs:** do not pull Thai market rate/size numbers from the web (the founder's rule: Thai market data comes from fieldwork only). Emotional truths, global research, and craft references are all fair game.

## Step 3 — Create 5 directions

Each direction must be a different animal. Force diversity across at least these axes:
- **Narrative device** — e.g., classic rise-and-fall of one cat · second-person "this is you" · mockumentary/game-show parody of the creator economy · a fable told through level furniture with almost no words · reverse chronology starting from the moment a creator quits · a "two routes" level where the same cat lives two versions of the same career. (These are sparks, not a menu — invent your own.)
- **Emotional register** — at least one *funny/satirical*, at least one *dark and quiet*, at least one *warm/hopeful*. Not five sad pianos.
- **Level mechanics as storytelling** — in each direction, the *game furniture itself* must carry the story (what ? blocks mean in this world, what the dark zone is, what coins/followers/pits represent). If the copy could be pasted onto a plain webpage without losing anything, the direction fails.

**For EACH of the 5 directions, deliver:**
1. **Title + logline** (2 sentences max)
2. **The hook** — why a creator at 1 a.m. keeps scrolling past screen 3, tied to a specific researched insight (cite it)
3. **Act structure** — beginning / middle / end in ~6 lines
4. **The level tells it how** — 5–8 key scenes: what's on screen, what the cat does, what the furniture means
5. **Sample copy** — 5–7 beats of actual draft copy, **Thai first (site language), English gloss underneath**, each ≤2 lines, readable in 3 seconds on 390px
6. **Where Plan C enters** — and how the sell stays invisible until the reader already wants it
7. **Fun factor** — what makes this one enjoyable, not just persuasive
8. **Risks / watch-outs** — where this direction could cringe, confuse, or feel like a scam post
9. **Production weight** — S / M / L (all directions must be buildable from repeated, re-placed pieces: one tileset, one cat sprite set, blocks, signs, pipes, palette swaps — never a freshly painted scene per level)

## Non-negotiables (short list — everything else is creative freedom)

1. Keep Plan C's spine true: creator-side · creators pay, brands don't · no commission ever · founder stays anonymous (an insider you can't verify — use that as story fuel, not a bug)
2. No fake testimonials, no invented success numbers, no "our clients grew X%"
3. Never call Plan C an "agency" · no "AI-powered" · no hype-urgency countdown energy
4. Don't end on "here's a list of your pains — DM us" (that's the signature of scam posts in the exact Facebook groups this audience lives in)
5. Voice: a competent friend who's been there — never a brochure, never a life coach selling a course

## Deliverable
