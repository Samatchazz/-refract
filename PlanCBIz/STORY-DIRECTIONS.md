# DIRECTION 1 — «แพตช์โน้ต» (Patch Notes)

### Register: funny / satirical · Narrative device: the creator economy as a badly-run live-service game

## Title + logline

**แพตช์โน้ต — เกมที่คุณเล่นทุกวัน กติกาเปลี่ยนทุกคืน**
Being a Thai creator is playing a live-service game where an invisible developer patches the rules every night and never publishes the changelog. Plan C is the one NPC who has actually read it — because for ten years he sat in the room where it's written.

## The hook — why they keep scrolling past screen 3

The visitor at 1 a.m. has *just lived this*: they posted, watched numbers do something inexplicable, and opened Facebook to ask why. Creators demonstrably experience the algorithm as an inscrutable god they appease with superstitious rituals — Polygon's 2018 reporting coined it "algorithm kremlinology" ([Boing Boing on Polygon](https://boingboing.net/2018/06/02/algorithm-grade.html)), and creators themselves describe uploading as "pulling the slot machine" ([Why YouTubers Quit, Substack](https://patapiropoppet.substack.com/p/why-youtubers-quit)). Naming that absurdity out loud, in game-patch language, is the comedy of recognition — and consumer research says funny is criminally underused: 90% remember humorous ads better while 95% of business leaders are afraid to be funny ([Oracle/Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/happiness-marketing-brand-consumer-oracle/625554/)). Nobody else in the Thai creator groups is doing deadpan comedy about this. Screens 1–3 are jokes the reader has personally paid for.

**Craft guardrail baked in:** Eisend's meta-analysis (369 effect sizes) shows humor lifts attention and liking but *reduces source credibility* ([JAMS](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11747-008-0096-y)). So the satire never touches the evidence: the 36% billboard, the <2% ceiling, and "nobody asked the quitters" appear as sudden straight-faced interruptions — patch notes stop being funny for three screens, then resume. The tonal cut is itself the punch.

## Act structure

1. **Act 1 — Tutorial bliss (screens 1–5):** classic sunny 1-1. Coins, likes, first ? block pays out. The game is fun and fair. Then a patch-note sign slams down: *v2.1 — visibility rebalanced.* Same level, coins now render smaller.
2. **Act 2 — Patch hell (screens 6–14):** each obstacle is a "feature": loot-box brand deals, the Revision #8 respawning thorn block, the brand NPC that stops loading after delivery, a "trend pipe" that closes while the cat is halfway in.
3. **False victory (midpoint):** the cat hits a golden block — VIRAL. Coin fountain. Next screen: *v3.0 — golden blocks deprecated.* (The false-victory midpoint per Save the Cat: let them win, then flip it.)
4. **The straight-faced interruption:** three deadpan data billboards in a grey zone — 36% · <2% · nobody ever asked the quitters. No jokes here. Sources + caveats attached.
5. **Act 3 — The changelog exists (screens 18–24):** reveal sign: this game *has* a manual — it's only handed out on the paying side. Enter the one character who's read it. Services = readable patch notes (the weekly feed IS the changelog, finally in your language).
6. **End:** the cat keeps playing the same game — but holding the changelog. CTA on the last sign, after all evidence.

## Sample copy (7 beats)

> **ยินดีต้อนรับสู่เกมที่คุณเล่นอยู่ทุกวัน**
> **กติกาเปลี่ยนทุกคืน — ไม่มีประกาศ**
> *Welcome to the game you already play every day. The rules change nightly — no announcement.*

> **v2.4 — «ปรับสมดุลการมองเห็น»**

> *v2.4 — "visibility rebalanced." What it means, nobody knows. Possibly including whoever wrote it.*

> **กล่องสุ่มดีลแบรนด์:** บางกล่องมีเงิน
> บางกล่องมี «ขอเป็นสินค้าแทนได้ไหมคะ»
> *Brand-deal loot box: some contain money. Some contain "could we pay you in product?"*

> ด่านนี้ชื่อ **«แก้รอบที่ 8»**
> ไม่มีเช็คพอยต์
> *This stage is called "Revision #8." There is no checkpoint.*

> NPC แบรนด์หยุดโหลดหลังคุณส่งงาน
> **บั๊กนี้มีมาสิบปีแล้ว ไม่เคยถูกแก้**
> *The brand NPC stops loading after you deliver. This bug is ten years old. It has never been fixed.*

> เกมนี้มีคู่มือจริงๆ
> **แค่เขาแจกเฉพาะฝั่งที่จ่ายเงิน**
> *This game does have a manual. It's just only handed out on the paying side.*

> ผมอ่านมันมาสิบปี — **จากฝั่งที่เขียน**
> ตอนนี้ผมแปลมันทุกสัปดาห์ ให้ฝั่งคุณ
> *I've read it for ten years — from the side that writes it. Now I translate it weekly, for your side.*

## Where Plan C enters

Screen ~18 of ~24 — after the false victory and the deadpan data zone, never before. Plan C is not a power-up and not the game's hero: he's the one character who's read the developer docs, introduced by the lectern scene. The sell stays invisible because the membership is never pitched as a product — it's revealed as *the changelog finally being readable* (weekly feed), *the strategy guide* (contract checklists), *your save-file audit* (quarterly scorecard). By the time prices appear on a plain sign, the reader has already thought "I want the changelog." "No commission, ever" lands as the final joke that isn't a joke: **«ผมได้เท่าเดิม ไม่ว่าคุณจะกดรับเควสต์หรือไม่»** (*I get paid the same whether you accept the quest or not.*)

## Fun factor

It's the only direction where the reader laughs at the thing that's been quietly humiliating them — and laughing at the machine together is the fastest "this person gets it" there is. Every joke doubles as proof of insiderness: only someone who has sat through real briefs can parody them accurately (specific, unfakeable detail is precisely what makes testimony credible — Bell & Loftus's "trivial persuasion" effect, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2724064/)). Highly screenshot-able beats → shares in the exact Facebook groups the channel strategy depends on.

## Risks / watch-outs

- **Humor erodes credibility** (Eisend) — mitigated by the strict no-jokes data zone, but if satire bleeds into the billboards, the whole evidence engine collapses.
- Punching direction must stay UP at the system, never at brands by name (NDA / "patterns, never cases") and never at the reader.
- If the jokes are only *okay*, deadpan reads as bitter. Thai comedic timing needs a native pass.
- Parody of platform patch notes must not name TikTok/Meta as villains — the founder still works with brands daily.

## Production weight: **M**

One extra palette (grey data zone), one glitch/wireframe shader on the standard NPC sprite, patch-note sign = the small sign asset with a header strip. Everything else is re-placed standard pieces.

# DIRECTION 2 — «ด่านร้าง» (The Abandoned Level)

### Register: dark and quiet · Narrative device: reverse chronology — walking backwards through the career of a creator who quit

## Title + logline

**ด่านร้าง — เดินย้อนหาจุดที่มันเริ่มพัง**
A creator stopped posting fourteen months ago; nobody ever asked why. The visitor's cat walks rightward through the frozen ruins of that career — from the day it went quiet back to the first bright coin — and realizes, at the end, that the level is their own.

## The hook — why they keep scrolling past screen 3

Every creator knows one who vanished — no goodbye post, no drama, just quiet. Research confirms both the scale and the silence: 71% of surveyed creators have considered quitting ([Vibely via Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2021/11/11/vibely-content-creators-burnout-study/)), and Nguyen & Duffy's study of 198 accounts found creator burnout is partly *"unspeakable"* — creators frame systemic failure as personal failure and go silent rather than explain ([New Media & Society](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/14614448261459667)). That silence is exactly why Plan C's strongest data block exists: *no study anywhere has ever asked the quitters why they quit.* This direction turns that sentence into the entire level. The pull past screen 3 is forensic: a mystery with a body but no cause of death, told the way Limbo and Inside tell stories — zero exposition, dread staged in the furniture, the story "not told to the player so much as felt" ([CJ Leo on Inside](https://cjleo.com/blog/the-subtle-storytelling-of-the-video-game-inside/); [Game Developer on Limbo](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/design-bits-analysis-limbo-s-level-design---mostly-perfect)). At 1 a.m., the unspoken question doing the scrolling is: *am I next?*

## Act structure

1. **Act 1 — The end (screens 1–4):** dusk palette. A castle with no flag ever raised. A signpost: *last upload — 14 months ago.* The cat (the visitor) steps over the boundary rope and walks INTO the past — each screen rightward is a month earlier.
2. **Act 2 — The dig (screens 5–14):** the silence stratum (delivered work, empty reply bubble frozen mid-air) → the dark stratum (the meeting-room door he never entered; through a window, the comparison table, one column glowing) → the grind stratum (thorn blocks worn down 8 times; a rate sign covered in scratched-out guesses).
3. **Deeper = brighter:** the further back the cat walks, the warmer the palette gets — first deal bunting, the night the numbers ran, the first coin, still golden. The brightest place in the ruins is the beginning. That inversion is the gut-punch.
4. **The turn (screen ~16):** at the very start of the dead career sits a mirror-frame. Through it, the ruins' details match the visitor's present: the same scratched rate guesses, the same unread contract pile. *This level isn't his. It's the one you're standing in.*
5. **Act 3 — The question (screens 17–21):** billboard: *no study on earth ever asked the ones who quit, why.* Then: *we're asking.* Plan C appears as the people running the missing study — the first conversation framed as being asked, properly, for once. Evidence blocks, six lines, plain pricing.
6. **End:** the cat walks back out of the ruins into its own level — same furniture, but now legible. Door with a light on. CTA.

## Sample copy (7 beats)

> มีครีเอเตอร์คนหนึ่งเคยอยู่ตรงนี้
> **เขาหยุดโพสต์เมื่อ 14 เดือนก่อน**
> *A creator used to live here. He stopped posting fourteen months ago.*

> ไม่มีโพสต์อำลา ไม่มีดราม่า
> เงียบไปเฉยๆ — **แบบที่คุณเคยเห็นคนอื่นเป็น**

> ข้อความสุดท้ายในแชต: **«ได้รับแล้วครับ»**

> ประตูนี้ เขาไม่เคยได้เข้า
> **ชื่อเขาถูกขีดออกข้างใน — เขาไม่เคยรู้ด้วยซ้ำ**

> ที่ที่สว่างที่สุดของด่านร้าง **คือจุดเริ่มต้น**

> ด่านนี้ไม่ใช่ของเขาคนเดียว
> **มันคือด่านเดียวกับที่คุณยืนอยู่ตอนนี้**
> *This level isn't only his. It's the same one you're standing in right now.*

> ไม่มีงานวิจัยไหนในโลก เคยถามคนที่เลิก ว่าเลิกเพราะอะไร
> **เรากำลังถาม — และเราเริ่มถามตอนที่คุณยังอยู่**
> *No study on earth has ever asked the ones who quit, why. We're asking — and we ask while you're still here.*

## Where Plan C enters

Screen ~17 of ~21, and only as **the asker of the missing question** — the single most defensible identity in the whole BMC (Block D is the founder's strongest citable asset). No services appear until the visitor has walked the whole ruin. Then the free first session is not "a consultation": it's *the interview nobody gave him* — 45 minutes where, for once, someone from the paying side asks you the questions and hands you everything (the one-page compass, nothing held back). Membership surfaces afterwards as "the reason there'll be fewer ruins": the feed, the checklists, the debrief doctrine — each mapped to one stratum of damage the reader just walked through. The anonymous founder is story fuel here: the ruins prove he's seen this from inside; you don't need his name to check the details. CTA is an invitation to talk *while it's still repairable* — understanding-first, and structurally the opposite of a scam post because nothing was listed AT the reader; they walked through someone else's evidence.

## Fun factor

The fun is detective fun — Limbo's pleasure, not Mario's: reading a frozen scene, working out what happened one beat before you're told. Every prop is a micro-mystery with a one-screen payoff, and the mirror reveal is a genuine twist the reader gets to *have* rather than be told. It's also the direction with the most powerful screenshot: the empty flagpole with «อัปโหลดล่าสุด: 14 เดือนก่อน» is a whole story in one frame.

## Risks / watch-outs

- **Bleakness overdose at 1 a.m.** — a tired, anxious reader may close it to protect their mood. The warm-toward-the-beginning palette inversion and the gentle ending are the counterweights; they must not be cut.
- **Fear-marketing adjacency:** "this could be you" is one bad line away from a scare-then-sell scam post. The protection: the level never threatens the reader; the reveal is *recognition*, and the CTA offers being understood, not rescue from doom.
- The dead creator must stay a **pattern, never a case** — no details traceable to any real person (also the NDA rule).
- Reverse chronology must be signposted hard (month markers on signs) or 1 a.m. readers will read it as a normal level and miss the device.

## Production weight: **M**

Standard pieces + one "decay" overlay set (vines, dust, tally marks, frozen speech bubble) and a dusk→warm palette ramp. The mirror-frame is one new prop. No new character sprites — the quitter is never shown, which is the point.

# DIRECTION 3 — «ด่านเดิม รอบสอง» (Same Level, Second Run)

### Register: analytical → quietly hopeful · Narrative device: the same career played twice — the only patch applied is information

## Title + logline

**ด่านเดิม รอบสอง — แผนที่เปลี่ยนคน ไม่ใช่ด่านเปลี่ยน**
The cat runs one level, guessing at every sign, and finishes with a handful of coins. A pipe sends it back to the exact same level — same blocks, same pits, same locked door — and this run, someone has told it what the furniture means. Same career, two prices.

## The hook — why they keep scrolling past screen 3

Run 1 is the reader's normal life, compressed and specific: guessing rates, signing page 2 of 11, waiting in silence. The scroll-hook is the promise implicit in the format itself — *you can feel there's going to be a second run.* The craft basis is Miyamoto's World 1-1 principle: players taught through placement come to own the game — "once they grasp the rules, it becomes their game" ([Game Developer on World 1-1](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-miyamoto-built-i-super-mario-bros-i-legendary-world-1-1)). The insight basis is documented and brutal: rates are so opaque that creator Melanie Demi discovered via pay-transparency data she was charging **three to four times less than smaller creators** ([dot.LA on FYPM](https://dot.la/fypm-2659277093.html)) — same creator, same audience, different information, different price. And the mechanism that closes the loop is Plan C's own: unclear identity isn't an aesthetic problem, it's a pricing problem. This direction doesn't *state* that mechanism — it lets the reader watch information alone change the outcome of an identical level. Spot-the-difference keeps thumbs moving: every Run-2 screen is a small puzzle ("what changed here?") with an instant answer.

## Act structure

1. **Act 1 — Run 1, muted palette (screens 1–8):** the cat plays honestly and blind. Scribbled rate sign → guess. Contract paper-pile → leaps it, clips its tail. Delivery → silence zone. Meeting-room door → shut. Finish line: a small coin count. Caption: *feel familiar?*
2. **The pipe (midpoint, screen 9):** not a warp to somewhere new — a warp to the SAME start. One sign: *exact same level. One change: this time, someone tells you the rules.* This is where the mechanism line lands: unclear identity = too expensive to gamble on.
3. **Act 2 — Run 2, warm palette (screens 10–17):** identical furniture, now legible. The rate sign shows a *method* (cost-based, no numbers — the public-floor rule respected). The paper pile has a checklist ladder leaned against it. The silence zone has a debrief bell. The door still doesn't open — but the "any problems?" column visibly has an answer in it now.
4. **The honest limit:** Run 2 is *not* a victory lap — same pits exist, the door still doesn't open, and one sign says some players should finish this level as a side quest, not a main quest. Honesty is the brand.
5. **Act 3 — Who rewrote the signs (screens 18–22):** the sign-painter is revealed: ten years on the paying side, still there daily, no commission either way. Six fence posts. Plain pricing.
6. **End:** two coin counters shown side by side — Run 1 and Run 2. Same level. The difference was never talent. CTA.

|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Muted palette. Rate sign covered in scratched guesses | squints, shrugs, walks on | rate roulette — pricing by guesswork, every single time |
| 2 | Paper pile 3 tiles high, pen on top | takes a run-up and jumps it blind, clips tail | contracts signed unread because reading them all is impossible alone |
| 3 | Delivery flag planted; the world goes still; nothing animates for a whole screen | sits, ears drooping, checks an empty mailbox | silence after delivery — stillness as furniture |
| 4 | The meeting-room door, shut; muffled light underneath | knocks once; nothing | the decision happens without you |
| 5 | Finish flag, small coin counter: ×7. Sign: «คุ้นไหม» | plants the flag without celebrating | the honest result of playing blind |

| 8 | Same door, still shut — but through the window, the table's «เคยมีปัญหาไหม» column has writing in it | looks through, breathes out | you still don't enter the room; but for the first time, something answers for you in there |

## Sample copy (7 beats)

> ป้ายเรทเขียนว่าอะไร อ่านไม่ออก
> **«งานนี้คิดเท่าไหร่ดี» — เดาเอา เหมือนทุกครั้ง**
> *The rate sign is illegible. "How much should I charge?" — guess. Like every time.*

> (คุณอ่านไปถึงหน้า 2 จาก 11)

> **เปลี่ยนอย่างเดียว: รอบนี้มีคนบอกกติกา**
> *The exact same level — one more run. One change: this time, someone tells you the rules.*

> ตัวตนไม่ชัด ไม่ได้แปลว่าคุณไม่น่าสนใจ
> **มันแปลว่าคุณแพงเกินไป สำหรับคนที่ต้องเดา**

> เรทไม่ได้มาจากงบของเขา
> **มันมาจากต้นทุนของคุณ — และมันมีวิธีคิด**

> ด่านไม่เคยเปลี่ยน
> **ข้อมูลต่างหากที่เปลี่ยน**
> *The level never changed. The information did.*

## Where Plan C enters

Two-stage entrance, both late. The **pipe** is Plan C's mechanism entering *anonymously* — the mid-level sign speaks the pricing-problem line without any brand attached, so the idea proves itself before anyone asks to be trusted. The **sign-painter** is revealed only in Act 3, after Run 2 has already demonstrated the value: every improvement the reader just watched (rate method, checklist ladder, debrief bell, the answered column) maps one-to-one onto what the ฿2,000 membership actually contains — so the product demo happened inside the story, disguised as level design. That is the "taste of B" rule from the simulations (the creator who got real substance bought without being pitched) executed as structure. The no-commission line closes it: the sign-painter earns the same whether the cat takes any deal or not — which is why the signs can tell the truth.

## Fun factor

Spot-the-difference is one of the most compulsive micro-games there is, and here every difference is *meaningful* — the reader is rewarded for noticing with a small "ohh" each screen. It's also the only direction whose pleasure is **hope with receipts**: nothing magical happens in Run 2, the pits stay, the door stays shut, and it's still visibly a better career. For the analytical reader (the exact profile that pays ฿2,000 and checks citations), this is the direction that respects their intelligence most.

## Risks / watch-outs

- **Length discipline:** two runs ≈ double beats. Run 1 must be ruthlessly short (≤8 screens) or 1 a.m. thumbs die before the pipe. Front-load per NN/g: 74% of attention is spent in the first two screenfuls ([NN/g Scrolling & Attention](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/)).
- **Run 2 must not oversell.** If run 2 looks like a jackpot, it violates "no invented results" in spirit. The kept-shut door and the "side-quest" sign are the honesty anchors — do not polish them away.
- Repetition can read as a bug on mobile ("did the page reload?") — the pipe transition and palette shift must be unmistakable.
- The rate-method sign must show *method only* — a single concrete baht number leaks the forbidden public floor.

## Production weight: **S**

The cheapest direction relative to impact: it is *literally the same level twice* — one arrangement, two palettes, a handful of overlay props for Run 2 (clean sign faces, checklist ladder, bell, window writing). The World 1-1/1-2 trick is the entire production plan.

# DIRECTION 4 — «ปุ่มสีเทา» (The Greyed-Out Buttons)

### Register: wry, intimate → empowering · Narrative device: second-person interactive — your career told through the choices you were never able to press

## Title + logline

**ปุ่มสีเทา — คืนนี้คุณเล่นเป็นตัวเอง**
Tonight you play as yourself: at every obstacle the screen offers the *right* choice — visibly there, and greyed out, because the information it needs has never existed on your side of the industry. The story of a creator career, told through buttons you can't press.

## The hook — why they keep scrolling past screen 3

The greyed-out choice is the single most viscerally understood UI element alive — and it has a proven storytelling pedigree: Depression Quest shows players the "normal" options crossed out and unavailable, converting outside judgment ("just ask them") into *experienced constraint*, which analysts credit as its entire empathy engine ([Video Games of the Oppressed](https://videogamesoftheoppressed.wordpress.com/2013/12/11/depression-quest-and-active-empathy/)). Reader-response research on second-person narration adds the design rule that makes it safe: "you" narration produces strong identification **when the offered role is plausible and doesn't shame the reader** — readers actively resist a "you" that insults them ([Bell et al. via BYU](https://editingresearch.byu.edu/2022/06/08/who-am-i-reader-response-to-second-person-narration/)). Here every locked button explicitly blames the *missing information*, never the reader — which is also, precisely, Plan C's actual thesis. The 1 a.m. scroller keeps going because every greyed button is something they literally tried to do this month: look up a standard rate (doesn't exist), ask why they weren't rehired (got "we changed direction"), see the campaign report (lives on the brand's side — rate opacity is so total that 92% of surveyed influencers name pay transparency as the fix, [MSL study](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/msl-study-reveals-racial-pay-gap-in-influencer-marketing-301437451.html)). Tapping a locked button gives a one-line tooltip — so even the *frustration* is playable.

## Act structure

1. **Act 1 — Character select (screens 1–3):** «คืนนี้คุณเล่นเป็นตัวเอง โชคดีนะ». The cat = you. First obstacle, first choice box: one live option, one greyed. The grammar is taught in one screen (Miyamoto rule: teach by doing, not by tutorial).
2. **Act 2 — The locked run (screens 4–12):** rate sign, contract pile, revision block, silence zone — each with its choice box. The right buttons are all grey, each with a reason-tag. The only live options are the ones the reader recognizes as their real life: [เดาเอา] [เซ็นไปก่อน] [รอต่อไป] [ยิ้มรับ].
3. **False victory (midpoint):** a clip goes viral — for one glorious screen EVERY button lights up. Brands reply fast, rates jump. Next deal, next screen: grey again. (Virality rents the buttons; it doesn't buy them.)
4. **The dark zone:** the meeting-room door. Choice box: [แก้ต่างในห้องประชุม] — locked: *you are not in the room.* No live option at all on this screen. The only screen with zero choices is the loneliest beat in the piece.
5. **Act 3 — The unlock (screens 15–20):** the thesis sign: the buttons were never locked because you lack talent — they're locked because the information sits on the other side of the table. Then, one by one, buttons flip live as their key appears: checklist → [อ่านสัญญาให้ทัน] unlocks; debrief doctrine → [ขอผลตอบรับ] unlocks; weekly feed → [รู้ว่าห้องนั้นคุยอะไรกัน] unlocks *partially* (honesty: you still don't enter the room).
6. **End:** the last screen reveals one button that was never locked at all: [คุยกับคนที่เคยนั่งฝั่งนั้น] — first time free. CTA is the button.

## The level tells it — key scenes

| # | On screen | The cat | What the furniture means |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Night level; a floating choice box appears over the first obstacle | looks up at the box, then at the reader | the game grammar: this is your life, with its real options shown |
| 2 | Rate sign; box: [ดูเรทกลางของตลาด]-grey «ไม่มีอยู่จริง» / [เดาเอา]-live | presses the only live button; the sign spins like a slot reel | rate roulette — the correct move doesn't exist to be chosen |
| 3 | Contract pile; box: [ให้ทนายอ่าน]-grey «ทนายไหน» / [เซ็นไปก่อน]-live | signs; pile wobbles ominously | professional infrastructure has never been available at your size |
| 4 | Viral screen: confetti, every button suddenly gold and live | taps everything joyfully | the false victory — attention temporarily rents you access |
| 5 | Next deal: same boxes, grey again | stares at the buttons that worked yesterday | virality is not infrastructure |
| 6 | Meeting-room door; box: [แก้ต่างในห้องประชุม]-grey «คุณไม่ได้อยู่ในห้องนั้น»; no live buttons | sits — the only screen where the cat can do nothing | the room you're not in, felt as UI: zero agency, rendered honestly |
| 7 | Thesis sign; then a checklist item slots into the UI and one button flips to color with a soft *click* | ears up; presses it | the unlock isn't magic — each unlocked button has a visible, mundane key |

## Sample copy (7 beats)

> **คืนนี้ คุณเล่นเป็นตัวเอง**
> โชคดีนะ
> *Tonight, you play as yourself. Good luck.*

> [ดูเรทกลางของตลาด] — **ล็อก: ไม่มีอยู่จริง**
> [เดาเอา] — กดได้
> *[View the standard market rate] — locked: it doesn't exist. [Guess] — available.*

> [ถามตรงๆ ว่าทำไมไม่จ้างต่อ] — **ล็อก: จะได้ยินแค่ «ปีนี้ปรับ direction ครับ»**
> [ยิ้มรับ] — กดได้
> *[Ask directly why they didn't rehire you] — locked: you'll only hear "we changed direction this year." [Smile and accept] — available.*

> [แก้ต่างในห้องประชุม] — **ล็อก: คุณไม่ได้อยู่ในห้องนั้น**
> — ไม่มีปุ่มอื่นให้กด —
> *[Defend yourself in the meeting room] — locked: you are not in that room. — no other buttons available —*

> ปุ่มพวกนี้ไม่ได้ล็อกเพราะคุณไม่เก่ง
> **มันล็อกเพราะข้อมูลอยู่อีกฝั่งของโต๊ะ**
> *These buttons aren't locked because you lack talent. They're locked because the information sits on the other side of the table.*

> [อ่านสัญญาให้ทันก่อนเซ็น] — **ปลดล็อกแล้ว: มีเช็กลิสต์**
> [ขอผลตอบรับหลังจบงาน] — **ปลดล็อกแล้ว: มีวิธีขอ**
> *[Actually read the contract in time] — unlocked: there's a checklist. [Ask for a debrief] — unlocked: there's a way to ask.*

> มีปุ่มหนึ่งที่ไม่เคยล็อกเลย
> **[คุยกับคนที่เคยนั่งฝั่งนั้น] — ครั้งแรก ไม่มีค่าใช้จ่าย**
> *One button was never locked at all: [Talk to someone who sat on that side] — first time, no charge.*

## Where Plan C enters

Later than any other direction — Plan C is not a character at all until Act 3, and even then it enters as *keys, not a person*: a checklist slides into the UI and a button audibly unlocks. The reader experiences the product's exact value proposition (information converts locked choices into live ones) as an interaction pattern before ever seeing a name or a price. The honesty beats are structural: the room button unlocks only *partially* — membership gives you the weekly feed of what that side is thinking, not a seat in the room — and one grey button stays grey forever: [รับประกันว่าจะได้งาน] — locked: *no one honest can press this for you* (the written no-guarantee rule, turned into the direction's best trust moment). The founder's anonymity becomes a button too: [เช็กชื่อคนทำเว็บนี้] — locked: *not yet — read what he knows and judge for yourself,* which converts the site's biggest weakness into a self-aware beat.

## Fun factor

It *feels like a game*, not a story about one — the only direction where the reader's thumb does something other than scroll. Tapping locked buttons for rueful tooltip jokes is a compulsive loop (every tap = one more beat consumed), the all-gold viral screen is a genuine dopamine spike, and the final never-locked button is the rare CTA that lands as a plot twist. Screenshot power: any single choice-box screen is a shareable meme about creator life.

## Risks / watch-outs

- **Frustration is the medium** — a reader having a bad week may find nine locked screens punishing rather than validating. The blame-the-system reason-tags (per Bell et al.) are load-bearing on every single button; one shaming tag breaks the direction.
- **Scope creep:** tappable boxes + tooltips + unlock states is real interaction design, not just scroll animation. If build time threatens the critical path (the site blocks the entire go-to-market), this direction should be simplified or shelved — ship-fast beats clever.
- Scrolljack temptation: choice boxes must ride ON the scroll, never pause it — hijacked scrolling tests disastrously on mobile ([NN/g Scrolljacking 101](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolljacking-101/)).
- The "we changed direction" tooltip must stay pattern-level; no brand-identifiable phrasing.

## Production weight: **L**

Standard level pieces are reused, but the choice-box UI system (three states, tap-tooltips, unlock animation, one all-gold screen) is a real component library on top. The single most build-expensive direction.

# DIRECTION 5 — «กะดึก» (The Night Shift)

### Register: warm, quiet, hopeful · Narrative device: a near-wordless fable — one night walk, told almost entirely by the level furniture

## Title + logline

**กะดึก — เมืองหลับแล้ว คุณยังเดินอยู่**
It is 1 a.m. in the level, exactly as it is on the reader's phone. The whole city sleeps; the cat is still working. Somewhere along the road it finds a second set of pawprints — ten years older, same direction — and follows them to the one window still lit. Almost no words. The furniture says everything.

## The hook — why they keep scrolling past screen 3

This direction cheats: it doesn't need to *earn* identification, because the reader is already inside the premise at the moment of scrolling. The founder's own intake data shows it — forms arriving at 1:47 a.m., messages at 22:40; his working hours (21:00–24:00) are the audience's waking hours. The level simply mirrors the reader's present tense: dark city, lit phone, still awake. The emotional truth it walks toward is the best-documented one in creator research: the job is lonely in a way outsiders can't see — "the most fun job imaginable quickly slid into something deeply bleak and lonely" (Matt Lees, [The Guardian](https://social.cs.washington.edu/cse481social/readings/W5S2/youtube-stars-burnout-fun-bleak-stressed.pdf)); a creator with 24k followers: "we're all on social media, but that doesn't mean there's much that's really social about these platforms anymore" ([Refinery29](https://www.refinery29.com/en-us/social-media-loneliness-influencer)); and at the top, "your peers are gone and you're the only one left" ([Fortune on executive loneliness](https://fortune.com/2023/11/14/ceos-loneliness-epidemic-mental-health-employee-well-being)). The craft model is Journey: Jenova Chen's team found that *presence itself* — a companion who walks the same road, with no verbs for using or pushing you — is what produces the strongest emotional response, and struggle must precede the summit for catharsis to land ([GDC 2013](https://journey-archive.fandom.com/wiki/User_blog:JAlbor/GDC_2013:_Designing_Journey_with_Jenova_Chen)). Minimal text also fits the reader's actual state: tired eyes, one thumb, 57% of attention already spent above the fold ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/)).

## Act structure

1. **Act 1 — The walk (screens 1–5):** night palette. Dark houses, drawn curtains, one alarm clock glowing 06:30 in a window — the salaried world, asleep. The cat walks with a lit phone for a lantern. Notification-fireflies drift up and fade. Beautiful, calm, and quietly alone.
2. **Act 2 — The climb (screens 6–10):** the road rises on scaffolding as follower-count milestones pass (10k … 100k … 1M as roadside markers). With every marker, fewer lights below. At the top: the piece's ONE full-screen line — «อาชีพนี้ ยิ่งสูง ยิ่งเหงา». Held for a whole screen. Silence before and after (this is the line that closed a real sale; it gets the weight the previous site never gave it).
3. **The turn (screen 11):** pawprints. A second set, older, pressed deeper, same direction. Not approaching the cat's path — *already on it.*
4. **Act 3 — The notes (screens 12–16):** whoever walked here first left small handwritten notes nailed to posts — insider observations from the paying side (pattern-level, feed-flavored: this is the taste of B, placed as trail markers). The cat reads one per screen. The road feels different with notes on it.
5. **Arrival (screens 17–20):** the prints lead to the one lit window — light on from 21:00 to 24:00, the same hours the reader is awake now. Six fence posts before the door (the six lines). The door is unlocked, not open: the cat may enter or keep walking. Both are fine. That non-insistence IS the brand.
6. **End:** plain pricing on a small porch sign, the no-guarantee line in writing, CTA as a doorbell — not a demand.

## The level tells it — key scenes

| # | On screen | The cat | What the furniture means |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Night city; every window dark; one glowing alarm clock set for 06:30 | walks, phone-glow for a lantern | the employed world sleeps on a schedule; your job has no clock-out |
| 2 | Notification-fireflies rise off the phone and fade before reaching the sky | doesn't chase them | engagement evaporates; it was never company |
| 3 | Roadside milestones: 10k → 100k → 1M; with each, fewer lit windows below | climbs without pausing at any of them | the numbers went up; the world got quieter — growth as subtraction |
| 4 | Full-screen: the summit platform, empty. One line of text. Nothing else | sits — the same sit as screen 1, meaning inverted | ยิ่งสูงยิ่งเหงา given a whole screen of silence to land in |
| 5 | A second set of pawprints, older, deeper, same direction | stops; puts its paw inside one print — it fits, larger | someone walked this exact road first, and further |
| 6 | Small handwritten notes nailed to fence posts along the path | reads one per screen, walking slower now | the taste of B: insider pattern-notes left where the road actually hurts |
| 7 | The one lit window (21:00–24:00 on a small plaque); six fence posts leading to the door | walks the fence line, reading each post | the six lines we don't cross — trust built before the door, not claimed at it |
| 8 | The door: unlocked, ajar, warm light; the road also continues past it | pauses on the doorstep — the scroll decides, not the level | help that doesn't grab you: enter, or keep walking; both are respected |

## Sample copy (7 beats — nearly the entire script; this direction stays word-scarce)

> ตอนนี้ตีหนึ่ง
> **เมืองหลับแล้ว — คุณยังทำงานอยู่**
> *It's 1 a.m. The city is asleep — you're still working.*

> คนมีเงินเดือนตั้งนาฬิกาปลุกหกโมงครึ่ง
> **อาชีพของคุณ ไม่มีเวลาเลิกงาน**
> *The salaried set alarms for six-thirty. Your job has no clock-out time.*

> ยอดผู้ติดตามเพิ่มขึ้นเรื่อยๆ
> **แสงไฟข้างล่าง ลดลงเรื่อยๆ**
> *The follower count keeps rising. The lights below keep going out.*

> อาชีพนี้ **ยิ่งสูง**
> **ยิ่งเหงา**
> *In this career — the higher you climb, the lonelier it gets.*

> รอยเท้าอีกชุดหนึ่ง เก่ากว่าของคุณสิบปี
> **ทางเดียวกัน**
> *Another set of pawprints, ten years older than yours. Same road.*

> โน้ตที่เขาทิ้งไว้ข้างทาง: «แบรนด์ไม่ได้ตัดคุณเพราะยอด —
> **เขาตัดกันในห้องที่คุณไม่ได้อยู่»**
> *A note left by the road: "Brands don't cut you over your numbers — they cut you in a room you're not in."*

> ไฟดวงเดียวที่ยังเปิด เปิดสามทุ่มถึงเที่ยงคืนทุกวัน
> **ประตูไม่ได้ล็อก — จะแวะ หรือเดินต่อ ก็ได้ทั้งคู่**
> *The one light still on burns nine-to-midnight, every night. The door isn't locked — stop in, or keep walking. Both are fine.*

## Where Plan C enters

Plan C never "appears" — it *accumulates*: first as pawprints (someone real walked this road, further than you), then as trail notes (the weekly feed sampled as physical objects — this is the free taste of B, delivered mid-level without a pitch), then as six fence posts, and finally as a lit window with its hours on a plaque. The founder's anonymity is handled entirely by furniture: you never see who left the prints — you judge the prints. The services resolve on the porch, in plain type, smallest of any direction: first talk free, single session ฿999, membership ฿2,000 with the feed named as "โน้ตแบบนี้ ทุกสัปดาห์" (*notes like these, every week*) — tying the product to the exact objects the reader just enjoyed. The no-guarantee sentence sits in writing beside the doorbell. The sell stays invisible because the entire act of selling is replaced by an unlocked door the reader was allowed to walk past.

## Fun factor

The fun is *relief*. At 1 a.m. this is the only direction that lowers the reader's heart rate instead of raising it — an ambient, Journey-like walk that's beautiful on a phone in a dark room, with one twist (the pawprints) placed exactly where the loneliness peaks. Its pleasure is the pleasure of being kept company without being talked at — which is, not coincidentally, the product. It's also the direction most likely to be sent to a friend with just «อันนี้มึงต้องดู» — it's an experience, not an argument.

## Risks / watch-outs

- **Under-informing the stranger:** the site must convince someone who can't verify the founder — and this direction spends the least on evidence. Mitigation: at least one data note among the trail notes (e.g., the "nobody asked the quitters" line works natively as a handwritten note), and the fence-post six lines must survive any cut.
- **Sentimentality cliff:** one degree too warm and it becomes a life-coach lullaby — the exact tone the voice rules ban. The dry, concrete Thai of the approved copy blocks is the antidote; no metaphors about dreams, journeys, or stars.
- Word-scarcity puts enormous weight on art quality; if the night scenes read as "cheap dark level" rather than "beautiful night," the whole mood collapses. Palette and lighting polish are not optional here.
- The pawprint reveal must not read as surveillance-creepy ("someone is following you") — prints are *ahead of* the cat, never behind it.

## Production weight: **S–M**

One night palette + a lighting/glow pass, pawprint decals, note props, the plaque, and the alarm-clock window. No new sprites, no UI system, fewest copy blocks of all five. S if the glow pass is simple; M if the lighting needs real love (it does).
