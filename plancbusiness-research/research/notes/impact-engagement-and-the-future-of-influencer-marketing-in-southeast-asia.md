## Influencer marketing is set to grow bigger, become increasingly complex
Influencers are the biggest products of the digital age, especially in Southeast Asia, where social media usage is more than the global average. These digital personalities have spawned a whole new sector we now call the “creator economy” that connects people on the Internet in ways TV commercials or colorful flyers simply can’t.
It’s no surprise, then, that most brands today are leveraging influencer power as part of a winning campaign strategy—or, at the very least, are considering starting. Globally, the influencer advertising market is set to reach USD 480 billion by 2027, with the **Southeast Asia influencer segment growing at a blistering 10.83% CAGR in the next five years.**
These days, as much as 90% of influencers’ income comes from brand sponsorship contracts, Vero’s survey finds. Most influencers post sponsored content at least once a week, balancing brand promotions with regular engagement.
And as influencers become more integral to brand success, the ground rules for collaborations are also expanding. It’s no longer about chasing trends with proven viral effects or focusing on online personalities with the biggest followings. It’s not even about the surface-level metrics like likes, shares, or views anymore. [Nano- and micro-influencers](https://vero-asean.com/micro-influencers-indonesia-consumer-engagement/), with niche specialties and hyper-targeted audience bases, are going to make more—and possibly greater—impact.
**Content creators, influencers, and key opinion leaders (KOLs) want to be co-creators of products and services they personally believe in and trust.** Their unique perspectives, authentic voices, and carefully nurtured relationships with their audiences are what drive their bold creative expressions—making their influence powerful and genuinely earned.
The rise of social media has paved the way for content creators to thrive, to express themselves more freely, and to be loved for it. Unsurprisingly,**Instagram has become the go-to platform where 78% of influencers in Southeast Asia are most active.** Thanks to the platform’s highly engaged user base, sophisticated UI, and wide range of content creation formats, influencers and content creators consider it a dynamic digital playground for daily interaction.
TikTok, which only became available in Southeast Asia in 2018 and gained significant traction during the pandemic, has emerged as a powerful contender, with **81% of Southeast Asian influencers in our survey having some form of presence on TikTok.** Influencer presence in the market is only set to grow, particularly as they continue to explore new content formats and engage with audiences in fresh, dynamic ways. A considerably high percentage of influencers in Vietnam (39%) and Thailand (26%) are using TikTok as their primary platform, leveraging its viral potential, creative tools, and much younger audience base.
TikTok’s lightning-fast and user-driven content design means the platform’s unique algorithm amplifies certain types of content or campaigns with audience preferences in real-time—likely a cause for worry for specific branding and messaging, but also an opportunity to bring an influencer’s content to audiences that resonate with them.
But whichever platform influencers choose, those who stand out are those who can tell stories that captivate and connect. **34% of respondents across countries acknowledge storytelling as the most effective strategy for audience engagement.** As modern-day storytellers, influencers are open to showing glimpses of their everyday lives, being more candid about their thoughts, showcasing their individuality, and enhancing relatability. All this, in turn, helps foster an emotional connection not just between creators and their followers but also between their followers and the brands they choose to work with.
Our [social stunt for Singapore Changi Airport](https://vero-asean.com/case-study/singapore-changi-airport-social-stunt/) featured Vietnamese influencers ST Son Thach and Ninh Duong Lan Ngoc in an engaging real-time story—a strategy that 34% of Southeast Asian influencers consider their top choice for making engaging content.
_On a smaller scale, other strategies are applied in various ways across the region._
  * Live streams are seen as effective in Singapore and Indonesia but less so in the Philippines. Engaging and interactive in nature, live streams are often used for product launches, promotions, and even product selling.
  * Quizzes are more often used in Singapore, Indonesia, and Vietnam. Gamified content is fun and engaging, often tied to rewards and discounts, which encourage followers and viewers to interact and participate.
  * User-generated content (UGC), where one would repost or feature their followers’ comments and testimonials, is more popular among Thai influencers. This is a testament to the Thai audiences’ preferences for interactivity, with the local UGC market set to be worth [USD 426.7 million by 2028](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/horizon/outlook/user-generated-content-platform-market/thailand).


#### What brands should know:
  * Incorporate influencers’ unique storytelling to elevate your brand message—whether through a larger campaign story or smaller moments, like vlogs or event coverage, showing how your product fits into their lives.
  * Look into the unique ways each country’s influencers strategize their content, as their strategies differ due to cultural preferences and audience behavior. A campaign might tap Thai influencers for user-generated content that engages their followers, for instance, while partnering with Singaporean or Indonesian influencers for fun quizzes.


_“While social media platforms are constantly evolving, with algorithms shifting regularly, content creators continue to captivate audiences and achieve significant reach and engagement. A key factor driving this success is the ability to craft compelling narratives and foster genuine connections with viewers. By telling unique stories that resonate with their audience, creators can transcend algorithmic barriers and build loyal followings.”_
**_— Charissa Sevilla, Vero Influencer Relations Specialist, Philippines_** _“Thai social media users interact with their favorite influencers more actively than most other markets, driving the popularity of user-generated content. This underscores the value of co-creating content that feels personal and participatory, where influencers inspire conversations and turn campaigns into community-driven moments.__”_
_**— Rachat Ploungphet, Vero Senior KOLs and Influencer Manager, Thailand** _
The success of any influencer depends on how well they can build, sustain, and innovate their own personal brand. For many in our survey, this comes down to knowing how to be authentic in a way that others will understand and resonate with. That’s why **over half (58%) of influencers say that they prioritize maintaining their personal style while aligning their content with brand messaging** , taking great lengths to ensure that all posts stay true to who they are—a brand that their followers know very well and are loyal to.
Influencers across Southeast Asia apply key strategies to fulfil campaign requirements while remaining authentic and engaging their followers.
Southeast Asia is a culturally diverse region, and influencers have a good vantage point for understanding and catering to their followers’ particular nuances and complexities. Most influencers in our survey, particularly in Indonesia, the Philippines, Thailand, and Vietnam, emphasize the importance of understanding their target audience, while influencers in Singapore are more particular about localizing international or regional content. Many also tap into local trends or popular culture to increase engagement.
1 in 3 influencers in Southeast Asia agree that understanding your target audience is the most important factor in boosting engagement. Indeed, a good grasp of our audience’s tendencies, anxieties, and aspirations helped us craft our influencer campaigns for [TrueCaller in Indonesia](https://vero-asean.com/case-study/truecaller-ramadan-campaign/) and [Duolingo in the Philippines](https://vero-asean.com/case-study/duolingo-duet-challenge-philippines/).
In Thailand, there is a heightened emphasis on understanding cultural nuances in tailoring content for an influencer’s audience, suggesting that cultural sensitivity and awareness of phenomena like [the T-Wave](https://vero-asean.com/how-brands-can-tap-into-the-t-wave-cultural-phenomenon/) are crucial for successful engagement in this market.
When communicating effectively with their audiences, most influencers highlight the importance of maintaining their personal style. Because authenticity is an influencer’s main currency, brand messaging must be balanced with their unique voice.
#### What brands should know:
  * **About a third of influencers will politely decline brand requests that don’t match their personal values and content style.** Others will propose alternatives or try to align brand messaging with their style to ensure that the campaign translates well with their audiences and that their authenticity stays intact.
  * Influencers are experts in their audience and brand, making collaboration key to creating impactful content. Provide guidance, but trust them to translate ideas effectively.
  * When partnering with influencers, listen to their ideas to balance their style with your messaging and find common ground.


Authenticity and creativity go hand in hand in influencer marketing—stifle one (or worse, both), and influencers risk losing their identity. It’s understandable that **most Southeast Asian influencers consider the lack of creative freedom a major challenge in sponsored collaborations**. For content creators, the freedom to be bold and unique isn’t just about expression; it is what connects them to their audience and, ultimately, what allows them to amplify a partner brand’s message and inspire engagement.
Because personal style and authenticity matter so much to influencers, too many regulations from brands may come off as restrictive and hinder the effectiveness of collaborations. **Unrealistic expectations rank as the second most common challenge faced by influencers, particularly in Singapore and Thailand.** This reflects a potential mismatch between brands’ demands and influencers’ capabilities or audience dynamics.
For a more solid partnership, **Singaporean and Vietnamese influencers urge brands to give out clearer briefs and expectations**. They are more inclined to align their content with brand requirements if those requirements are well-communicated. While this might seem counterintuitive to pursuing more creative freedom, many influencers expressed that when brands better understand their creative process, it helps them craft more tailored briefs, ultimately enabling more effective collaborations.
By contrast, **Filipino and Thai influencers value greater freedom** in the content they create, as it allows them to bring unique perspectives to a brand’s marketing efforts. In the Philippines specifically, influencers have also highlighted the need for more flexible timelines to help balance brand collaborations with other commitments.
Ultimately, influencers are more than happy to meet brands halfway: **69% of influencers said that open communication with brands often enhances their sense of value and loyalty to them** and**38% are willing to offer alternatives to resolve potential conflicts.** Despite the challenges they face, influencers in Southeast Asia are optimistic about the future of their careers, with sustainability scores averaging 4.02/5.
#### What brands should know:
  * Influencers value detailed briefs that outline expectations without stifling their creativity. Clear guidance helps ensure the brand’s message aligns with the influencer’s style, leading to more authentic and effective content.
  * While aligning on key messaging is important, giving influencers the freedom to add their personal touch allows them to connect with their audience more naturally, making campaigns more impactful and meaningful.
  * Transparent dialogue enhances loyalty and encourages influencers to offer solutions for smoother collaborations. When influencers know they are valued and their creative process is acknowledged and respected, they become more willing to adapt and collaborate to overcome challenges.


Korean influencer Jessica Lee was front and center in [our campaign for TikTok Philippines’ Food Fest](https://vero-asean.com/case-study/tiktok-food-fest-video-campaign/), as she explored Manila’s local foodie favorites with the help of the app’s location tagging feature. The choice of influencer is critical, as great campaigns are the result of an alignment of values, audience, and creative vision.
_“Influencers in Vietnam have gained popularity because of their authenticity and their fearlessness to challenge social norms and stereotypes, particularly in a conservative society. This makes them the real voices of the community–and effective channels for addressing sensitive topics with nuance and reliability. Brands that know how to harness this power can build trust, resonate with local values, and drive more meaningful engagement.”_
**_— Doan Ngoc Mai Khanh, Vero Strategic KOLs and Influencer Manager, Vietnam_**
Though higher compensation is on the wishlist of 27% of influencers, **29% of influencers cited long-term partnership opportunities as a major consideration for prioritizing brand collaborations.** This number is higher in Thailand (32%) and reflects a desire among influencers for enduring relationships with brands, as **86% of brand partnerships are structured on a per-project basis.** Though the status quo provides a certain level of flexibility and specificity to campaigns, influencers would appreciate sustained partnerships with the right brands.
Moreover, **influencers across Southeast Asia feel valued by brands when they are given invites to brand events (19%) and early access to products and services (14%).** These perks provide influencers with unique experiences and a greater sense of exclusivity, which can be motivating factors for engagement.
With authenticity always going to be important for influencers, providing them with a chance to showcase their personal values and build their brand is another key opportunity to consider when entering any collaboration. In our experience, campaigns that address social issues and look for unique solutions to collective problems tend to attract the attention of high-visibility celebrities and influencers—who have an added incentive to amplify our message.
14% of Southeast Asian influencers express a growing focus on social issues, such as gender inequality, mental health, and animal rights—the issue at the center of [the #SaveRalph campaign](https://vero-asean.com/case-study/how-saveralph-raised-awareness-of-cosmetics-testing-on-animals-in-southeast-asia/).
#### What brands should know:
  * Fostering long-term partnerships with influencers can help enhance authenticity and ensure consistent brand messaging over a longer period of time.
  * A steady stream of sponsored content with a few influencers across different campaigns can help with brand recognition, conversion, and loyalty.
  * Involve influencers in brand events or invite them to review a new product or service ahead of the market. This not only helps with having them prioritize your brand over others—it also lets them know that you are serious about achieving shared growth together.


_“Long-term partnerships are a game-changer for both brands and influencers. It can enhance brand recognition and loyalty, while empowering influencers to build stronger audience connections through authentic, consistent content. Over time, these collaborations foster trust and deliver meaningful, lasting results for both parties.”_
_**— Adisty Primatya, Vero Creative KOLs Communication Senior Manager, Indonesia** _
Looking ahead,**influencers anticipate an increase in influencer-led events (28%)** and **collaborations with other influencers (23%) in 2025** , pointing to a stronger emphasis on community and strategic partnerships. In response, many are **planning to adapt their content strategies by building stronger communities (23%)** , **diversifying content topics (19%)** , and **incorporating newsletters (16%)** in hopes of fostering deeper engagement and loyalty that brands can tap into.
One of our biggest influencer-led events (an emerging trend that 28% of Southeast Asian influencers have an eye on) was for [Prime Video and the BTS: Yet to Come Concert Movie](https://vero-asean.com/case-study/campaign-for-bts-yet-to-come-concert-movie/). The campaign gathered influencers and BTS fans alike in an unforgettable on-ground activation at Siam Scape.
**Many influencers in Southeast Asia also see a greater emphasis on authenticity (28%).** Part of this is a push from 14% of influencers to have a greater focus on social issues, reflecting a desire to address topics that matter to them and resonate with socially conscious audiences.
When asked about emerging technologies, **most influencers pointed to artificial intelligence (36%)** and **social commerce (33%)** as the ones that are disrupting the world of influencer marketing. With both AI and social commerce on the rise [in recent years](https://vero-asean.com/5-key-trends-that-shaped-the-southeast-asian-marketing-industry-in-2023/), it’s no surprise that influencers are beginning to adapt.
On the one hand, AI offers some exciting opportunities for the creative industry, with the potential for providing AI-driven insights, bridging language gaps, and enhancing efficiency.
Meanwhile, social commerce has been marked by the explosive entrance of the TikTok shop and the rise of Shopee Live, but this phenomenon of more personalized and seamless shopping experiences can take different forms in each market. For instance, the banning of TikTok Shop in Indonesia has given rise to an even busier [live shopping industry](https://vero-asean.com/live-shopping-trend-in-indonesia-is-here-to-stay/) outside of it, while TikTok has been embraced as part of the [Philippines’ budol culture](https://vero-asean.com/i-saw-it-on-tiktok-a-look-at-budol-culture-in-the-philippines-and-what-it-means-for-brands/) for peer and influencer product recommendations.
#### What brands should know:
  * Emphasizing community-building and authenticity can inspire new campaign ideas. Consider influencer-led events or gathering multiple influencers to create buzz across different communities. Newsletters, a growing format for many influencers, can offer a fresh partnership opportunity.
  * The growing focus on social issues provides inspiration for campaigns. Reflect on your brand’s values and the problems your product solves to choose influencers who align with your goals and can make a strong impact.
  * New technologies can boost efficiency, enhance content, and streamline the customer buying journey. Using Instagram’s e-commerce features can drive influencer-driven sales, while AI tools can generate insights from engagement data and create localized content, such as captioned or dubbed videos, for different markets.


_“There’s more than enough evidence that social media as a platform and influencers as trendsetters are going to be bigger, and their impact on society (and economy) will become even more pronounced. The rise of social commerce and the increase of artificial intelligence and other emerging technologies will only amplify their influence in the coming years. Adapting to these changes, with ethical responsibility, will be a make-or-break factor for both influencers and brands in Southeast Asia. It is important to understand the extent of the power of influencers and to leverage it with transparency and accountability.”_
_**— Claudia Pusung, Vero KOLs and Influencer Relations Manager, Indonesia**_
## Every influencer-brand partnership begins with finding the right match
All relationships, by nature, rely on chemistry—that spark of mutual trust and understanding. For partnerships between influencers and brands, this chemistry is defined as the alignment of values, audience, and creative vision.
With a **striking 72% increase in sponsored content requests this year,** as noted by surveyed influencers in Southeast Asia, we can expect an even greater surge in content sponsorships and collaborative campaigns throughout 2025.
Finding the right influencer-brand fit is the first step towards forging a connection that’s both natural and mutually beneficial. And as competition continues to grow, it is more crucial than ever to start any influencer marketing campaign _**right**_.
To ensure the right partnerships are made, Vero created TrueVibe, a proprietary data-led solution to influencer marketing. The TrueVibe index works by scoring influencers for brand fit based on six criteria, namely: Reach, Interests, Engagement, Content Quality, Authority, and Values. This allows Vero’s influencer specialists to methodically pair the right influencers with the right brands, helping pave the way for great working experiences and impactful collaborations.
Vero’s influencer marketing strategy, governed by [InFluent](https://vero-asean.com/vero-introduces-influent-a-new-influencer-marketing-stack-for-brands-and-creators/) – a combination of products and services designed to help brands achieve their business and reputational goals – uncovers hidden opportunities for creative synergy. This ensures campaigns don’t just serve as one-time promotional gimmicks but powerful narratives that drive engagement and long-term loyalty between brands and influencers and with their audiences in Southeast Asia.
As the world of influencer marketing expands and the playbook on effective collaborations gets constantly rewritten, brands and influencers that invest in authenticity and purpose-driven content are set to make noise—one that resonates, gets heard, and creates real impact.
