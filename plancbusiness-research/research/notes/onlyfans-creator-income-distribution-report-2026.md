## OnlyFans Creator Income Distribution Report — 2026 Edition
This report consolidates the best available evidence on how OnlyFans creator income is distributed. Estimated annual income spans four orders of magnitude: the top 0.1% earn about $735,000 per year, the top 1% about $49,000, the top 10% about $5,200, the median creator roughly $180, and the bottom 50% roughly $50 — a top-0.1%-to-median ratio of 4,083×. Cross-checking those tier estimates against engagement we can measure directly (100,200 public creator profiles, June 2026) shows attention is even more concentrated than the income estimates suggest: the top 1% of creators hold 35.5% of all likes and the top 10% hold 82%. Income tiers are panel-derived estimates; the only audited figure is the $5.80B aggregate creator payout in Fenix International's FY2024 filing.

## Average vs median: why this matters
Splitting $5.80B across 4.63M creator accounts gives an arithmetic mean of **$1,253 per creator per year** , or about $104 per month. But that number is misleading. OnlyFans's earnings distribution is one of the most extreme power laws in the consumer internet — analyses of leaked payout data and third-party panel studies consistently show the median creator earns closer to **$180/year** , with the top 1% capturing approximately 33% of all platform payouts. 
Put another way: the average is dragged up by a small number of creators earning seven and eight figures. The median is what most creators actually experience. When you see the "$1,253 average" cited in press coverage, it's the mathematically correct headline, but it is not the typical experience.

The honest finding: **at every point where the two curves can be compared, measured attention is more concentrated than the earnings-tier estimates suggest.** The top 10% of creators hold 82% of all likes versus an estimated ~73% of payouts; the bottom 50% hold roughly 0.2% of likes versus an estimated ~3% of payouts. Two readings are possible: either the earnings estimates understate how winner-take-most the platform really is, or money genuinely spreads slightly wider than attention — plausible, since even low-engagement creators charge real prices (the median subscription is $9.99), which puts a floor under small-creator revenue that likes have no equivalent of. 
Caveats: likes are cumulative lifetime counts, and directory-listed profiles skew toward active, discoverable creators rather than the full 4.63M registered accounts. Treat the comparison as a directional cross-check, not proof. Full engagement data lives on our [creator statistics](https://onlyfansstatistics.com/creators) page. 
## The 80/20 split — what creators keep
OnlyFans uses a flat 80/20 creator-favorable split. Every dollar a fan spends — on subscriptions, pay-per-view, tips, or DMs — sees 80 cents flow to the creator before any other deductions. There are no engagement-based tiers, no carve-outs for top creators, and no minimum thresholds before payouts start. 
The catch is what happens after the 80 cents arrives. From the creator's $0.80: 
  * **Payment processing fees** (typically 2.9% + ~$0.30 per transaction) on the original fan payment, often passed through to creator earnings.
  * **Currency conversion** for non-USD fan payments — typically a 1–3% spread.
  * **Tax** at the creator's local rate (income tax + self-employment tax in the US, VAT obligations in the EU/UK for some categories).


Real take-home for a US-resident creator typically lands at 65–72% of the original fan payment. International creators with VAT obligations and FX spreads can land closer to 55–62%.

## How to read creator income statistics
### The mean-vs-median trap (and three other pitfalls)
  * **The mean is not the typical creator.** Dividing $5.80B in payouts by 4.63M creator accounts gives a $1,253/year mean — but that mean sits _above_ the estimated top-25% threshold (~$1,050/year). On a power-law distribution, the "average" is an income that more than three-quarters of creators never reach. The median (~$180) is the honest headline number.
  * **Watch the denominator.** "Per creator" figures divide by all 4.63M registered accounts, including millions that are dormant or never posted. Medians computed over _active_ creators only run far higher — neither view is wrong, but they answer different questions.
  * **Only one number here is audited.** The $5.80B aggregate payout comes from Fenix International's FY2024 UK filing. Every percentile tier is a panel-derived estimate — useful and directionally consistent across sources, but not disclosure.
  * **Attention proxies are not income.** Like-concentration data (top 10% = 82% of likes) is measured, not estimated, but likes accumulate over a profile's lifetime and don't map one-to-one onto dollars. Use it to sanity-check the shape of the curve, not to read off earnings.


## FAQ
What is the average OnlyFans creator income?
Average creator income is $1,253/year (mean) but the median is ~$180/year. The distribution is long-tail — the top 0.1% earn an estimated $735,000/year, the top 1% ~$49,000, the top 10% ~$5,200, while the bottom 50% earn roughly $50/year.
Who are the top OnlyFans earners?
Reported peak monthly earnings include Blac Chyna ($20M/mo per MTV), Bella Thorne ($11M week-1 per IG), Cardi B ($9.5M/mo per Forbes), Iggy Azalea ($9.2M/mo per Variety), and Coco Austin ($9M/mo self-reported).
How is OnlyFans creator revenue split?
OnlyFans uses an 80/20 creator-favorable split — creators receive 80% of all subscription, tip, PPV, and DM unlock revenue. The platform retains 20% to cover processing, hosting, moderation, compliance, and operations.
What percentage of OnlyFans creators make money?
Nearly every account with a paying subscriber earns something, but the honest tier answer: the bottom 50% of creators earn roughly $50/year, the median is ~$180/year, and reaching the top 10% takes ~$5,200/year. Only about 1 in 100 creators reach the ~$49,000/year top-1% tier, and an estimated ~73% of total payouts flow to the top 10%.
