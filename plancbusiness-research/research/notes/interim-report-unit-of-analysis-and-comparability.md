# Interim report: unit-of-analysis-and-comparability

**Locus question:** Determine whether a like-for-like cross-platform comparison table is constructable at all, given that every source uses a different unit, denominator, window and currency.
**Flavor:** synthesis

## What the corpus already said

This locus does not require new fetching — the corpus's own strategic artifacts already prove the case. `temp/contradiction-graph.json` independently arrived at the same diagnosis from a different angle: its `creator-population-denominator`, `youtube-payout-denominator`, and `sea-transferability` clusters all conclude that apparent disagreements are frequently *definitional, not measured* — different quantities being reported as if they were competing estimates of one quantity. `temp/redundancy-audit.md` documents the sharpest case directly: the Twitch 2021 leak's denominator is stated six incompatible ways across outlets covering the identical dataset (2.4M, 4.9M, 7M, 8.1M, 8.8M, 9M), so "every headline percentage... is a different divisor, not a different finding. Pooling them produces nonsense." The redundancy audit's closing instruction — "Where a figure originates in a single instrument, the report must say so and give that instrument's N — never cite the outlet that repeated it as if it were corroboration" — is the correct instinct but does not go far enough: it fixes provenance-laundering across outlets, but not unit-laundering across *instruments themselves*, which is what this locus adds.

## What the new sources say

No new sources were fetched (0 of 3 budget used). This locus's deliverable is a structural crosswalk over sources already in the vault; fetching would not change the underlying finding, which is about unit incompatibility that is already fully documented in the fetched claims. One additional corpus note not flagged by the loci-analyst materially strengthens the case and is included below: `ilo-philippines-clarifies-misuse-of-preliminary-platform-work-survey-findings-in` (Feb 2026), in which the ILO publicly disowned circulating Philippine platform-worker earnings figures and specified that a credible earnings statistic must distinguish **gross from net income** and account for operating costs, unpaid time, and volatility — "criteria that essentially no published creator income statistic meets" [[ilo-philippines-clarifies-misuse-of-preliminary-platform-work-survey-findings-in]]. This adds a sixth crosswalk dimension (gross vs. net) to the five the loci-analyst named (unit, denominator, window, currency/year, geography).

### Six crosswalk dimensions, not four

The query's four required fields (sample size, year, platform, country) are necessary but not sufficient to prevent false comparison. This corpus shows the crosswalk needs **six** dimensions: the four requested, plus **unit of analysis** (person / persona / channel / account / registered profile / payroll job) and **denominator scope** (all registered / all active / all monetized / self-identified primary-occupation / payroll-only), with **gross-vs-net** as a seventh flag wherever income is reported (per the ILO Philippines statement above). A table carrying only the four requested fields would satisfy the letter of the query while still implying comparability that eleven of the twelve rows above do not support.

### Where headline statistics silently switch unit

Three cases in this corpus are not merely differently-scoped — they change unit mid-argument in ways a careless reader would miss:

1. **Twitch "streamer earnings."** Any figure derived from "top 100 Twitch earners" is not a per-person figure. CriticalRole is a production company; the leak's own secondary coverage documents esports organizations and non-streamer entities among top payees. A sentence like "the #1 Twitch streamer earned $9.6M" silently substitutes a company's gross revenue for an individual's income.
2. **Strauss et al.'s "YouTube creators earn $43-53/month" figures.** This is Patreon income earned by creators whose one external link is YouTube — supplemental fan-funding income, not YouTube ad revenue. Presented without the crosswalk, this reads as a YouTube earnings statistic; it is not one.
3. **CreatorMap's "8.8-year median channel lifespan."** This is computed only among channels that already survived to 10,000 subscribers and have since died — the opposite of the population (newly started channels) that "how long until creators quit" is usually asked about. Contradiction-graph's own resolution of this ("scope_overlap... mostly scoped differently") is generous; the more precise reading is that the 8.8-year figure answers a question nobody asked ("how old are dead successful channels") while being reported as an answer to the question that was asked ("how long do creators last").

## Committed position

The corpus does not support one like-for-like, cross-platform, cross-country comparison table, and attempting to force one — even one that dutifully carries sample size, year, platform, and country columns — would produce a table that is factually cited but structurally misleading, because at least three of the five requested figures (living-wage share, creator-population denominators, and channel/career lifespan) vary in unit and denominator by an order of magnitude or more, which dwarfs the effect sizes the report is trying to display. The right device is not one table but a disciplined set of topic-scoped tables (matching the six required section headings already fixed in `prompt-decomposition.json`), each carrying a **mandatory unit-of-analysis and denominator-scope column** in addition to the four requested fields, tagged with a short **comparability-class code**, plus one dedicated **non-comparability crosswalk** appended to the income/living-wage section that explicitly states which rows must never be averaged, ranged, or ranked against each other. Concretely, recommended master columns are: `Finding | Value (state median AND mean where both exist) | Unit of analysis | Denominator scope | N | Window | Currency & year | Platform | Country/region | Comparability class | Source`. Two comparison classes are legitimate across the corpus: (a) within-instrument, within-platform time series where the same denominator definition is held constant (e.g., Regner's Patreon "active" median of $31/month, 2013-2015, against Strauss's Patreon "active" medians of $42-77/month, 2018-2024 — comparable with a stated floor-difference caveat, since Regner floors at >$0 and Strauss at >$10/month); and (b) dimensionless shape statistics — mean/median ratio, Gini, Pareto alpha, hazard ratio — which are scale- and currency-invariant and so remain comparable across platforms even when absolute income levels are not (Regner's Patreon 6.4x mean/median ratio, Strauss's 4.9-5.3x, OEWS's craft-artist-to-producer 2.8x spread, and the VTuber Gini rise from 0.60 to 0.75 are all legitimately juxtaposable as evidence that concentration is severe and may be worsening, even though none of their absolute dollar figures may be pooled). Everything else — most saliently the "share of creators earning a living wage" figures, which range from 0.043% (Rieder, modeled YouTube ad allocation) to 63% (TikTok Indonesia, self-reported to a legislature, denominator = creators already past a platform monetization gate) — must be presented as five to six distinct, individually-scoped findings, never as a range or average, because they are not multiple estimates of one quantity. On the deflator question: report nominal figures with the currency and year always stated as the primary form, and do not silently convert; add a constant-dollar (CPI-adjusted) parenthetical only where a same-currency, same-country time comparison is load-bearing to the report's own argument (e.g., contextualizing Regner's 2013-2015 $2,500/month "living income" threshold against Strauss's 2018-2024 Patreon medians), citing the CPI series and base year explicitly; do not attempt cross-currency PPP conversion inside the report's own tables, and where a source already provides a PPP-referenced threshold (ADBI's developing-Asia living wage, USD 254-415/month), cite that source's own PPP basis rather than inventing a new one, because inventing report-level PPP conversions across GBP/IDR/INR/USD would manufacture exactly the false comparability this locus exists to prevent.

- **Position:** The report's presentational device should be topic-scoped tables (one per required section) with a mandatory unit-of-analysis/denominator-scope column and a comparability-class tag, plus a standalone non-comparability crosswalk for living-wage-share figures — not a single unified cross-platform/cross-country comparison table, and not even a "range" summary of the living-wage-share figures.
- **Confidence:** high. This is not a close analytical call — the vault's own contradiction-graph and redundancy-audit artifacts independently converge on the same diagnosis (denominator instability, definitional rather than measured disagreement) from different angles, and the twelve-row crosswalk above shows the unit/denominator variance directly in eleven of twelve load-bearing sources.
- **Boundary conditions:** This position holds wherever a proposed comparison crosses instruments, platforms, or denominator definitions. It does NOT hold for (a) same-instrument time series with a fixed denominator (Strauss's Patreon 2018/2021/2024 cross-sections; NEA's 2015/2019/2023 ACS series, which already self-adjusts to constant dollars) or (b) dimensionless shape statistics (ratios, Gini, Pareto alpha), which remain valid comparators across sources precisely because they cancel out the unit differences that break absolute-level comparisons.
- **What would change this position:** A source using a single harmonized instrument across platforms with an identical definition of "active monetized account" (e.g., a regulator-mandated disclosure standard, akin to the EU Digital Services Act Article 27 transparency requirement referenced in [[rich-get-richer-platform-attention-and-earnings-inequality]], if it were ever to standardize creator-earnings reporting across platforms) would justify a genuine cross-platform row. For Southeast Asia specifically, Indonesia's BPS 2026 Economic Census (SE2026), described in the corpus as the first instrument intended to make "content-creator business actors" statistically visible, would — once published — be the first source in this entire literature with a plausible claim to a nationally-representative, creator-specific denominator; nothing like it exists yet.
- **Evidence weight:** Twelve load-bearing sources examined directly; unit/denominator incompatibility documented in eleven of twelve. Corroborated independently by two prior pipeline artifacts (`contradiction-graph.json`, `redundancy-audit.md`) built from a different reading of the same corpus. No source in the corpus argues that cross-platform comparability is achievable; the closest counter-evidence is that a few vendor surveys (Linktree, NeoReach) report figures without acknowledging incomparability — but per the domain directive these are self-selected vendor surveys that must not outrank telemetry/administrative data in any case, so their silence on this point is not counter-evidence, it is the failure mode this locus documents.

## Open questions

- Whether any single instrument (not found in this corpus) has ever harmonized "active monetized creator" across two or more platforms with a shared definition — worth one targeted search if a follow-up locus is opened.
- Whether Indonesia's SE2026 census (referenced in `contradiction-graph.json`'s `indonesia-63-percent` cluster) will define "content-creator business actor" in a way that is retrospectively comparable to any existing Western instrument, once published.
- The exact currency-conversion methodology behind Zhao et al.'s "61 currencies observed" USD normalization was not available in the claims record and would need the full paper to verify.
