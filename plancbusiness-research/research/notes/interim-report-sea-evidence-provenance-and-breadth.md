# Interim report: sea-evidence-provenance-and-breadth

**Locus question:** Establish what quantitative creator evidence exists for Southeast Asia beyond Indonesia, and finish tracing the one national-scale figure to a primary record.
**Flavor:** dialectical

## What the corpus already said

Before this investigation, the corpus held 14 Indonesia-tagged notes, 2 Philippines-tagged (both freelancing/gig-work, not creator), and zero for Vietnam, Thailand, Malaysia, or Singapore. The width sweep had already identified the "8 million creators, 63% above minimum wage" figure as the single highest-value SEA datapoint and traced it as far as TikTok Indonesia's testimony to DPR Komisi I on 15 July 2025 (Hilmi Adrianto), separately repeated by Menekraf Teuku Riefky Harsya as if it were a national statistic [[menekraf-8-juta-orang-berprofesi-konten-kreatof-63-persen-gajinya-di-atas-umr-ku]] [[tiktok-buka-data-63-persen-kreator-di-indonesia-berpenghasilan-tinggi-merdekacom]] [[pendapatan-63-persen-konten-kreator-di-tiktok-di-atas-umr-tempoco]]. The corpus had already confirmed BPS publishes **no** official content-creator count — only a much broader "creative economy workforce" series (27.4M, 2025) — and that the 2026 Economic Census (SE2026) is billed as the first instrument to make content-creator business actors statistically visible [[bps-dan-kemenekraf-bersinergi-kreator-lokal-dilibatkan-dalam-sosialisasi-se2026]]. A separate, non-reconcilable "only 14% profitable" figure was already flagged as tracing to an unverifiable Linktree/IMF-attributed claim of ambiguous geographic scope [[ada-8-juta-kreator-konten-tapi-baru-14-persen-yang-cuan]], and the contradiction graph (`indonesia-63-percent`, `sea-transferability` clusters) had already concluded Side B (platform-statistic-repackaged-as-national, no methodology) was evidentially stronger than Side A (bare ministerial assertion). The `sea-transferability` cluster had also already assembled three independently-measured structural breaks between Western and SEA creator economics: a 5-6x RPM/geographic monetization gap [[httpsdoiorg10117720563051241234693]], ~90% brand-sponsorship income composition for Indonesian influencers versus ad-revenue-dominated Western corpora [[vero-indonesia-influencer-whitepaper]] (referenced via [[impact-engagement-and-the-future-of-influencer-marketing-in-southeast-asia]]), and the World Bank's finding that 71% of Indonesian e-commerce participants sell exclusively via social media/chat apps — i.e., "making money on social media" in Indonesia is substantially C2C selling, not content monetization [[technologies]]. Adjacent but explicitly NOT creator evidence: PIDS's Philippine online-freelancing paper [[discussion-paper-series-no-2020-29]], the ILO/World Bank gig-work literature [[x-policy-issues-in-the-digital]] [[x-opportunities-and-challenges]] [[the-promise-and-peril]], and the ADBI living-wage denominators for developing Asia (USD 254.28-414.88/month) [[adbi-working-paper-series]] which any SEA creator-income figure must be judged against.

## What the new sources say

**Provenance trace (Task 1).** Four new fetches close the chain. The official DPR RI record of the 15 July 2025 hearing — a Rapat Dengar Pendapat Umum (RDPU), Panja Penyiaran, Komisi I, attended by Google/YouTube, Meta and TikTok — was recovered at jdih.dpr.go.id after both www.dpr.go.id (Akamai-blocked) and emedia.dpr.go.id (empty scrape) failed [[jdih-setjen-dpr-ri-jaringan-dokumentasi-dan-informasi-hukum-sekretariat-jenderal]]. It confirms Hilmi Adrianto stated on the record: 125 million MAU, 8 million creators of whom 63% earn above minimum wage, 21 million active local sellers on TikTok Shop/Tokopedia, 60% of promotional content supporting local products — with **zero methodology stated anywhere in the official record**. The same record shows TikTok's tax-remittance report was described as "only accessible to limited circles and not yet publicly available," and Deputy Chair Dave Akbarshah Fikarno Laksono explicitly pressed for independent economic-contribution data rather than platform self-reporting.

A second new source, Bloomberg Technoz's contemporaneous coverage, adds the single most load-bearing new detail in this investigation [[anggota-dpr-cecar-omzet-tiktok-mengaku-belum-siap-data-teknologi]]: MP Nico Siahaan directly pressed YouTube, Meta and TikTok for actual Indonesia revenue figures, and Hilmi Adrianto — the same official who had just cited the precise 8-million/63% figures — answered on the record: "Data tersebut saya belum memiliki saat ini pak, mungkin saya bisa sampaikan secara tertulis nanti" ("I don't have that data right now, sir, perhaps I can submit it in writing later"). No written follow-up submission has surfaced in any source located.

The third and most consequential new source pushes the origin back a full year and changes the finding's character. Kompas.id's 10 July 2024 article — a year *before* the parliamentary hearing, covering a TikTok/PT Pos Indonesia "Creator House" launch — already carries the "63 percent... more than minimum wage" language, attributed not to a hearing but to "The TikTok Effect: Accelerating Southeast Asia's Businesses, Education and Community Report," released by TikTok in June 2023, via TikTok Indonesia's Director of Communications Anggini Setiawan, who separately cites "8 million+ creators" from unspecified internal data as of December 2023 [[63-percent-of-tiktok-creators-earn-more-than-minimum-wage]]. That means the 8-million figure and the 63% figure are **two different, uncited internal claims that were already circulating bundled together a year before the DPR testimony** — the hearing did not originate them, it recycled them.

The fourth new source is the closest thing to a primary methodology document: Kadence International's own summary of the report it built for TikTok, covering 9 Southeast Asian markets with n=3,400+ TikTok users plus interviews with 25 non-profit organizations, mixed online-survey and offline-interview method [[tiktoks-socio-economic-impact-in-southeast-asia-a-summary-of-the-report-kadence]]. Critically, **the vendor's own methodology summary of its own named report does not contain the "63 percent" or "8 million" figures at all.** Its only earnings-related statement is the non-numeric "Most creators using TikTok as a source of income earn more than the minimum wage in their respective countries" — no Indonesia-specific sample size, no creator definition, no revenue threshold, no date range. Searches for a downloadable PDF of "The TikTok Effect" itself, a BPS statement using the phrase "kreator konten," and a TikTok Indonesia newsroom post disclosing methodology all came back empty — documented absences, not fetch failures.

**Breadth beyond Indonesia (Task 2).** Five fetches were made across Thailand, Vietnam, Philippines, Malaysia, and Singapore; all five were deprecated on review because none cleared the creator-specific quantitative bar — itself the finding. Searches in Thai ("รายได้ยูทูปเบอร์", "อาชีพครีเอเตอร์ สถิติ", "อินฟลูเอนเซอร์ รายได้ สำรวจ") plus a targeted NSO Thailand query returned only top-earner puff pieces with no methodology or distributional data; NSO Thailand's Labour Force Survey (2023 wave, 76,735 households) shows no evidence of coding content creator as a distinct occupation. Vietnamese-language search ("khảo sát thu nhập nhà sáng tạo nội dung KOL Việt Nam") surfaced two Vietnamese-titled pieces that on inspection are Western-data pass-throughs: a "Content Creator Income Report 2025" whose body paraphrases NeoReach's global survey against a US living-wage benchmark with zero Vietnam-specific data [[báo-cáo-thu-nhập-nhà-sáng-tạo-nội-dung-2025]], and a kenh14.vn piece that is Vietnamese-language but profiles three American creators using Goldman Sachs/NeoReach/Emarketer US figures [[khổ-như-làm-kol-mạng-vỡ-mộng-làm-giàu-bằng-tiktok-youtube-có-người-chẳng-đủ-tiền]]. The Philippines search found that PSA does publish a Philippine Creative Economy Satellite Account (PHP 1.94 trillion GVA, 2024, 7.51 million employed, 15.4% of total employment) — but this is an industry national-accounts aggregate (advertising, publishing, design, film) at the wrong unit of analysis entirely, not an individual creator income or attrition measurement; everything else found was generic JobStreet/Glassdoor salary bands for employed media roles (PHP 23k-33k/month), not creator income. Malaysia's DOSM tracks gig/informal workers (559k-4M depending on definition; median informal-worker income RM2,000 vs. national median RM3,600) with no content-creator breakout — this is exactly the generic gig-work population the brief requires excluding. Singapore produced one promising but unconfirmed lead: trade press (Campaign Asia, Campaign US) cites an IMDA 2024 survey finding roughly 8% of Singaporeans aged 18-35 identify content creation as a primary income source or career goal, typical income S$2,500-6,000/month, with no CPF, paid leave, or health insurance [[why-its-time-we-took-singaporean-content-creators-more-seriously-campaign-asia]] [[why-its-time-we-took-singaporean-content-creators-more-seriously-campaign-us]]. Both article fetches hit hard paywalls and were deprecated; the original IMDA publication could not be located at imda.gov.sg despite a targeted attempt. This claim survives only as a low-confidence, unverified search-snippet-level assertion, not verified primary text.

## Evidence synthesis

The Indonesian figure and the SEA-breadth question turn out to be two faces of the same finding: **regional creator-economy "data" is overwhelmingly platform or vendor marketing material, recirculated through media and political testimony until it acquires the appearance of an independent, government-grade statistic — and independent efforts to verify it consistently fail to locate a sample frame, a creator definition, a date range, or a wage comparator.** The Indonesian trace is the clearest demonstration: a single bundled 8-million/63% claim, sourced to TikTok's own June 2023 marketing report ("The TikTok Effect"), was repeated by TikTok's own communications director in July 2024, repeated again by TikTok's own public-policy head at a July 2025 parliamentary hearing (where he simultaneously admitted under direct questioning that he lacked the underlying revenue data), and repeated a third time by Indonesia's Minister of Creative Economy as a national statistic. At no point in this four-instance, two-year chain does a sample frame, a definition of "content creator" (follower/monetization/activity threshold), a stated date range, or a stated minimum-wage comparator (which UMR, which year) ever surface. The vendor's own methodology document — the one artifact that should contain this information — does not even contain the numbers being attributed to it.

### Position A: the figure can be cited as a directional signal of Indonesian creator earnings

TikTok's platform-monetization data, even self-reported, is not worthless: it is the largest platform in the country by the stated 125 million MAU figure, and its earnings claim is at least a real corporate assertion made under some accountability pressure (parliamentary testimony carries legal and reputational stakes that a marketing brochure does not). Multiple independent Indonesian outlets (merdeka.com, tempo.co, Kompas.id, Bloomberg Technoz) converged on repeating the same number without contradicting it, and it is consistent across two years and two contexts (marketing report, parliamentary testimony), suggesting it reflects a real internal number TikTok has settled on, not a one-off fabrication. In a region where almost no other creator-income measurement exists at all, a repeatedly-cited platform figure — properly labeled as platform-specific and methodology-free — is better than a total absence of any number, and readers of a report on this topic will expect the Indonesian figure to appear somewhere given how widely it circulates.

### Position B: the figure cannot be cited as evidence of anything except TikTok's messaging discipline

The figure fails on every axis the domain norms in this run require: it is not administrative, not peer-reviewed, not even a vendor survey with a disclosed sample — it is a bare corporate assertion that the vendor's own methodology summary does not contain. The person delivering it at the moment of maximum accountability (sworn-adjacent parliamentary testimony) admitted, in the same breath, that he did not have the underlying data when a legislator asked for it directly — this is not incidental, it is dispositive: the official source of the figure could not substantiate it when directly tested. The apparent "independent corroboration" across merdeka.com/tempo.co/Kompas.id is not independent corroboration at all — it is four outlets quoting the same unaudited platform talking point, which is exactly the mechanism that makes a marketing number look like a fact through repetition (the identical failure mode the ILO explicitly warned about in the Philippines platform-work leak episode, where a draft, unvalidated figure escaped into media and had to be publicly disowned). A national minister repeating a single platform's self-reported statistic as if it summed the entire national creator economy, when the country's own statistics agency (BPS) has no comparable series and says its first attempt to measure this won't exist until the 2026 census, is the definitional case of a press-cited round number with no traceable methodology — which this run's own evidence norms require excluding from the report, or citing only with that status explicitly stated.

Between these, Position B is stronger, for a decisive reason: this is not a dispute about whether the underlying phenomenon (some Indonesian creators earning well) is real — it obviously is — but about whether "8 million / 63%" is a *measurement* of it, and on that narrower question, the chain of custody breaks at every link. The number should appear in the final report, but only as a case study of provenance failure, never as a data point in an income-distribution table.

## Committed position

The Southeast Asian evidence base for this query is not merely thin, it is asymmetric in a way that changes what the report can honestly claim under required heading 5: Indonesia has one repeatedly-cited but non-traceable platform figure, the Philippines has an industry-aggregate national-accounts statistic at the wrong unit of analysis, Singapore has one plausible but unverified survey claim recoverable only from paywalled trade-press snippets, and Thailand, Vietnam, and Malaysia have nothing that clears the bar of creator-specific quantitative evidence at all — despite dedicated local-language searching. Layered on top of this, a second and arguably more important structural finding emerged from the Vietnam search specifically: regional creator-economy media does not fail to cover the topic, it recirculates Western aggregate figures (NeoReach, Goldman Sachs, Emarketer) under local-language headlines and bylines, meaning a reader in the region who encounters what looks like local coverage of local creator income is very often reading US benchmarks in translation, with no disclosure that the underlying sample is not local. This is a second, distinct mechanism of non-generalizability beyond the three the corpus's `sea-transferability` cluster had already measured (RPM gap, income-composition difference, living-wage denominator gap): it is not just that Western figures don't transfer to SEA, it is that SEA media actively presents Western figures as if they already were local ones. The report's required heading 5 must therefore make three separate moves, not one: (1) state the general non-transferability finding with its three measured mechanisms, now joined by this fourth "borrowed-authority" pattern; (2) present the Indonesian 8-million/63% figure explicitly as a traced case study in provenance failure — naming the TikTok marketing-report origin, the year-long circulation before the parliamentary repetition, and the official's on-record admission that he lacked the underlying data — rather than as a data point; and (3) state plainly, per country, that Thailand/Vietnam/Malaysia yielded no creator-specific quantitative evidence despite targeted local-language and national-statistics-office search, that the Philippines has only an industry-GVA aggregate, and that Singapore has one unverified lead worth flagging as a research gap rather than a citable finding.

- **Position:** No Southeast Asian country currently has creator-specific quantitative evidence (income distribution, attrition/survival, or time-to-income) that meets this run's evidence-quality floor; the region's most widely-circulated figure (Indonesia's "8 million/63%") is traceable to an unaudited TikTok marketing claim whose own vendor-methodology document does not contain the numbers, and whose official messenger admitted under direct legislative questioning that he lacked the underlying data — it should be reported as a case study in provenance failure, not cited as an income-distribution data point; Thailand, Vietnam, and Malaysia return a genuine documented absence of creator-specific evidence, the Philippines has only a wrong-unit industry aggregate, and Singapore has exactly one unverified, paywalled survey claim.
- **Confidence:** high on the Indonesia provenance conclusion (four independent, mutually-reinforcing sources traced across two years, including the official DPR record and the vendor's own methodology summary); medium-high on the "nothing in TH/VN/MY" absence (targeted local-language search plus national-statistics-office queries came up empty, but absence-of-evidence claims can never be fully certain — a single unindexed thesis or agency report could exist); low on the Singapore IMDA figure specifically (single unverified secondary citation, original not located, could be a paywall-distorted or misreported summary of the real IMDA publication).
- **Boundary conditions:** the Indonesia provenance-failure finding applies specifically to the "8 million / 63% above minimum wage" figure and its lineage; it does not mean no Indonesian creator data exists at all (the vault separately holds Vero's ~150-influencer income-composition survey and Rieder et al.'s YouTube monetization-linking analysis, both of which are weak but at least methodologically stated). The TH/VN/MY absence claim holds for content-creation specifically; it does not extend to platform gig work (ride-hailing, delivery, freelancing), where DOSM Malaysia, ILO, and World Bank data are comparatively rich — that population must not be substituted for creator data, per this locus's core mandate.
- **What would change this position:** recovery of the actual "TikTok Effect" (June 2023) PDF with a disclosed sample and creator definition would upgrade the Indonesia figure from "case study in failure" to "weak but methodologically-stated vendor survey" (still not administrative-grade, but citable with appropriate caveats); location of the underlying IMDA Singapore publication (rather than trade-press citations of it) would upgrade that finding from low to medium confidence; discovery of any Thai, Vietnamese, or Malaysian university thesis, national-statistics-office micro-survey, or platform transparency disclosure specifically measuring individual creator earnings or channel survival would overturn the "nothing" verdict for that country — none was found despite a genuine attempt, but the search was bounded by an 8-9 fetch budget and could not exhaustively cover Google Scholar or grey academic repositories in local languages.
- **Evidence weight:** Indonesia provenance — 4 independent primary/near-primary sources (official DPR record, contemporaneous Bloomberg Technoz reporting, year-earlier Kompas.id piece, vendor's own methodology summary) all converge on "no disclosed methodology, no data when tested"; zero sources contradict this. SEA breadth — 5 targeted fetches across 5 countries, all deprecated as not meeting the bar, plus prior corpus searches (2 Philippines gig-work notes, extensive brand-side marketing reports) that independently corroborate the "brand-side-only" pattern for the region's published creator-economy literature.

## Open questions

- Does "The TikTok Effect" (TikTok, June 2023) exist as a downloadable primary document anywhere, and if recovered, does it disclose an Indonesia-specific sample and a definition of "creator"?
- Was a written follow-up ever submitted by TikTok Indonesia to DPR Komisi I after Hilmi Adrianto's "I don't have that data now" admission, and if so, does it appear in any subsequent DPR JDIH record?
- Does the IMDA (Singapore) publication behind the Campaign Asia/Campaign US "8% of 18-35-year-olds" figure exist and disclose a sample size and instrument? (imda.gov.sg/about-imda/research-and-statistics/tech-and-media-talent was identified as an unfetched lead.)
- Would a dedicated Google Scholar sweep in Thai, Vietnamese, or Bahasa Malaysia (university theses specifically) surface anything the general web search missed? This locus's budget did not allow exhaustive academic-repository search in three additional languages.
- Is there a Thai NSO Labour Force Survey microdata cut, or a Vietnamese GSO module, that codes "content creator" or "influencer" as a distinct occupational category even if never published as a standalone report?
