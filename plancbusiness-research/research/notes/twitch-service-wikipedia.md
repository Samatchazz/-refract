**Twitch** is an American video [live-streaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live-streaming "Live-streaming") service popular in [video games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_live_streaming "Video game live streaming"), including broadcasts of [esports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esports "Esports") competitions. It also offers music broadcasts, creative content, and "in [real life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_life "Real life")" streams. Twitch is operated by Twitch Interactive, a subsidiary of [Amazon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_\(company\) "Amazon \(company\)"). It was introduced in June 2011 as a spin-off of the general-interest streaming platform [Justin.tv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin.tv "Justin.tv").
Content on the site can be viewed either live or via [video on demand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_on_demand "Video on demand"). The games shown on Twitch's current[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Twitch_\(service\)&action=edit)
The popularity of Twitch eclipsed that of Justin.tv. In October 2013, the website had 45 million unique viewers, and by February 2014, it was considered the fourth-largest source of peak [Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet "Internet") traffic in the United States. At the same time, Justin.tv's parent company was re-branded as Twitch Interactive to represent the shift in focus when Justin.tv was getting shut down in August 2014. The same month, the service was acquired by Amazon for US$970 million, which later led to the introduction of synergies with the company's subscription service [Amazon Prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Prime "Amazon Prime").
By 2015, Twitch had more than 100 million viewers per month. In 2017, Twitch remained the leading live-streaming video service for video games in the US, and had an advantage over [YouTube Gaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube_Gaming "YouTube Gaming"), which shut down its standalone app in May 2019. As of February2020,[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Twitch_\(service\)&action=edit)[concurrent users](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concurrent_user "Concurrent user"). As of May2018,[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Twitch_\(service\)&action=edit) As of January2025,[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Twitch_\(service\)&action=edit)[30th-most-visited website in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_most-visited_websites "List of most-visited websites") with 23.46% of its traffic coming from the United States, followed by Russia with 8.87%, Germany with 7.08% and France with 6.26%. In late 2023, Twitch announced that it would stop operating in South Korea in 2024 because of its [network fee policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_in_South_Korea#Network_Fee_policy "Internet in South Korea"), citing prohibitive costs.
## History
### Founding and initial growth (2007–2013)
When [Justin.tv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin.tv "Justin.tv") was launched in 2007 by [Justin Kan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Kan "Justin Kan") and [Emmett Shear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmett_Shear "Emmett Shear"), two recent [Yale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University") graduates, the site was divided into several content categories. The gaming category grew especially fast, and became the most popular content on the site. In June 2011, the company decided to spin off the gaming content as TwitchTV, inspired by the term . It launched officially in public beta on June 6, 2011. Since then, Twitch has attracted more than 35 million unique visitors a month. Twitch had about 80 employees in June 2013, which increased to 100 by December 2013. The company was headquartered in [San Francisco's Financial District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_District,_San_Francisco "Financial District, San Francisco").
Twitch has been supported by significant investments of [venture capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venture_capital "Venture capital"), with US$15 million in 2012 (on top of US$7 million originally raised for Justin.tv), and US$20 million in 2013. Investors during three rounds of fund raising leading up to the end of 2013 included [Draper Associates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Draper_Associates "Draper Associates"), [Bessemer Venture Partners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bessemer_Venture_Partners "Bessemer Venture Partners") and [Thrive Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrive_Capital "Thrive Capital"). In addition to the influx of venture funding, it was believed in 2013 that the company had become profitable.
Especially since the shutdown of its direct competitor Own3d.tv in early 2013, Twitch has become the most popular e-sports streaming service by a large margin, leading some to conclude that the website has a "near monopoly on the market". Competing video services, such as [YouTube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube "YouTube") and [Dailymotion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dailymotion "Dailymotion"), began to increase the prominence of their gaming content to compete, but have had a much smaller impact so far. As of mid-2013, there were over 43 million viewers on Twitch monthly, with the average viewer watching an hour and a half a day. By February 2014, Twitch was the fourth largest source of Internet traffic during peak times in the United States, behind [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix"), [Google](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google "Google"), and [Apple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_Inc. "Apple Inc."). Twitch made up 1.8% of total US Internet traffic during peak periods.
In late 2013, particularly due to increasing viewership, Twitch had issues with [lag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latency_\(engineering\) "Latency \(engineering\)") and low [frame rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frame_rate "Frame rate") in Europe. Twitch has subsequently added new servers in the region. Also in order to address these problems, Twitch implemented a new video system shown to be more efficient than the previous system. Initially, the new video system was criticized by users because it caused a significant stream delay, interfering with broadcaster–viewer interaction.[] Twitch staff said that the increased delay was likely temporary and at the time, was an acceptable trade-off for the decrease in [buffering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_buffer "Data buffer").
### Growth, YouTube acquisition speculation (2014)
Twitch headquarters at 350 Bush Street in San Francisco (which reuses the facade of the old [San Francisco Curb Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Curb_Exchange "San Francisco Curb Exchange") as an entrance hall for the modern office tower behind it)
On February 10, 2014, Twitch's parent company (Justin.tv, Inc.) was renamed Twitch Interactive, reflecting the increased prominence of the service over Justin.tv as the company's main business. That same month, a stream known as [Twitch Plays _Pokémon_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_Plays_Pok%C3%A9mon "Twitch Plays Pokémon"), a [crowdsourced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crowdsourcing "Crowdsourcing") attempt to play using a system translating chat commands into game controls, went viral. By February 17, the channel reached over 6.5 million total views and averaged concurrent viewership between 60 and 70,000 viewers with at least 10% participating. Vice President of Marketing Matthew DiPietro praised the stream as "one more example of how video games have become a platform for entertainment and creativity that extends WAY beyond the original intent of the game creator. By merging a video game, live video and a participatory experience, the broadcaster has created an entertainment hybrid custom made for the Twitch community. This is a wonderful proof of concept that we hope to see more of in the future." Beginning with its [2014 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E3_2014 "E3 2014"), Twitch was made the official live-streaming platform of the [Electronic Entertainment Expo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Entertainment_Expo "Electronic Entertainment Expo").
On May 18, 2014, first reported that Google had reached a preliminary deal to acquire Twitch through its YouTube subsidiary for approximately US$1 billion.
On August 5, 2014, the original Justin.tv site suddenly ceased operations, citing a need to focus resources entirely on Twitch. On August 6, 2014, Twitch introduced an updated archive system, with multi-platform access to highlights from past broadcasts by a channel, higher quality video, increased server backups, and a new Video Manager interface for managing past broadcasts and compiling "highlights" from broadcasts that can also be exported to YouTube. Due to technological limitations and resource requirements, the new system contained several regressions; the option to archive complete broadcasts on an indefinite basis ("save forever") was removed, meaning that they can only be retained for a maximum of 14 days, or 60 for partners and Turbo subscribers. While compiled highlights can be archived indefinitely, they were limited to two hours in length. In addition, Twitch introduced a copyright fingerprinting system that would mute audio in archived clips if it detected a copyrighted song in the stream.
### Amazon subsidiary (2014–present)
Twitch booth at the 2018 [PAX West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PAX_West "PAX West")
On August 25, 2014, Amazon acquired Twitch Interactive for US$970 million in an [all-cash deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-cash_deal "All-cash deal"). Sources reported that the rumoured Google deal had fallen through and allowed Amazon to make the bid, with reporting that Google had backed out of the deal due to potential [antitrust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antitrust_law "Antitrust law") concerns surrounding it and its existing ownership of YouTube. The acquisition closed on September 25, 2014. [Take-Two Interactive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Take-Two_Interactive "Take-Two Interactive"), which owned a 2% stake at the time of the acquisition, made a windfall of $22 million.
Under Amazon, Shear continued as [chief executive officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_executive_officer "Chief executive officer") of Twitch Interactive, with Sara Clemens added to the executive team as [chief operating officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_operating_officer "Chief operating officer") in January 2018. Shear touted the [Amazon Web Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Web_Services "Amazon Web Services") platform as an "attractive" aspect of the deal, and that Amazon had "built relationships with the big players in media", which could be used to the service's advantage—particularly in the realm of content licensing. The purchase of Twitch marked the third recent video gaming–oriented acquisition by Amazon, which had previously acquired the developers [Reflexive Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflexive_Entertainment "Reflexive Entertainment") and [Double Helix Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_Helix_Games "Double Helix Games").
On December 9, 2014, Twitch announced it had acquired GoodGame Agency, an organisation that owns the [esports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esports "Esports") teams [Evil Geniuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evil_Geniuses "Evil Geniuses") and [Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_\(esports\) "Alliance \(esports\)"). In March 2015, Twitch reset all user passwords and disabled all connections to external [Twitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitter "Twitter") and YouTube accounts after the service reported that someone had gained "unauthorised access" to the user information of some Twitch users.
In June 2016, Twitch added a new feature known as "Cheering", a special form of emoticon purchased as a [microtransaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microtransaction "Microtransaction") using an in-site currency known as "Bits". Bits are bought using [Amazon Payments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Payments "Amazon Payments"), and cheers act as donations to the channel. Users also earn badges within a channel based on how much they have cheered.
On August 1, 2016, it was reported that Twitch had signed a lease for 185,000 square feet (17,187 m2) in a new office tower to be constructed at 350 Bush Street in San Francisco.
On August 16, 2016, Twitch acquired [Curse LLC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curse_LLC "Curse LLC"), an operator of online video gaming communities and gaming-oriented VoIP software. In December 2016, GoodGame Agency was divested by Amazon to their respective members due to [conflict of interest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conflict_of_interest "Conflict of interest") concerns. On September 30, 2016, Twitch announced Twitch Prime, a service which provides premium features that are exclusive to users who have an active [Amazon Prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Prime "Amazon Prime") subscription. This included advertising-free streaming, monthly offers of free add-on content ("Game Loot"), and game discounts. Games included with the game loot rewards were , , , , _[Mobile Legends: Bang Bang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_Legends:_Bang_Bang "Mobile Legends: Bang Bang")_ , , and more.
In December 2016, Twitch announced a semi-automated chat moderation tool (AutoMod), which uses [natural language processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_language_processing "Natural language processing") and [machine learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_learning "Machine learning") to set aside potentially unwanted content for human review. In February 2017, Twitch announced the Twitch Game Store, a digital distribution platform that would expose digital purchases of games within the site's browsing interface. When streaming games available on the store, partnered channels could display a [referral link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Referral_marketing "Referral marketing") to purchase the game—receiving a 5% commission. Users also received a "Twitch Crate" on every purchase, which included Bits and a collection of random chat emotes.
In August 2017, Twitch announced it had acquired video indexing platform ClipMine. In March 2018, Twitch Prime was expanded with free monthly game bundles, including games from its Indie Amplifier program. Indie Amplifier had users vote for a game to be given away by Prime.
On August 20, 2018, Twitch announced that it will no longer offer advertising-free access to the entire service to Amazon Prime subscribers, with this privilege requiring the separate "Twitch Turbo" subscription or an individual channel subscription. This privilege ended for new customers effective September 14, 2018, and for existing customers October 2018. In October 2018, Twitch announced Amazon Blacksmith, a new extension allowing broadcasters to configure displays of products associated with their streams with Amazon affiliate links. On November 27, 2018, Twitch discontinued the Game Store service, citing that it did not generate as much additional revenue for partners as it had hoped, and new revenue opportunities such as Amazon Blacksmith. Users retain access to their purchased games. On December 12, 2018, [Fandom, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fandom_Inc. "Fandom Inc.") had reached an agreement to acquire [Curse Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curse_LLC#Curse_Media "Curse LLC"), a spin-off of Curse, from Twitch Interactive for an undisclosed amount. Curse was dissolved and its assets were moved under Twitch Interactive.
Twitch's new headquarters at 350 Bush Street opened in August 2019. To comply with [historic preservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_preservation "Historic preservation") requirements, the developer kept the front facade of the [San Francisco Curb Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Curb_Exchange "San Francisco Curb Exchange"), but tore down everything behind the facade, and built a reconstruction of the old trading hall through which visitors must walk to reach the modern high-rise office tower behind it.
Twitch acquired the [Internet Games Database](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IGDB "IGDB") (IGDb), a user-driven website similar in functionality to [Internet Movie Database](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_Movie_Database "Internet Movie Database") (IMDb) to catalog details of video games in September 2019. Twitch plans to use the database service to improve its own internal search features and help users find games they are interested in. On September 26, 2019, Twitch unveiled a new logo and updated site design. The design is accompanied by a new advertising campaign, "You're already one of us", which will seek to promote the platform's community members. Twitch began signing exclusivity deals with high-profile streamers in December 2019.
Twitch introduced a Safety Advisory Council in May 2020, made up from streamers, academics, and [think tanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Think_tanks "Think tanks"), with a goal to develop guidelines for moderation, work-life balance, and safeguarding the interests of marginalized communities for the platform. The announcement attracted controversy, and CEO Emmett Shear later clarified that the role of the council was purely advisory. On June 22, 2020, Twitch Interactive sold [CurseForge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CurseForge "CurseForge") to [Overwolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overwolf "Overwolf") for an undisclosed sum. In August 2020, Twitch Prime was renamed Prime Gaming, aligning it closer with the Amazon Prime family of services. In 2020, Twitch sold Union For Gamers to Magic Find.
In May 2021, Twitch announced that it would introduce over 350 new tags to categorize streams, including finer tags for [gender identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_identity "Gender identity"), [sexual identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_identity "Sexual identity"), and [disabilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disability "Disability"), as well as tags for other types of themes (such as [virtual streamers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vtubers "Vtubers")). The disability and LGBT-oriented tags were developed in consultation with the video game accessibility charities [AbleGamers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AbleGamers "AbleGamers") and [SpecialEffect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpecialEffect "SpecialEffect"), and the LGBT organizations [GLAAD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GLAAD "GLAAD") and [The Trevor Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Trevor_Project "The Trevor Project").
On October 6, 2021, an anonymous hacker reportedly leaked "the entirety" of Twitch, including its source code of the Twitch client and APIs, and details of the payouts made to almost 2.4 million streamers since August 2019. The user posted a 128GB torrent link to [4chan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4chan "4chan") and said that the leak, which includes source code from almost 6,000 internal [Git](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Git "Git") [repositories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repository_\(version_control\) "Repository \(version control\)"), is also "part one" of a larger release. The leak also included details of plans for a digital storefront under the codename of "Vapor" meant to be a competitor to [Steam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_\(service\) "Steam \(service\)") along with details on payment received by streamers for their work on Twitch. Twitch verified they had suffered a data leak which they attributed to a server misconfiguration used by a "malicious third party". While Twitch found no indication of login credentials or credit card information to have been taken in the breach, the company reset all stream keys as a precaution.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-100)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-101)
On August 23, 2022, Twitch announced that it would no longer enforce its [exclusivity agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusivity_agreement "Exclusivity agreement"), allowing Twitch streamers to livestream on other streaming platforms.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-The_Verge-2022a-102)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-NBC_News-2022-103)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-104)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-Washington_Post-2-105) The announcement noted that simulcasting on Twitch and other "Twitch-like" streaming platforms was still prohibited; however, an exemption to the simulcasting restriction was applied to short-form streaming platforms such as [Instagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instagram "Instagram") and [TikTok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TikTok "TikTok").[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-The_Verge-2022a-102)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-NBC_News-2022-103) Despite the specific mention of restrictions on simulcasting, former Twitch employees noted that Twitch would likely not enforce the restriction, as doing so would be very difficult, and they had not been enforcing it for several months prior to the announcement.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-Washington_Post-2-105) After the announcement, many high-profile streamers who were limited by exclusivity, such as Ninja and Pokimane, started streaming on other platforms.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-106)
On September 21, 2022, Twitch announced it would be reducing the subscription revenue earned by large streamers.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-Metz-2022-107)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-108) Though most streamers get a 50% of revenue from subscriptions, some larger streamers have premium subscription terms, which give them 70% of subscription revenue. The new change, set to take effect on June 1, 2023, would mean premium streamers would keep 70% of the first $100,000 earned from subscriptions, after which their cut would be lowered to 50%.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-109)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-110) The announcement came after Twitch declined a popular request for all streamers to have 70% subscription revenue, which many noted is the same revenue already offered by YouTube.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-Eurogamer.net-2022-111) Twitch President [Dan Clancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_J._Clancy "Daniel J. Clancy") justified the change in a statement issued on Twitch's blog, stating it was done to cover Twitch's operating costs, noting the premium 70% split stopped being offered to new streamers over a year prior, and pointing to alternate streamer revenue sources that would not be affected by the subscription revenue reductions, such as Prime Subs or advertisement breaks.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-112) Though Clancy claimed 90% of streamers would not be affected by the revenue reduction, the change drew criticism from many streamers,[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-113) who viewed it as harmful to the security of streaming careers and more beneficial to Twitch and its advertisers than their users, with several streamers expressing doubt at Clancy's claims of Twitch's high operating costs, and noting that Twitch already has alternative revenue sources that make reducing streamer revenue unnecessary.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-Metz-2022-107)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-Eurogamer.net-2022-111)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-The_Verge-2022b-114) The announcement led to some streamers considering leaving Twitch or organizing boycotts.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-Metz-2022-107)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-The_Verge-2022b-114)
In December 2022, Amazon Senior Vice President Jeff Blackburn retired and was replaced by [Steve Boom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Boom "Steve Boom") as Vice President of Audio, Twitch, and Games.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-115)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-116) Twitch CEO Dan Clancy reports directly to Boom.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-117)
In March 2023, Clancy became CEO of Twitch, after previous CEO and Justin.tv co-founder Emmett Shear announced he would step down after 16 years at the company.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-118)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-119) Both Shear and Clancy have been described as "more product-focused than creator-focused".[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-120) On March 20, Clancy announced that Twitch would be laying off 400 employees, as part of Amazon-wide layoffs affecting 9,000 workers across the company.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-121)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-122)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-123)
On June 6, 2023, Twitch announced restrictions on third-party sponsor placements in streams, including restricting the size of sponsor logos, and prohibiting "burned-in" audio, video, or [display](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Display_advertising "Display advertising") advertising. The changes were met with criticism from major streamers such as [Asmongold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asmongold "Asmongold") (who threatened to leave the service), [Cr1TiKaL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cr1TiKaL "Cr1TiKaL"), and [Zentreya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VShojo "VShojo") due to their broad wording, concerns that it would impact streamers' existing relationships with advertisers, and their impact on charity and esports events that rely extensively on sponsorship. The service quickly retracted the new branded content policy and announced that it would be clarified, stating that it was intended to "clarify our existing ads policy that was intended to prohibit third party ad networks from selling burned in video and display ads on Twitch, which is consistent with other services", and that Twitch "[does] not intend to limit streamers' ability to enter into direct relationships with sponsors."[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-124)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-125)
In August 2023, Twitch began to trial a "Discovery Feed" feature in its mobile apps, populated by "featured" clips from followed users.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-126) In October 2023, Twitch began to implement [stories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Story_\(social_media\) "Story \(social media\)").[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-127) At TwitchCon 2023, Twitch announced upcoming updates to its Guest Star feature (concurrently renamed "Stream Together") to allow for merged chat rooms, and that streamers under an affiliation or partnership agreement with the service (unless contractually required) would be allowed to simulcast their streams on competing platforms such as YouTube, as opposed to only mobile-centric video platforms.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-128)
On December 6, 2023, Twitch announced that it would exit the South Korea market effective February 27, 2024, citing the prohibitive costs of offering the service in the country.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-129) Due to demands from internet service providers that Twitch pay network access fees, Twitch restricted streams to [720p](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/720p "720p") quality in September 2022, and blocked access to video-on-demand (VOD) content (including archived broadcasts and clips) in February 2023.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-130)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-131) Users in South Korea will no longer be allowed to monetize their streams, and will be offboarded from the affiliate and partnership programs.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-132)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-133) In February 2024, Twitch was additionally fined [₩](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korean_won "South Korean won")435 million ($327,067) by the Korea Telecommunications Commission, deeming Twitch's degradation of service in the country to be unjustified and undermining the interests of users.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-134)
In January 2024, Twitch announced another [mass layoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%932024_video_game_industry_layoffs "2023–2024 video game industry layoffs"), affecting 500 employees or 35% of total staff, after previous layoffs in early 2023.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-135)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-136) The announcement came amid ongoing struggles and ensuing layoffs across the [tech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tech_industry "Tech industry") and [digital media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_media_companies "Digital media companies") sectors.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-137)
In October 2024, Twitch's longtime head of music Cindy Charles died.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-138)
## Content
Twitch is designed to be a platform for content, including [esports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esports "Esports") tournaments, personal streams of individual players, and gaming-related talk shows.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-139) A number of channels do live [speedrunning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speedrunning "Speedrunning").[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-140) The Twitch homepage currently displays games based on viewership. As of June2018,[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Twitch_\(service\)&action=edit), , , , _[PlayerUnknown's Battlegrounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayerUnknown's_Battlegrounds "PlayerUnknown's Battlegrounds")_ , , and _[Counter-Strike: Global Offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-Strike:_Global_Offensive "Counter-Strike: Global Offensive")_ with a combined total of over 356 million hours watched.[]
Twitch has also made expansions into non-gaming content; such as in July 2013, the site streamed a performance of 'Fester's Feast' from [San Diego Comic-Con](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Comic-Con "San Diego Comic-Con"), and on July 30, 2014, [electronic dance music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_dance_music "Electronic dance music") act [Steve Aoki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Aoki "Steve Aoki") broadcast a live performance from a nightclub in [Ibiza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibiza "Ibiza").[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-141)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-142)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-143)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-144) In January 2015, Twitch introduced an official category for music streams, such as radio shows and music production activities, and in March 2015, announced that it would become the new official live-streaming partner of the [Ultra Music Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra_Music_Festival "Ultra Music Festival"), an [electronic music festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_music_festival "Electronic music festival") in [Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami "Miami").[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-PC_World-2015-145)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-146)
On October 28, 2015, Twitch launched a second non-gaming category, "Creative", which is intended for streams showcasing the creation of artistic and creative works. To promote the launch, the service also streamed an eight-day [marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathon_\(television\) "Marathon \(television\)") of [Bob Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Ross "Bob Ross") .[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-147) In July 2016, Twitch launched "Social eating" as a beta; it was inspired by the Korean phenomenon of [mukbang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukbang "Mukbang") and Korean players having engaged in the practice as intermissions on their gaming streams.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-148)
In March 2017, Twitch added an "IRL" category, which is designed for content within Twitch guidelines that does not fall within any of the other established categories on the site (such as [lifelogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lifelog "Lifelog")).[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-149)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-150) reported that "while gameplay still makes up the vast majority of the content broadcast via Twitch, the 'Just Chatting' category—a catch-all term that encompasses anything from candid conversation to reality programming—took the top spot by a comfortable margin overall in December [2019]. While the category has been on the rise for the last couple of months, this was the first time that it's actually achieved No. 1 overall for a tracked period on the platform".[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-GeekWire-2020-151)
In 2020, described Twitch as "talk radio for the [extremely online](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extremely_online "Extremely online")".[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-152) Michael Espinosa, for in 2021, highlighted that "Twitch dominates the live content space, with 17 billion hours watched last year (per StreamElements), compared to YouTube Gaming Live's 10 billion (per the company). But the vast majority of gaming content is still consumed on-demand, where YouTube is the clear leader with over 100 billion hours watched last year".[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-Espinosa-2021-153)
### As a teaching tool
Twitch is often used for video game tutorials; the nature of Twitch allows mass numbers of learners to interact with each other and the instructor in real time.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-Payne-2017-154) Twitch is also used for software development learning,[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-Payne-2017-154) with communities of users streaming programming projects and talking through their work.

### Users
It was reported in the early 2010s that the typical Twitch viewer is male and aged between 18 and 34 years of age, although the site has also made attempts at pursuing other demographics, including women. By 2015, Twitch had more than 100 million viewers per month. In 2017, Twitch remained the leading live-streaming video service for video games in the US.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-225)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-226) _GeekWire_ reported that "while Twitch's overall share of the streaming market has been steadily diminishing over the course of the year, from 67.1 percent in December 2018 to 61 percent at the end of the 2019, the steady growth of the overall market means that the overall amount of content watched on the service has done nothing but increase".[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-GeekWire-2020-151) The [journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_journal "Academic journal") article _World of Streaming. Motivation and Gratification on Twitch_ reported the results of a Twitch user survey in 2017.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-Gros-2017-227) In ranking user motivations on the use of Twitch, users were motivated (in descending order) to watch Twitch: "to be entertained", "to follow gaming events", and to "have an alternative for television". Motivations classified as "socialization" and "information" ranked lower than motivations classified as "entertainment".[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-Gros-2017-227)
As of February2020,[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Twitch_\(service\)&action=edit)[concurrent users](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concurrent_user "Concurrent user"). [Statista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statista "Statista"), a company specializing in market and consumer data, reported that "as of May 2020, users in their teens and twenties accounted for more than three-quarters of Twitch's active app user accounts in the United States. According to recent data users aged 20 to 29 years, accounted for 40.6 percent of the video streaming app's user base on the [Android](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Android_\(operating_system\) "Android \(operating system\)") platform".[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-228) They also reported that the "distribution of Twitch users in the United States as of 2nd quarter 2021" was 75% male and 25% female.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-229)
As of 2022, the countries with the most Twitch users were the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States") (93 million), [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil "Brazil") (16.9 million), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany "Germany") (16.8 million), [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France "France") (15.4 million), the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom "United Kingdom") (13.4 million), [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia "Russia") (10.5 million), [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain "Spain") (10.5 million), [Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina "Argentina") (10 million), [Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico "Mexico") (9.2 million), and [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy "Italy") (8.3 million users).[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-Twitch_Users_By_Country_2023-2023-230) The United States accounts for roughly 36% of all Twitch users.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-Twitch_Users_By_Country_2023-2023-230)
Twitch allows anyone to watch a live broadcast and does not require viewers to log in.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-231) Users also have the option to follow and subscribe (also known as subbing) to streamers. Following is a free option, similar to other platforms such as Instagram and Twitter, where the user will see their followed streamers on the front page of Twitch when signed in and can receive notifications of specific broadcasts.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-Lifewire-2020-232)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-Morris-233) Subscribing is a way for users to financially support streamers in exchange for exclusive benefits determined by the individual streamer.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-Lifewire-2020-232)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-Morris-233)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-234) Users who link their Twitch account to their Amazon Prime account gain access to [Prime Gaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Gaming "Prime Gaming") which includes one complimentary Twitch subscription per month that the user can assign to the streamer of their choice.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-235) The aforementioned 2017 academic survey stated that 31.5% of users "spent money on Twitch"; of those users, 22.6% "donated to a streamer", 31.6% subscribed to a streamer and 45.8% "did both". The majority of these users stated the "main motivation is to support a streamer financially".[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-Gros-2017-227) Twitch's Terms of Service does not allow people under 13 years of age to use its services. Additionally, people who are at least 13 years old but below the [age of majority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_majority "Age of majority") in their jurisdiction (18 in most jurisdictions), may only use the services under the supervision or permission of a parent or other legal guardian who agrees to abide to the Terms of Service.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-236)
### Partner and affiliate programs
In July 2011, Twitch launched its Partner Program,[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-237) which reached over 11,000 members by August 2015.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-238)
Similar to the Partner Program of other video sites like YouTube, the Partner Program allows popular content producers to share in the advertisement revenue generated from their streams. Additionally, Twitch users can subscribe to partnered streamers' channels for US$4.99 a month, often granting the user access to unique [emoticons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emoticons "Emoticons"), live chat privileges, and other various perks. Twitch retains US$2.49 of every US$4.99 channel subscription, with the remaining US$2.50 going directly to the partnered streamer.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-239) Although exceptions were made, Twitch previously required that prospective partners have an "average concurrent viewership of 500+", as well as a consistent streaming schedule of at least three days a week.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-240) However, since the launch of the 'Achievements' feature, there is a clearer "Path to Partnership" with trackable goals for concurrent viewership, duration and frequency of streams.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-241)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-242)
In April 2017, Twitch launched its "Affiliate Program" that allows smaller channels to generate revenue as well, also announcing that it would allow channels access to multi-priced subscription tiers.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-243)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-244) The participants of this program get some but not all of the benefits of the Twitch Partners. Streamers can make profit from cheering with Bits which are purchasable from Twitch directly.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-245) Affiliates are also able to access the Twitch Subscriptions feature, with all the same functionality that Partners have access to, with a maximum of five subscriber emotes.[] In September 2019, the service announced that Affiliates would now receive a share of ad revenue.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-246)
Additionally, in June 2023, Twitch introduced the Partner Plus Program. This program was designed to recognize Twitch partners who consistently bring a large audience and engagement to the platform. Streamers in this program receive a 70/30 revenue share on subscription revenue. To qualify for the program, creators had to maintain a sub count of at least 350 subscribers for three consecutive months.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-247) Once that is complete, qualifying members will be enrolled for the next 12 months. The program was officially launched on October 1, 2023.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-248) This enabled partners to earn more as they continue to grow their community. However, a number of streamers were not happy with the program. Streamers argued that it excluded certain creators because of the criteria and that creators would burn themselves out by trying to achieve 350 monthly subscribers.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-249)
In January 2024, Twitch made some changes to the program. The platform announced that it would be introducing a new tier to its revenue share program that would grant a 60/40 revenue split and has lower requirements.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-250) In addition, the program would also become open to affiliates, expanding access to smaller creators. The program is now known as the "Plus Program." When the program was first launched the year prior, Twitch would only pay out the 70/30 revenue split until streamers made $100,000. Along with expanding the Partner Plus Program and adding a new revenue level, Twitch also announced that it would be eliminating the $100,000 cap for the 70/30 revenue share for all streamers.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-251) This was part of Twitch's strategy to provide more earning opportunities for streamers. The program uses a points system to determine which revenue split a streamer qualifies for. Each monthly subscription counts towards the points total. However, some subscriptions have higher point values. One tier 1 subscription is one point, one tier 2 subscription is two points, and one tier 3 subscription equals six points.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-252) To qualify for the 60/40 revenue split, streamers must maintain 100 Plus Points for three consecutive months. For the 70/30 revenue split, streamers must maintain 300 Plus Points.
Advertising on the site has been handled by a number of partners. In 2011, Twitch had an exclusive deal with [Future US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_US "Future US").[] On April 17, 2012, Twitch announced a deal to give [CBS Interactive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Interactive "CBS Interactive") the rights to exclusively sell advertising, promotions and sponsorships for the community.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-253) On June 5, 2013, Twitch announced the formation of the Twitch Media Group, a new in-house advertisement sales team which has taken over CBS Interactive's role of selling advertisements.
For users who do not have ad-free access to a channel or Twitch Turbo, pre-roll advertising, and mid-roll commercial breaks that are manually triggered by the streamer, are displayed on streams. In September 2020, Twitch announced that it would test automated mid-roll advertising on streams, which cannot be controlled by the streamer.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-254)
## Content moderation and restrictions
### Copyrighted content
On August 6, 2014, Twitch announced that all on-demand videos on Twitch became subject to [acoustic fingerprinting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acoustic_fingerprint "Acoustic fingerprint") using software provided by content protection company [Audible Magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audible_Magic "Audible Magic"); if copyrighted music (particularly, songs played by users from outside of the game they are playing) is detected, the 30-minute portion of the video which contains the music will be muted. Live broadcasts were not subject to these filters.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-255)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-256) A system was available for those who believed they were inappropriately affected and had rights to the music they used to challenge the filtering.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-257) Twitch offered a selection of [royalty-free music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Production_music#Royalty-free_libraries "Production music") for streamers to use, which was expanded upon later in January 2015.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-PC_World-2015-145) The audio filtering system, along with the lack of communication surrounding the changes in general, proved to be controversial among users. In a [Reddit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reddit "Reddit") AMA, co-founder Emmett Shear admitted that his staff had "screwed up" and should have provided advance warning of the changes, and promised that Twitch had "absolutely no intention" of implementing audio filtering on live broadcasts.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-258)
In June 2020, Twitch received a large wave of [DMCA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DMCA "DMCA") takedown notices aimed at year-old VODs and "clips" (short segments of streams that can be captured by users) that contain copyrighted music from 2017 to 2019. Twitch complied with the takedowns and also issued a number of copyright strikes against viewers. Concerned streamers were notified that they should remove all VODs and clips if not certain they did not contain copyrighted material. This provoked major backlash, both at the loss of prior content but also based on viability concerns due to an inability to review or even rapidly delete content. There were also complaints based that strikes were being issued on viewer-created clips, even where the streamer-created content was deleted.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-259)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-260)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-261)
On September 15, 2020, Twitch signed a licensing agreement with the French performance society [SACEM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SACEM "SACEM"), allowing composers and publishers to collect royalties whenever their music is streamed in France. Twitch already had licensing deals with the American societies [ASCAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASCAP "ASCAP"), [BMI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcast_Music,_Inc. "Broadcast Music, Inc."), [SESAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SESAC "SESAC") and [Global Music Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Music_Rights?action=edit&redlink=1 "Global Music Rights \(page does not exist\)").[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-262)
To address these issues and also build upon the growth of music-based content on Twitch, Twitch introduced an extension known as "Soundtrack" in September 2020, which plays rights-cleared music with curated genre-based playlists. It is contained within a separate audio track that is not recorded with VODs,[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-263) and had agreements with 24 music distributors and independent record labels at launch.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-264)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-265)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-266) A group of US [performance rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Performance_rights_organisation "Performance rights organisation") and music associations accused Twitch of designing Soundtrack in such a way as to avoid payment of [mechanical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_license "Mechanical license") and [synchronization licenses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synchronization_license "Synchronization license")—claims which Twitch has defended.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-267)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-268)
In September 2021, Twitch and the [National Music Publishers' Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Music_Publishers'_Association "National Music Publishers' Association") signed a creative partnership.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-269)
In September 2024, Twitch introduced a new program for [DJs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disk_jockey "Disk jockey") on the platform, allowing them access to a new stream category for [DJ mixes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DJ_mix "DJ mix") where music from participating labels may be streamed without the risk of a DMCA takedown, with a share of revenue being used to pay royalties (for affiliates and partners, this is deducted from the channel's existing split). This program has limitations: features such as VODs and clips are disabled on any channel enrolled in the DJ program, even if streaming non-DJ content (with Twitch officially recommending the use of a separate account for non-DJ streams), and DJs are restricted to playing music from certain labels and publishers that have reached agreements with the service (potentially restricting the ability to play unofficial remixes).[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-270)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-271)
### Mature content
Twitch users are not allowed to stream any game that is rated "Adults Only" (AO) in the United States by the [Entertainment Software Rating Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_Software_Rating_Board "Entertainment Software Rating Board") (ESRB), regardless of its rating in any other geographical region, and any game that contains "overtly sexual content" or "gratuitous violence", or content which violates the terms of use of third-party services.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-272)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-Polygon-2015-273)
Twitch has also explicitly banned specific games from streaming, regardless of rating; this includes games such as , [eroge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eroge "Eroge") [visual novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_novel "Visual novel") games (such as ), , , , and .[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-Polygon-2015-273)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-274)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-Polygon-2016-275)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-276) The banning of _Yandere Simulator_ was criticized by [YandereDev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YandereDev "YandereDev"), the developer of the game. He believed that the game was being arbitrarily singled out with no explanation, as Twitch has not banned other games with sexual or violent content such as , , or .[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-Polygon-2016-275)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-277)
Twitch took temporary action in May 2019 after channels related to the video game began to be used for inappropriate content. _Artifact_ , a major game by [Valve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valve_Corporation "Valve Corporation"), had lost most of its audience in just months from its release, and by late May 2019, several popular [livestreamers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livestream "Livestream") commented that the total viewership for _Artifact_ streams had dropped to near zero. In the days that followed, several streamers started to make streams purporting to be _Artifact_ gameplay but was [trolling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolling "Trolling") or other off-topic content. Initially these new streams were playing with the viewers or were jokes, such as showing animal videos or matches. After a few days, other _Artifact_ channel streams appeared containing content that was against the terms of Twitch's use policy, including full copyrighted movies, pornography, Nazi propaganda, and at least one stream that showed the entirety of the shooter's video from the [Christchurch mosque shootings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christchurch_mosque_shootings "Christchurch mosque shootings"). The titles of such streams were usually presented to imply they were showing other content while waiting in queue for _Artifact_ matches as to appear legitimate. As word of these streams in the _Artifact_ section grew, Twitch took action, deleting the account that streamed the Christchurch shooting.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-278) Twitch then took steps to temporarily ban new accounts from streaming until it can resolve the issue.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-279) By June 2019, Twitch started taking legal actions against one hundred "John Doe" streamers in a California court, accusing them of trademark infringement, breach of contract, fraud, and unlawful use of the service that was harming and scaring away users of the service.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-280)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-281)
In early 2021, some streams began to use their Twitch channel to broadcast themselves from [hot tubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_tub "Hot tub") while wearing swimsuits. Twitch considered these streams to be "not advertiser friendly", banning some of the more predominant channels that had taken this route. In May 2021, Twitch clarified in a "Pools, Hot Tubs, and Beaches" post that it was not trying to discriminate against women or others through this action, but through content that it deemed to be "sexually suggestive".[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-282) In June that year, Twitch also took similar action against users that performed [yoga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoga "Yoga") while at the same time made [autonomous sensory meridian response](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_sensory_meridian_response "Autonomous sensory meridian response") (ASMR) sounds via their microphones, which Twitch also stated was approaching sexual content.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-283)
In December 2023, the platform introduced a new policy that relaxed prohibitions on certain adult content, given that said content would be appropriately labeled. This came amidst the popularity of supposedly "topless" videos on the platform. Popularized by streamer [Morgpie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgpie "Morgpie"), the genre saw creators push the previous boundaries of Twitch's guidelines by implying but not explicitly showing nudity.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-284) Twitch's new policy also allowed depictions of "fictionalized nudity". However, Twitch rescinded its policy very shortly after, following backlash from users and streamers.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-285) Twitch CEO Dan Clancy acknowledged that the new policy was a step too far, and that distinguishing between digital art and photography was challenging. As a result, Twitch will no longer permit depictions of real or fictional nudity, regardless of the medium. The company also removed content that violated the updated policy and issued channel enforcements. While some changes to Twitch's Sexual Content Policy remain in effect, such as allowing content that highlights certain body parts and specific dances without a label, games featuring nudity or sexual violence as a core focus are still prohibited.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-286)
### Hate speech and harassment
In February 2018, Twitch updated its acceptable content policies to direct that any content that it deemed hateful be suspended from its platform.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-287)
In June 2020, a number of women stepped forward with accusations towards several streamers on Twitch and other services related to sexual misconduct and harassment claims.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-288) Twitch stated it would review all reported incidents and comply with law enforcement in any investigative efforts. However, several popular streamers on Twitch's service believed that the platform could do more to evaluate the accused individuals, prevent incidents, and protect others in the future, and used June 24, 2020, as a Twitch blackout day, not streaming any content through Twitch to show their support.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-289) By the evening of June 24, 2020, Twitch had placed several bans on the accounts of those accused after completing its investigation, and stated in a blog post it would be forwarding additional details to law enforcement.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-290)
Twitch temporarily suspended an account belonging to then [US President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_United_States "President of the United States") [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump")'s campaign on June 29, 2020. Twitch stated that "hateful conduct is not allowed" as the reason for the suspension.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-291)
Twitch announced a new policy towards harassment and hateful content in December 2020 that would take effect on January 22, 2021, aimed to better protect marginalized users of the service. While the new policy is more strict, Twitch said that this also includes a larger sliding scale of remedies or punishments to better deal with edge cases, such as temporarily blocking one's channel for a short time rather than a full ban. The new rules include a ban on imagery containing the [Confederate Flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flags_of_the_Confederate_States_of_America "Flags of the Confederate States of America"), and 'racist emotes', though the list of such emotes has not been clarified yet.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-292) The new policy included banning words that were considered sexual insults, such as "[incel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incel "Incel")" and "[virgin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin "Virgin")" when used for harassment. The banned words included "[simp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simp "Simp")", which raised criticism by streamers and long-time viewers. While its slang origins have defined "simp" derogatorily as "a man who invests a lot of time and energy into women who don't want him", the term had become common on Twitch as an insult related to men being nice to women on the service or simply to refer to a person with loyalty to another. Twitch, in response, clarified that penalties for using these terms would only be enforced if they were being used in harassment of other users.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-293)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-294)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-295)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-296)
On December 4, 2020, Twitch removed the "blind playthrough" tag due to concerns of [ableism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ableism "Ableism") that it may be offensive to those who are [visually impaired](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visually_impaired "Visually impaired"). Suggestions for non-offensive and more neutral labels include "first playthrough", "undiscovered," and "no spoilers."[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-297)
A popular feature of Twitch is the ability to "raid" another channel, where multiple users, coordinated from a different Twitch channel or another social media service, all join a target channel to provide support and encouragement. This is typically used to help boost the popularity of the target channel, particularly if the raid is organized by a popular streamer. Twitch had officially supported this type of activity since 2017 with the ability for a streamer to send all of their viewers to another channel as a raid.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-298) However, around mid-2021, new types of "hate raids" began to occur with increasing frequency on Twitch. In these cases, numerous users would flood a channel and its chat with messages of harassment and hate towards the streamer as a form of [cyberbullying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyberbullying "Cyberbullying"). Most of these users are typically from [automated bots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automated_bot "Automated bot"), which made it difficult for channel moderators to deal with the amount of messages.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-299) Despite warning Twitch about these hate raids, Twitch had shown little action towards stopping them, leading numerous streams to organize a "#ADayOffTwitch" on September 1, 2021, as a form of protest in anticipation that Twitch will find ways to take action against them.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-300)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-301) After acknowledging a problem with dealing with hate raids, Twitch launched a lawsuit in early September 2021 against two individuals it had determined to be responsible for managing several hate raids after permanently banning their accounts.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-302) At the end of September 2021, Twitch introduced tools for streamers to be able to limit who may participate in their chat as to prevent hate raids. These tools allow streamers to limit chat to those that have verified their phone number or email with Twitch, as well as to those that have followed their channel for a minimum amount of time.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-303)
In May 2022, following the [mass shooting in Buffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Buffalo_shooting "2022 Buffalo shooting"), the New York state attorney general's office announced an investigation into multiple online platforms, including Twitch, to determine their part in platforming or promoting hateful content. The investigation will also focus on the platforms' moderation efforts. A spokesperson for Twitch stated that the service would comply with the investigation.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-304)
In November 2024, Twitch banned the use of ["Zionist" as a slur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionist_as_a_pejorative "Zionist as a pejorative"). Twitch specified that the rule was conditional, saying, "You're allowed to discuss the political movement of that name, but not attack or demean another individual or group of people on the basis of their background or religious belief."[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-305)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-Andy_Chalk-2024-306) The [Anti-Defamation League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Defamation_League "Anti-Defamation League"), [non-governmental organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-governmental_organization "Non-governmental organization") that was founded to combat [antisemitism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitism "Antisemitism"), took credit for the change.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-307)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-Andy_Chalk-2024-306) The move was condemned by pro-Palestinian activists, such as [Omar Zahzah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_Zahzah?action=edit&redlink=1 "Omar Zahzah \(page does not exist\)"), in the , said it would ultimately lead to the ban of Palestine from the platform.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-308)[]
### Extremism
In April 2021, it was reported that Twitch was providing a financial lifeline to extremists such as [QAnon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QAnon "QAnon") adherents and far-right influencers.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-309) Another report in August and September 2021, by the Institute of Strategic Dialogue also identified Twitch as a platform where far right extremists run rampant. Clips from far-right extremists, such as 'Omegle Redpilling' become quite popular before they are removed from the platform and in some cases, are not removed at all according to [ISD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Strategic_Dialogue "Institute for Strategic Dialogue").[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-310)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-311)
In 2024, it was reported that new Twitch account sign‑ups from Israeli IP addresses had been blocked for an extended period. In public posts that month, Twitch claimed it had temporarily disabled email‑verified sign‑ups in [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel "Israel") after the [October 7, 2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_7_attacks "October 7 attacks") attacks to prevent graphic content, and further stated the block was inadvertently left in place, calling this an "unacceptable miss." Twitch also asserted phone verification remained available, a point disputed by many users. The restriction was lifted in October 2024.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-312)
### Gambling
On September 20, 2022, Twitch announced that beginning October 18, it would prohibit the streaming of slots, roulette and dice games on [gambling websites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_gambling "Online gambling") not licensed in the US or "other jurisdictions that provide sufficient consumer protection". The policy change does not affect [sports betting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_betting "Sports betting"), [fantasy sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fantasy_sport "Fantasy sport"), and [poker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poker "Poker").[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-BBC_News-2022-313)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-Washington_Post-314)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-315) Gambling has been extremely popular on Twitch for years, being one of the most popular types of content on the platform, with many streamers being sponsored by online gambling services; however, it has also been controversial, with prominent streamers such as [Mizkif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizkif "Mizkif") and Pokimane speaking out against the negative effects of gambling streams.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-BBC_News-2022-313)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-Washington_Post-314) The announcement came shortly after a popular streamer admitted he spent $200,000 in donations on _CS:GO_ [skin gambling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skin_gambling "Skin gambling").[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-316)

### Twitch Desktop App and CurseForge
After acquiring [Curse LLC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curse_LLC "Curse LLC"), Twitch Interactive rebranded the Curse app as the Twitch Desktop App in March 2017. It kept features for [mod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_modding "Video game modding") installation and management for supported games via [CurseForge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CurseForge "CurseForge"); kept Curse Voice features such as screen sharing, text chat, voice chat, video chat and community server creation; added a dedicated browser for the Twitch website; added Twitch's friends system; and added activity sharing. This update also redesigned the application.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-349)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-350) The software also served as the client for the former Twitch Game Store.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-351)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-352) The Curse mobile app was subsequently rebranded as Twitch Messenger[] which was later shut down.[] Twitch removed the app's servers, group messaging, voice chat and video chat functionality in February 2019.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-353)
On June 22, 2020, Twitch Interactive sold CurseForge to [Overwolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overwolf "Overwolf") for an undisclosed sum. On December 2, 2020, mod management functionality was removed from the Twitch Desktop App.[] The mod management functionality previously found in the Twitch app can since be found in Overwolf's CurseForge app.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-354)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-355)
On March 30, 2022, Twitch announced that it would officially end support for the Twitch Desktop App on April 30, 2022, opting users of the desktop app to use a web browser to interact with Twitch on desktop platforms.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-356)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-357)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-358)
### Creator Dashboard
The Creator Dashboard on Twitch is a tool that helps streamers manage and optimize their channels. In 2019, the platform announced a new set of features to make streaming more accessible and interactive. This new Creator Dashboard introduced features such as Stream Manager, Quick Actions, Creator Updates, and Assistant.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-359) These features were introduced so that creators could set subscriber goals, analyze engagement trends, and simplify streaming tasks. The Stream Manager allows streamers to view various aspects of their livestream such as their live chat, recent followers and subscribers, and a playback of the stream to see what the viewers are seeing.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-360) The Quick Actions panel on the Creator Dashboard allows streamers to perform actions such as running ads, enabling followers-only or emote-only chat, and creating clips. The Creator Updates section is a dedicated space for streamers to learn about important product updates and feature changes. The Assistant section provides creators with resources to help them grow their channel and become an affiliate or partner.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-361)
## TwitchCon
Main article: [TwitchCon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TwitchCon "TwitchCon")
TwitchCon 2016
TwitchCon is a biannual [fan convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fan_convention "Fan convention") devoted to Twitch and the culture of video game streaming.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-362) The inaugural event was held at the [Moscone Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscone_Center "Moscone Center") in [San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco "San Francisco") from September 25 to 26, 2015. Since its inception TwitchCon has been an annual event. The second TwitchCon was held in [San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego "San Diego") at the [San Diego Convention Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Convention_Center "San Diego Convention Center") from September 30 to October 2, 2016.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-363) The third annual TwitchCon was held in [Long Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Beach "Long Beach") at the [Long Beach Convention and Entertainment Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Beach_Convention_and_Entertainment_Center "Long Beach Convention and Entertainment Center") from October 20 to 22, 2017.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-364) The fourth annual TwitchCon was held at the [San Jose McEnery Convention Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose_Convention_Center "San Jose Convention Center") in [San Jose, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose,_California "San Jose, California"), from October 26 to 28, 2018.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-365) In 2019, TwitchCon expanded overseas and hosted its first ever European event in Berlin in April 2019,[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-366) alongside a North American event later in November 2019 in San Diego.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-367) TwitchCon had planned to host an event in Amsterdam in May 2020, but this was cancelled due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic").[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-368) Another TwitchCon event was planned in San Diego in September 2020,[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-369) but was also cancelled due to COVID-19.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)#cite_note-370)
