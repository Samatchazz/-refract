# Influencer Marketing Across Southeast Asia: Country-by-Country Guide
[Southeast Asia’s influencer marketing market](https://creativeformore.com/influencer-marketing/) is one of the most commercially productive in the world and one of the most misunderstood by brands entering from outside the region. The numbers that describe it are striking: brand engagements with influencers and creators contributed approximately USD 11 billion in direct e-commerce sales value in Southeast Asia in 2023 alone, according to research by [Impact.com’s Southeast Asia influencer e-commerce study](https://go.impact.com/rs/280-XQP-994/images/RRT-INFL-ED-eCommerce-Influencer-Marketing-SEA.pdf). More than 90% of Southeast Asian online shoppers actively follow influencers, and more than 80% have previously purchased a product because an influencer recommended it.
Those figures describe the opportunity. What they do not describe is the operational reality that brands discover when they try to run a single influencer strategy across multiple Southeast Asian markets simultaneously. Rates differ by a factor of ten between Singapore and Vietnam. Disclosure rules range from legally mandated in Singapore’s financial sector to entirely absent in Indonesia. The platform that drives commerce in the Philippines produces minimal results in Malaysia. The creator who resonates with Thailand’s Gen Z audience has no relevance in Vietnam’s relationship-driven content ecosystem.
This guide covers each of the six main Southeast Asian markets in the detail that actually informs campaign planning: platform preferences, tier economics, typical campaign costs in local market context, and the disclosure and compliance landscape that determines what brands can and cannot do in each country.
## Why Does Southeast Asia Require a Country-by-Country Approach?
The instinct to run a regional influencer campaign, briefing creators across Singapore, Indonesia, Thailand, and Vietnam simultaneously with the same creative direction and the same deliverables framework, is understandable from a resource efficiency perspective. It consistently underperforms against market-specific programs because the factors that determine influencer campaign success are almost entirely local.
Creator trust in Southeast Asia is hyperlocal. According to [Impact.com’s SEA research](https://go.impact.com/rs/280-XQP-994/images/RRT-INFL-ED-eCommerce-Influencer-Marketing-SEA.pdf), more than 80% of the top influencers in each Southeast Asian market are local rather than regional creators, and authenticity ranks as the number one factor that consumers cite for following and acting on an influencer’s recommendation. A creator who has built credibility with Indonesian consumers by speaking Bahasa Indonesia, referencing local cultural touchpoints, and reflecting the aspirations of her specific community has no transferable authority in Thai or Vietnamese markets.
The commercial infrastructure also differs by market. TikTok Shop’s commerce integration in Indonesia, through the Tokopedia merger, creates a platform environment that is structurally different from TikTok’s role in Singapore or Malaysia. Shopee Live drives meaningful conversion in Vietnam and Indonesia in ways that have limited direct equivalent in Singapore, where the audience expects a more polished brand store environment. A campaign designed for one market’s commercial infrastructure will not work as written in another.
According to global research cited by [Influencer Marketing Hub’s regulatory analysis](https://influencermarketinghub.com/influencer-marketing-regulations/), the global influencer marketing industry reached USD 21.1 billion in 2024. Southeast Asia is one of the fastest-growing regional contributors to that figure. The brands capturing the most value from that growth are those operating with market-specific programs, not regional templates.

## Singapore: Premium Rates, Professional Ecosystem, Tightening Regulation
### Platform preferences
Instagram is Singapore’s primary influencer commerce platform for beauty, lifestyle, and F&B. TikTok drives discovery for brands targeting consumers under 35. Xiaohongshu is the critical platform for brands targeting Singapore’s Chinese-speaking community, with approximately 580,000 active users growing at 67% year-on-year, according to [Hashmeta’s Singapore social media statistics](https://hashmeta.com/blog/social-media-statistics-singapore-complete-data-marketing-insights/). YouTube serves long-form review and comparison content. LinkedIn is the influencer channel for B2B brands, where thought leadership content from senior professionals builds the trust that drives enterprise pipeline.
### Tier economics and typical campaign costs
Singapore’s influencer market is the most professionalised in Southeast Asia. Most established creators have formal rate cards with fees specified by content format, platform, and usage rights. According to [Marketing Agency Singapore’s 2026 influencer rate card](https://marketingagency.sg/influencer-pricing-rate-card-singapore/) and [Hamilton Sherwind’s Singapore advertising benchmarks](https://hamiltonsherwind.com/2025-singapore-social-media-advertising-playbook-benchmarks-budgets-creative-best-practices/), typical per-post rates in 2025 and 2026 are as follows:  
| Tier  | Follower Range  | Instagram Post (SGD)  | TikTok Video (SGD)  | Avg. Engagement Rate  |  
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |  
| **Nano**  | 1K to 10K  | 300 to 600  | 200 to 500  | 3 to 7%  |  
| **Micro**  | 10K to 100K  | 600 to 3,000  | 500 to 2,500  | 2 to 5%  |  
| **Mid-tier**  | 100K to 500K  | 3,000 to 9,000  | 2,500 to 7,000  | 1 to 3%  |  
| **Macro**  | 500K+  | 9,000 to 25,000+  | 7,000 to 20,000+  | 0.5 to 1.5%  |  
Product gifting alone is generally not accepted by Singapore creators at micro tier and above. Most expect a fee alongside any gifted product. Xiaohongshu creators typically charge at the lower end of the Instagram range but are valued disproportionately for their category influence within Singapore’s Chinese-speaking community.
### Disclosure Rules
[Singapore’s Advertising Standards Authority of Singapore (ASAS)](https://asas.org.sg/) requires that all paid commercial content be clearly identified as advertising. Creators must use labels including “Ad,” “Sponsored,” or the platform’s native branded content tools. The requirement applies to gifted product content as well as paid partnerships.
In the financial services category, Singapore’s Monetary Authority of Singapore issued new Guidelines on Standards of Conduct for Digital Advertising Activities in September 2025, taking effect March 2026, according to [Lexology’s MAS guidelines analysis](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=72ac1455-9bec-4ac5-bf45-eb7f4cf9b7eb). These rules require financial institutions to ensure all digital marketing partners, including influencers and affiliate marketers, comply with MAS advertising requirements and consumer protection standards. Brands in financial services, investment products, or any regulated financial category must have explicit compliance frameworks for influencer partnerships in Singapore from March 2026 onwards.
## Indonesia: Massive Reach, Affordable Rates, No Disclosure Framework
### Platform Preferences
### Tier Economics and Typical Campaign Costs
Indonesia is the most affordable major influencer market in Southeast Asia, with rates substantially lower than Singapore across all tiers. According to [TikTok rate benchmarking data](https://www.showca.se/post/tiktok-influencer-rates), Indonesian TikTok CPMs run at USD 0.005 to 0.01 per view compared to USD 0.03 to 0.05 in Western markets, reflecting the overall cost differential. Typical per-post rates in USD for Indonesia-based creators:  
| Tier  | Follower Range  | Instagram Post (USD)  | TikTok Video (USD)  | Shopee Live Session (USD)  |  
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |  
| **Nano**  | 1K to 10K  | 20 to 80  | 15 to 60  | 30 to 100  |  
| **Micro**  | 10K to 100K  | 80 to 500  | 60 to 400  | 100 to 400  |  
| **Mid-tier**  | 100K to 500K  | 500 to 2,500  | 400 to 2,000  | 400 to 1,500  |  
| **Macro**  | 500K+  | 2,500 to 10,000+  | 2,000 to 8,000+  | 1,500 to 6,000+  |  
Product gifting alone works effectively with nano and micro Indonesian creators, particularly outside the beauty and lifestyle categories where creator professionalization is most advanced. Live commerce hosts, a distinct creator category from social content creators, typically charge per-session fees based on the duration of the stream and the product’s price complexity, rather than per-post fees.
### Disclosure Rules
Indonesia has no mandatory legal framework for influencer marketing disclosure, according to [CreatorDB’s Asia influencer regulation analysis](https://www.creatordb.app/blog/influencer-regulation-asia/). Consumer protection laws under YLKI exist but do not specify influencer disclosure requirements. Platform-native tools, including TikTok’s “Branded Content” label and Instagram’s “Paid Partnership” tag, are the primary disclosure mechanisms. Brands working in Indonesia should apply disclosure standards voluntarily rather than reactively, both for brand credibility reasons and in anticipation of regulatory development as the influencer market matures.
One category-specific exception: health product claims made through influencers in Indonesia must comply with BPOM (the Indonesian FDA equivalent) advertising regulations, which restrict certain health claims and require substantiation. Brands in health, beauty, supplements, and medical devices should verify that creator content does not make product claims that exceed BPOM’s permitted advertising scope.
## Thailand: Entertainment-First Creators, Mid-Tier Market, Platform Diversity
### Platform Preferences
Thailand’s platform ecosystem is more diverse than most Southeast Asian markets. Facebook maintains strong reach across all adult demographics. LINE is both a messaging platform and a commerce channel, with LINE Official Accounts enabling direct brand-to-consumer promotions that integrate influencer referral links. TikTok has strong traction among Gen Z and Millennial beauty and lifestyle consumers. Instagram performs well for aesthetics-driven categories. YouTube is the platform for long-form entertainment content and the beauty and lifestyle tutorial categories that have historically been Thailand’s strongest influencer commerce areas.
### Tier Economics and Typical Campaign Costs
Thailand’s influencer market sits at a moderate cost level between Singapore’s premium and Vietnam and Indonesia’s affordable tiers. Bangkok-based creators command higher rates than those based in other Thai cities. Typical per-post rates in USD for Thailand-based creators:  
| Tier  | Follower Range  | Instagram Post (USD)  | TikTok Video (USD)  | Facebook Post (USD)  |  
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |  
| **Nano**  | 1K to 10K  | 30 to 120  | 25 to 100  | 20 to 80  |  
| **Micro**  | 10K to 100K  | 120 to 700  | 100 to 600  | 80 to 400  |  
| **Mid-tier**  | 100K to 500K  | 700 to 3,500  | 600 to 3,000  | 400 to 2,000  |  
| **Macro**  | 500K+  | 3,500 to 12,000+  | 3,000 to 10,000+  | 2,000 to 8,000+  |  
Thai celebrity endorsement carries significant commercial weight and commands premium pricing well above the macro tier benchmarks above. Brands with sufficient budget for a Thai celebrity partnership can compress awareness-building significantly, given how strongly celebrity association influences consumer trust in this market. That said, micro creators with genuine category expertise consistently outperform celebrity partnerships on cost-per-engagement and conversion rate.
### Disclosure Rules
Thailand has no specific influencer marketing regulatory framework. The Consumer Protection Act and National Broadcasting and Telecommunications Commission (NBTC) regulations apply to advertising generally but do not specify influencer disclosure requirements. Platform-native disclosure tools are the standard approach. Brands in financial services, health products, and food should be aware that the Thai FDA and relevant sector regulators apply advertising claim restrictions to social media content in the same way they apply to traditional advertising, making creator brief compliance an important risk management step.
## Vietnam: Fastest-Growing Market, Lowest Rates, High Engagement
### Platform preferences
Vietnam’s influencer ecosystem is anchored by TikTok and Facebook, with Shopee Live as the primary commerce channel and Zalo as the essential messaging and community platform that most non-Vietnamese brands underestimate. TikTok grew exceptionally fast in Vietnam and now dominates discovery content for consumers under 35. Facebook retains broad reach across all demographics and age groups. Zalo, with approximately 74 million Vietnamese users, is where brands that want to build community relationships, send promotional notifications, and run influencer-referred campaigns at a community scale conduct that activity. YouTube has strong reach for tutorial, unboxing, and tech review categories.
### Tier Economics and Typical Campaign Costs
Vietnam offers the most affordable influencer rates in the six main Southeast Asian markets, reflecting both lower income levels and the earlier stage of creator professionalization relative to Singapore and Malaysia. Engagement rates in Vietnam’s influencer market are among the highest in the region, reflecting the high receptivity of the market’s predominantly young, digitally engaged consumer base. Typical per-post rates in USD for Vietnam-based creators:  
| Tier  | Follower Range  | TikTok Video (USD)  | Facebook Post (USD)  | Shopee Live Session (USD)  |  
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |  
| **Nano**  | 1K to 10K  | 10 to 50  | 15 to 60  | 20 to 80  |  
| **Micro**  | 10K to 100K  | 50 to 300  | 60 to 350  | 80 to 350  |  
| **Mid-tier**  | 100K to 500K  | 300 to 1,500  | 350 to 1,800  | 350 to 1,500  |  
| **Macro**  | 500K+  | 1,500 to 6,000+  | 1,800 to 7,000+  | 1,500 to 5,000+  |  
Vietnam’s creator market has a notable bifurcation between Ho Chi Minh City-based creators, who are more commercially professional and charge closer to the upper end of these ranges, and creators based in Hanoi or secondary cities, who often charge significantly less and may have more targeted regional audiences. For brands entering Vietnam for the first time, Shopee Live partnerships with category-specific hosts produce some of the highest conversion-to-cost ratios available in the region.
### Disclosure Rules
Vietnam has no disclosure regulations for influencer marketing. General advertising governance under Decree 70 on commercial advertising applies to digital content but does not specify influencer disclosure requirements, according to [CreatorDB’s regulatory analysis](https://www.creatordb.app/blog/influencer-regulation-asia/). Platform-native disclosure tools remain the only operative mechanism. The absence of regulation does not remove brand responsibility for ensuring creator content is truthful and does not make misleading product claims, particularly in health, food, and financial categories where Vietnamese consumer protection frameworks do apply to advertising claims regardless of channel.
## Philippines: Facebook-First Market, High Engagement, Price-Sensitive Economics
### Platform Preferences
Facebook is the dominant influencer platform in the Philippines and cannot be replicated by TikTok or Instagram for broad demographic reach in this market. The Philippines has one of the highest per-capita Facebook usage rates globally, and Facebook Marketplace functions as a significant commerce channel alongside formal e-commerce platforms. TikTok is growing rapidly among consumers under 30 and is the primary discovery platform for K-pop-adjacent content and beauty. YouTube reaches a broad audience for entertainment, lifestyle, and product review content. Instagram has strong traction in beauty and fashion among urban, aspirational consumers.
### Tier Economics and Typical Campaign Costs
The Philippines offers affordable influencer rates comparable to Indonesia’s, reflecting lower average incomes and an earlier stage of creator market professionalization outside the major cities. Filipino creators are among the most accessible for product gifting arrangements at the nano tier. Typical per-post rates in USD for Philippines-based creators:  
| Tier  | Follower Range  | Facebook Post (USD)  | TikTok Video (USD)  | Instagram Post (USD)  |  
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |  
| **Nano**  | 1K to 10K  | 15 to 70  | 15 to 60  | 20 to 80  |  
| **Micro**  | 10K to 100K  | 70 to 450  | 60 to 400  | 80 to 500  |  
| **Mid-tier**  | 100K to 500K  | 450 to 2,500  | 400 to 2,000  | 500 to 2,500  |  
| **Macro**  | 500K+  | 2,500 to 9,000+  | 2,000 to 8,000+  | 2,500 to 10,000+  |  
Filipino OFW (Overseas Filipino Worker) creator communities represent a distinct and valuable creator segment for brands targeting the diaspora or aspirational consumer segments that associate with the OFW identity. These creators, typically based internationally but producing content for Filipino audiences, command moderate fees and often achieve high engagement by speaking directly to the family remittance, homecoming, and aspirational lifestyle content that resonates most powerfully with their communities.
### Disclosure Rules
The Philippines has no mandatory regulatory framework for influencer marketing disclosure. The Advertising Standards Council (ASC) provides self-regulatory guidelines, and platform-native tools remain the operative mechanism for transparency, according to [CreatorDB](https://www.creatordb.app/blog/influencer-regulation-asia/). Health product claims made through social media creators in the Philippines are subject to FDA Philippines advertising regulations, which restrict therapeutic claims for cosmetics and supplements in a manner similar to Singapore’s HSA framework.
## Malaysia: Multicultural Segmentation, Mid-Range Rates, Growing Halal Creator Niche
### Platform Preferences
Malaysia’s influencer platform mix reflects its multicultural population. Instagram and TikTok are strong across all demographic segments for beauty, lifestyle, and fashion. Facebook retains broad reach particularly among Malay and Indian-Malaysian communities and older demographics. Shopee is the dominant e-commerce platform and its Live feature generates significant commerce conversion for lifestyle and beauty categories. WhatsApp operates as a community commerce and customer communication channel. Xiaohongshu is growing among the Chinese-Malaysian community and is worth including in campaigns targeting that specific segment.
### Tier Economics and Typical Campaign Costs
Malaysia’s influencer market sits at a moderate cost level, above Vietnam and the Philippines but below Singapore. Malaysian Ringgit fluctuations affect USD-denominated budgets, and brands should build currency buffer into campaign planning. Typical per-post rates for Malaysia-based creators:  
| Tier  | Follower Range  | Instagram Post (MYR)  | TikTok Video (MYR)  | Approx. USD Equivalent  |  
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |  
| **Nano**  | 1K to 10K  | 150 to 500  | 100 to 400  | USD 30 to 110  |  
| **Micro**  | 10K to 100K  | 500 to 2,500  | 400 to 2,000  | USD 110 to 550  |  
| **Mid-tier**  | 100K to 500K  | 2,500 to 8,000  | 2,000 to 6,500  | USD 550 to 1,750  |  
| **Macro**  | 500K+  | 8,000 to 25,000+  | 6,500 to 20,000+  | USD 1,750 to 4,400+  |  
Malaysia’s halal creator niche is a distinct and growing segment that brands in food, beauty, personal care, and lifestyle should treat as a priority rather than a specialty. Creators who have built credibility within Malaysia’s Muslim consumer community carry authority that secular lifestyle creators cannot replicate for halal-conscious purchasing decisions. A campaign targeting Malaysia’s Malay Muslim majority that uses only Chinese-Malaysian or secular lifestyle creators is missing the trust channel that most directly influences its target audience.
### Disclosure Rules
Malaysia’s influencer marketing disclosure environment is governed by the Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission (MCMC) Content Code, which operates on a self-regulatory basis. There is no mandatory law requiring specific disclosure language or placement for paid influencer content. Platform-native tools are the operative mechanism. As with other Southeast Asian markets, health and medical device advertising claims made through social media creators are subject to the Malaysian Ministry of Health’s advertising controls, which restrict claims that imply therapeutic benefit for cosmetics and supplements.
## How to Structure a Multi-Country SEA Influencer Campaign
Multi-country influencer campaigns across Southeast Asia work best when they share a strategic brief at the center and then have entirely locally executed creative, creator selection, and campaign management in each market. The center determines the brand message, the product story, and the measurement framework. Each market’s local team or agency partner determines which creators deliver that message credibly to their specific audience.
Budget allocation across markets should reflect commercial priority and market maturity, not equal distribution. A brand entering Southeast Asia for the first time should invest most heavily in Singapore, where creator relationships are the most professionally managed and campaign data is the most reliable. Vietnam and Indonesia offer the highest volume of creator output per dollar spent. Thailand and the Philippines offer mid-range economics with specific category strengths. Malaysia’s multicultural segmentation requires separate creator strategies by community rather than a single national approach.
The disclosure landscape across the six markets is at very different stages of regulatory development. Singapore is the most advanced and tightening, particularly for financial services. The other five markets operate largely on platform self-regulation and brand-initiated compliance. That asymmetry means brands should build Singapore-standard disclosure practices into their regional briefs, not because all markets legally require it, but because consumer trust is the most valuable asset in influencer marketing, and transparency consistently builds more of it than the absence of disclosure ever saves in campaign flexibility.
