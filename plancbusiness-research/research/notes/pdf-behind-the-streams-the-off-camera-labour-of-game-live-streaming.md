## Abstract
The live streaming of digital and analogue gaming has emerged as a major new topic in games research. One element involves understanding the labour of streaming: studies to date have focused on examining the "on-camera" labour of streamers, but what has yet to be examined is their "off-camera" labour. This includes developing stream aesthetics, networking with other streamers and viewers, building communities on other platforms, and management and maintenance activities. These forms of labour are undertaken by those who broadcast for leisure purposes and those who aspire to a part-time or even full-time job in game streaming. Drawing on extensive interview data with professional and semi-professional live streamers, this paper explores off-stream labour, and the demands it makes on game broadcasters. The paper sheds new light on the behind-the-scenes labours and lives of aspirational gaming live streamers, who are collectively becoming increasingly influential in global gaming communities.

## Key takeaways 
  1. The paper explores the extensive off-camera labour of Twitch game streamers, a neglected area in existing research.
  2. Off-camera activities include aesthetics, networking, community management, and day-to-day maintenance tasks.
  3. Streamers invest significant time on activities such as social media management and graphic design.
  4. Over 100 interviews reveal that streamers perceive off-camera work as essential yet often invisible to viewers.
  5. The study suggests off-camera labour influences the personal lives and well-being of streamers, demanding time and emotional investment.
