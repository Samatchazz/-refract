summary: 'US National Endowment for the Arts, NASERC indicator series, Indicator A.7
  ''What Are the Earnings for Artists and Other Cultural Workers?'' (report dated
  May 2025). Source is the US Census American Community Survey for 2015, 2019 and
  2023, all figures in constant 2023 dollars, differences reported only at p<.05.
  This is the FRESHEST nationally-representative US administrative earnings data for
  creative occupations in this batch and supersedes the 2015-2019 NEA brief on recency.
  Crucially, unlike BLS OEWS it ''captures earnings regardless of class of worker
  (private or wage-salary, government, or self-employed)'', so it does include independent
  creators — earnings are wage-salary plus NET self-employment income before deductions.


  THE LOAD-BEARING NUMBER FOR CREATOR-INCOME COMPARISON IS THE PART-TIME MEDIAN. Full-time
  employed artists had median annual earnings of $70,170 in 2023, versus $61,170 for
  all full-time workers (+15%) and $56,040 for non-artist cultural workers (+25%).
  But artists employed PART TIME (under 35 hrs/week and/or under 50 weeks/year) had
  a median of only $20,380 — 3.4 times lower than the full-time artist median, and
  statistically indistinguishable from all part-time workers ($19,330). Non-artist
  cultural workers part-time: $14,830. Since most people who make content do so part-time,
  $20,380 is the far more relevant administrative benchmark than the $70,170 that
  headlines usually quote. The NEA warns that part-time earnings trends should be
  read cautiously given variability.


  TREND: full-time artist median earnings rose 5 per cent between 2015 and 2023 ($66,910
  to $70,170), SLOWER than the 8 per cent rise for all full-time workers ($56,860
  to $61,170). Non-artist cultural workers showed no measurable net change: +10 per
  cent 2015-2019 ($54,370 to $59,760) then -6 per cent 2019-2023 (to $56,040). Earnings
  fell in 2023 for three artist occupations: dancers, producers and architects.


  BY OCCUPATION (2023, full-time): dancers $39,230 at the bottom to architects $99,630
  at the top, with producers $77,250 second. Non-artist cultural workers span $35,200
  (ushers and lobby attendants) to $81,070 (photographic process workers). By education:
  artists with less than high school earned $46,030 versus $38,720 for all similarly-educated
  full-time workers; at every college level artists out-earned non-artist cultural
  workers, but artists with a bachelor''s degree or higher earned LESS than the general
  workforce with the same credential.


  KEY CAVEAT the NEA states first: ''Individuals reporting primary employment as an
  artist or other cultural worker were less likely to be working full time than are
  workers in general.'' Occupation is self-reported PRIMARY occupation only — the
  ACS asks about the job with the most usual hours — so anyone whose creative work
  is a secondary activity is invisible in these counts. US-ONLY; does not generalize
  to Southeast Asia.'

Indicator A.7: Artist Earnings
Key Findings
• 
Individuals reporting primary employment as an artist or other cultural worker were less likely to be working full time 
than are workers in general.
• 
Median annual earnings of artists employed full time increased from $66,910 in 2015 to $70,170 in 2023.
• 
At every level of college education, full-time employed artists earned more than non-artist cultural workers. However, 
while artists with some college or an associate’s degree earned more than all workers with similar education, those with 
a bachelor’s degree or higher earned less than the general workforce.
• 
Median annual earnings of employed artists in 2023 varied by occupation. Earnings for full-time employed artists ranged 
from $39,230 for dancers to $99,630 for architects.
• 
In 2023, median annual earnings for non-artist cultural workers employed full time ranged from $35,200 for ushers and 
lobby attendants to $81,070 for photographic process workers.

Definitions of median earnings and  
household income
Earnings, as defined in the 
American Community Survey (ACS), 
include wage-salary income and 
net income from self-employment 
before deductions (e.g., personal income taxes, 
Social Security, Medicare). An individual with earnings 
has either wage/salary income, self-employment 
income, or both. This indicator uses median earnings 
from the past 12 months from all jobs to compare 
artists and other cultural workers aged 18 and over 
with all workers aged 18 and over. Median earnings 
split the income distribution into two equal groups, 
half with earnings above that amount and half with 
earnings below that amount. Unlike mean earnings, 
median earnings either do not change or change very 
little in response to extreme observations. Household 
income includes the income of the householder 
and all other individuals aged 15 and over in the 
household, regardless of their relationship to the 
householder. For more information, see the ACS 
Technical Documentation.

cultural occupations saw decreases in earnings

no measurable differences in the earnings of non-artist 
cultural workers employed full time for any cultural 
occupation in 2023.
Median annual household 
income of artists and other 
cultural workers
The median annual household incomes of artists 
and other (non-artist) cultural workers were also 
examined. Household income includes the income 
of the householder and all other individuals aged 15 
and over, regardless of their relationship with the 
householder. 
In 2023, the median household income for artists 
was $122,550, or 15 percent greater than that of non-
artist cultural workers ($107,020) and 9 percent greater 
than for all workers ($111,940; see Table A7-1). 
Considering workers by educational attainment, 
the median household income of all workers with a 
master’s degree or higher ($168,220) was 12 percent 
greater than for artists with similar degrees ($150,610) 
and 34 percent greater than for non-artist cultural 
workers with similar degrees ($125,270; Figure A7-6).
