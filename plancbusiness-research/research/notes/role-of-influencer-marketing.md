  directly attributed ~$15bn NMV in 2024 (10% of market); total influenced sales $25-31bn
  (17-21%), forecast 14-18% direct / 19-22% total by 2029. Total e-commerce marketing
  expenditure on influencers reaches ~$5bn in 2024, split influencer fees ~60% (fixed
  fees, sales commission, travel), product sponsorship cost ~25%, agencies/enablers
  ~10%, other ~5% -- implying a regional e-commerce influencer FEE pool of roughly
  $3bn across six countries in 2024 (excludes platform ad-revenue-share and non-commerce
  brand deals). Market concentration: Indonesia $64bn (42%), Thailand $24bn, Philippines
  $19bn, Vietnam $19bn, Malaysia $14bn, Singapore $9bn (2024F).\n\nDirectly relevant
  to income distribution: the report documents payment-in-kind as a standing structural
  feature, stating that influencers ''especially those with a smaller following, often
  receive free products as the sole remuneration for their work,'' and that brands
  routinely seed free product to more than 100 influencers per campaign -- i.e. a
  large share of the SEA long tail is compensated at zero cash. Commission economics
  from a study of 371 TikTok Shop open-affiliate products (August 2024): overall average
  9%, fashion 10%, beauty 9%, electronics 8%; full range 1-35% with 6-12% most common;
  sellers may set 1-80%; TikTok itself deducts a 6.7% commission fee. TikTok live
  streamers receive on average 9% of generated sales as affiliate commission plus
  a fixed hourly appearance fee.\n\nDemand-side context (n=2,400, Aug 2024): 82% of
  SEA consumers report having purchased because of an influencer recommendation (+3pp
  vs 2023); the average consumer follows 23 influencers (down from 26 in 2023); ~60%
  follow 1-20; influencer/celebrity content takes ~one third of social time, 40% on

Fixed versus variable influencer fees

agreed-upon social collaterals, remain the most common way to pay 
influencers; variable fees in the form of sales commissions are

TikTok Shop that are heavily focused on immediate sales conversion

like TikTok Shop leverage a combined ‘hybrid’ model
Product 
sponsorship / 
payment-in-
kind
Influencers, especially those with a 
smaller following, often receive free 
products as the sole remuneration for 
their work
Stable – Sponsorship of free products 
for review is table-stakes for influencer 
marketing; savvy brands often seed free 
product to >100 influencers per campaign
Fixed 
engagement 
fees
Cash fees for specific deliverables, for 
example, a product review with one 
Instagram Post and two Stories; can 
include brand usage rights and 
campaign expenses
Stable – Fixed fees remain the most

guaranteed impact; as agencies and enablers 
attempt to create more transparency about

removing low performers

59
Glossary
#
Vocabulary
Definition
1
Southeast Asia (SEA)
Refers to Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Vietnam,  Indonesia and Philippines in this report
2
Gross Merchandise Value (GMV)
Total value of merchandise that's sold over a given period
3
Net Merchandise Value (NMV)
Gross merchandise value excluding cancelled and returned orders
4
Formal e-commerce
A type of e-commerce where a platform mediates buyer and seller experiences end-to-end, including payment and delivery
5
Informal e-commerce
E-commerce without a platform facilitating payment and/or delivery, such as conversational commerce on WhatsApp
6
Live stream
A real-time transmission of video and audio content that allows viewers to interact with the broadcast
7
Live commerce
A type of live stream where products are featured for sale
8
Influencers
Individuals with a following on social media or other online channels
9
Direct-to-customer (D2C)
A business model where brands sell directly to consumers, bypassing third-party retailers or intermediaries
11
Brand Dotcom
A brand’s official e-commerce website where products are available for purchase
13
Affiliate
An individual or entity that drives sales or leads to a company in exchange for commission payments​
