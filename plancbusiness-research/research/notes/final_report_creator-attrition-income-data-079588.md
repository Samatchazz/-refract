# Content Creation as an Occupation: A Map of the Quantitative Record

The four quantities asked about here are answered unevenly. Attrition has five measurements counting five different units and no platform-scale survival curve. Income distribution is the best measured: the mean runs four to twenty-one times the median wherever both have been computed. Time to first meaningful income has no measured calendar-time distribution on any platform, and no instrument has ever put the reasons for quitting to a sample of creators who quit. Published shares of creators earning a living span 0.043% to 63%, a spread driven mostly by four things each study fixes silently: unit of analysis, denominator scope, income threshold, and observation window. Every figure carries sample size, year, platform and country; section 5 flags geographic scope.

## 1. Attrition and Survival Rates of Creators and Channels

Survival analysis follows a cohort forward from a common start and reports what fraction remains active at each interval. It needs a roster of everyone who started and a rule for when someone has stopped; platforms hold both and publish neither. What exists instead are measurements on **five incompatible units** — persona, channel, crowdfunding campaign, person, funded relationship.

### B. The absence, and who holds the missing data

The missing measurement is withheld rather than unfound. YouTube discloses eligibility rules, revenue splits and aggregate payouts — "over $100 billion to creators, artists, and media companies" across 2022–2025 — but no distribution and no count of monetized channels [[youtubes-impact-on-the-creator-economy-how-youtube-works]] [[powered-by-americans]]. External reconstruction is blocked by design: the YouTube application programming interface exposes only current, never historical, subscriber counts, and the leading commercial alternative lists only the top 5,000 channels with at most three years of history [[tube-census]]. Monetization switches off after six months of silence, a working definition of creator death whose counts are never published [[youtube-partner-program-overview-eligibility-computer-youtube-help]].

Five disclosure channels carry no creator cohort curve:

- **Bilibili** — the 20-F gives twelfth-month retention for users, not up-hosts.
- **Kuaishou** — the prospectus gives monetization and GMV.
- **Roblox** — the S-1 and 10-K give developer counts and payouts with no cohort framing.
- **Patreon** — *State of Create* is cross-sectional.
- **Spotify** — *Loud & Clear* gives the tier retention above, not retention of participation.

Meta publishes no per-creator payout distribution, and no Instagram or Facebook creator attrition or income figure with a stated sample size appears in the academic, telemetry or platform-disclosure record.

One vendor source claiming to be this telemetry is excluded: its title says 97,601 channels against its own body's 105,891 for a single presented 2019 cohort, no analyst or methodology is named, and its 8.8-year median lifespan is the age at death of channels that had already passed 10,000 subscribers [[youtube-channel-lifespan-half-life-study-of-97601-creators-creatormap-creatormap]].

## 2. Income Distribution Across Creators: Median vs Mean and the Living-Wage Share

Creator income is a right-skewed power law: a few very large earners pull the mean far above the median. The ratio between them is the most portable statistic here, surviving currency and scale changes. The *share earning a living wage* needs both a distribution and a threshold, and studies disagree about both — and more consequentially about who belongs in the denominator.

The three Patreon-adjacent medians differ by inclusion floor, not by disagreement: Regner's $31 is the median peak among campaigns that received any money [[vol0123456789-3]]; Strauss and colleagues' $42–77 excludes everyone under $10 a month [[rich-get-richer-platform-attention-and-earnings-inequality]]; Zhao's $127 is a different platform, product and population [[who-reaps-all-the-superchats-a-large-scale-analysis-of-income]].

Four constraints travel with the October 2021 Twitch leak [[twitch-streamer-earnings-increase-for-top-gamers-data-from-hack-shows-wsj]]:

- **896,261 zero- or negative-revenue accounts** were excluded before any percentile was computed.
- **Production companies** sit inside the top ranks.
- **Sponsorships and tips are excluded**, while the $100 payout floor censors the low tail.
- **No country composition** is disclosed.

The much-repeated finding is that **25%** of the top 10,000 highest-paid streamers do not clear US minimum wage: the 8,000th-ranked account cumulated $29,396 since 2019, roughly $14,698 a year against a $15,080 federal minimum. That comparison assumes US costs for a population of unknown nationality [[many-top-twitch-streamers-dont-make-minimum-wage-and-3-new-takeaways-from-the-tw]].

Regner reports the same roughly 250 creators as both the top 1% of active campaigns and 0.18% of all registered campaigns — a **5.75-fold swing** from the denominator alone, numerator untouched [[vol0123456789-3]]. Reading Rieder's published tier table against a US-minimum-wage rather than a US-median-household threshold raises the 0.043% share to 0.43%, exactly tenfold; that recalculation is an arithmetic re-cut of the published tiers by changing the threshold parameter, not a figure the authors state [[httpsdoiorg10117720563051231180628]]. The two levers, each demonstrated within one study's own data, multiply to roughly **58-fold**; the remaining component, sampling frame, is known in direction and unmeasured for income.

These rows must not be averaged, ranged or ranked. Rieder's 0.043% and TikTok's 63% are not two estimates of one quantity: the first divides by every crawled channel including the 31.7 million below the monetization gate that receive none of the ad revenue, the second by surveyed users already reporting the platform as an income source [[httpsdoiorg10117720563051231180628]] [[the-tiktok-effect]].

Two comparison classes survive: within-instrument time series holding the denominator constant, and dimensionless shape statistics — mean/median ratios, Gini, Pareto alpha (the exponent describing how fat a top tail is), hazard ratios. Where the second class has been computed it points one way:

- **Patreon within-topic Gini** rose from 0.30 in 2015 to 0.60 by December 2020 across the complete transaction record, while cross-topic Gini fell from 0.80 to 0.72 after mid-2015 [[quantifying-the-creator-economy-a-large-scale-analysis-of-patreon]].
- **VTuber between-creator Gini** rose from 0.60 to 0.75 between 2018 and 2023 [[who-reaps-all-the-superchats-a-large-scale-analysis-of-income]].
- **Pareto alpha** fell across YouTube, Instagram, Facebook and Twitch between 2018 and 2024, n=104,719 [[rich-get-richer-platform-attention-and-earnings-inequality]].

Two counter-cuts belong beside it: every Patreon earnings decile grew in absolute terms over that window [[quantifying-the-creator-economy-a-large-scale-analysis-of-patreon]], and Twitch's elite tier is near-static in composition rather than churning [[vol0123456789-2]]. Concentration widening across a population is compatible with a stable top.

One summary is defensible, scoped inline: across four telemetry and complete-record datasets covering Patreon and YouTube between 2013 and 2019, **under 2%** of everyone who registered an account or held a channel reached roughly $15,000 a year, falling **under 0.1%** at a US median-household bar, with one bias direction bounded and the other unquantified.

### C. Two measured biases that push in opposite directions

Platform payouts undercount total creator income, and the only clean coverage ratio comes from music: audio streaming royalties are 6% of the average UK music creator's income, all digital sources 14%, live performance alone 31% (n=708, 2019, UK) [[music-creators-earnings-in]]. Off-platform income also concentrates upward, with 60.6% of YouTube channels above 10,000 subscribers using at least one off-platform strategy [[characterizing-alternative-monetization-strategies-on]]; no individual-level platform-to-total ratio exists for any of these platforms.

Registration denominators push the other way. Partner Program enrolment is roughly **45–50%** of eligible channels: 2 million members disclosed in August 2021 against 4,415,180 channels above 1,000 subscribers in the separate 36,336,861-channel 2020 platform census [[youtube-says-its-partner-program-now-has-2-million-members-the-verge]] [[mapping-youtube-a-quantitative-exploration-of-a-platformed-media-system]]. The platform's own disclosure caps that correction: when YouTube retroactively expelled partners in February 2018, 99% of those affected had earned under $100 in the preceding year and 90% under $2.50 in the preceding month — creators who had already cleared the older, looser 10,000-lifetime-view bar [[el-komboz-lena-abou-kerkhof-anna-loh-johannes]]. Combining that disclosure with the enrolment gap implies that narrowing the denominator to everyone past a monetization gate removes fewer near-zero earners than its size suggests — an inference from two sourced claims, not a measured quantity.

### D. Surveys and administrative benchmarks

Cross-sectional academic surveys of active creators land closer to telemetry than vendor surveys do: 45.8% of 377 respondents across 30 countries reported any income, replicated at 46.1% of 382 in a companion study [[fame-and-fortune-or-just-fun]] [[play-playbour-or-labour-the-relationships-between-perception-of]]. US federal microdata puts the wage Gini for independent artists at 0.582 against a national 0.474 [[independent-artists-writers-and-performers-data-usa]], and the roughly 2.8-fold gap between that person count and the payroll count of 61,710 is the self-employment share payroll instruments cannot see [[independent-artists-writers-and-performers-may-2022-oews-industry-specific-occup]].

## 3. Time from Starting to First Meaningful Income

*Meaningful income* has no standard definition, and its three candidates are separately gated: a monetization eligibility rule, which is a behavioural threshold rather than a duration; a payout floor below which accrued money does not move; and, past both, an occupationally meaningful income level. No platform publishes a calendar-time distribution for any of the three.

A channel at the eligibility line earning the modal few dollars a month would need roughly **five to twenty months** of accrual to cross the $100 threshold, before review-queue delay — arithmetic over the disclosed payout floor and the vendor view distribution, measured directly by no source [[meet-youtubes-revenue-thresholds-for-payment-youtube-help]] [[how-much-does-youtube-pay-for-1000-subscribers-in-2026]].

Two independent measurements find output volume and threshold-crossing, not elapsed tenure, to be operative. Across the 36,336,861-channel census, correlation between channel age and success was 0.082 for subscribers and 0.101 for views within the elite tier, collapsing to 0.009 and 0.003 across the full population. Elite channels averaged 940 published videos against 294 for the next tier and average days active were "surprisingly constant between tiers" [[mapping-youtube-a-quantitative-exploration-of-a-platformed-media-system]]. On Twitch, a difference-in-differences design found newcomers reaching only Affiliate status streaming 15.4% less than new Partners and reverting to pre-pandemic levels within four to eight weeks (n=18,467, 2019–2020) [[vol0123456789]].

The circulating timelines have no measured basis: one widely mirrored advice source, selling watch-hour services, asserts one to six months [[how-long-does-it-take-to-get-monetized-on-youtube-with-examples]]; another asserts that "most creators reach this point within 6-12 months of consistent posting" with no sample, cohort or method stated [[when-do-you-start-making-money-on-youtube-how-monetization-really-works]]. The survey figures bearing on timing describe survivors: among Linktree respondents under 12 months in, 59% had not monetized at all and 6% had earned over $10,000 [[pdf-linktree-creatorreport-2022-02f3aa05a27be6fecb3537b13d5ec9depdf]].

### B. What models of actual continuation find

Four designs model persistence rather than feeling, and none finds income level a strong or immediate predictor. Zhao's Cox proportional-hazards model — a survival regression estimating how each variable multiplies the instantaneous risk of exit — returns a **hazard ratio of 1.980** for small-agency affiliation (p=0.003), while stream duration (p=0.207) and gender are not significant. Superchat income shows no significant main effect (β=−0.136, p=0.111) though its interaction with time is significant (p=0.010), making its protective effect small and cumulative rather than absent [[who-reaps-all-the-superchats-a-large-scale-analysis-of-income]]. The Twitch panel finds the Affiliate/Partner status line, not earnings, separating those who sustained effort from those who reverted at identical tenure [[vol0123456789]]. Törhönen's structural model of 377 creators finds intrinsic motivation predicting intention to continue at p=0.000 while extrinsic motivation as a block fails at p=0.052 [[fame-and-fortune-or-just-fun]].

A quasi-causal test points the same way. On the 2017–18 YouTube demonetization shock, a Patreon panel of 11,371 creators and 131,006 creator-months shows the affected population growing rather than shrinking, with multi-homing creators increasing paid content and patron counts relative to Patreon-only controls [[r-a-p-h-a-e-l-a-a-n-d-r-e-s-m-i-c-h-e-l-a-n-g-e-lo-r-oss-i]]. The regression-discontinuity companion — comparing units just above and below a cutoff — finds the complement for creators without an alternate channel. Losing Partner Program eligibility cut uploads by 2.816 per month against a sample mean of 3.248, an **87% collapse in output**, while exiting creators in that German cohort had higher average subscribers and watchtime than those who stayed [[el-komboz-lena-abou-kerkhof-anna-loh-johannes]]. The one behavioural datapoint linking income insufficiency to conduct is UK-specific: among 311 members of UK music trade bodies, **43%** said insufficient streaming income caused them to take a job outside music — diversification rather than exit [[music-creators-earnings-in]]. The threshold results and the shock results jointly imply that income determines who can continue while motivation determines who does; no source states this, and it follows from those four.

### C. The absence, and its near-misses

Two studies come close enough to make the gap precise. A census-weighted US survey of 5,034 podcast consumers reaches a genuinely lapsed creator population — 6% of the base, roughly half the size of the active creator group — and lists "what specific factors drive creator abandonment" as an open question rather than asking it [[the-creators-2025-understanding-the-modern-podcast-creator-landscape-sounds-prof]]. A CSCW study recruited both current and former YouTube creators for 20 interviews on demonetization and contains no mention of quitting, stopping, leaving or burnout in 37 pages [[give-everybody-a-little-bit-more-equity-content-creator-perspectives-and-respons]].

The nearest creator-side statements are unsampled: in the 2023 Bilibili 停更 wave a creator with 1.03 million followers said on 12 January they were stopping because they could not make ends meet, and one with 3.83 million said on 31 March that creating there was unprofitable, with 1,176 viewer comments coded that August [[stop-updating-a-textual-analysis-of-chinese-viewers]] — self-announcements naming income insufficiency, not a sampled exit survey. The closest sampled exit-reason elicitation samples the wrong side of the transaction. Among 1,356 lapsed Japanese Niconico viewers (screening n=3,328, fielded August–September 2021), "creators/streamers moved to other services" was the top or joint-top reason for stopping in every one of six demographic cells, at 29–42% [[why-niconico-in-japan-lost-game-jikkyo-streamers-and-their-audience]].

The most-cited Southeast Asian influencer sector report contains no creator-side survey at all; its only quantified instrument is a demand-side consumer survey of 2,400 people across six markets [[role-of-influencer-marketing]]. Of 133 countries responding to an ILO review, only 40 have ever measured digital platform employment, most of them European [[ilo-brief]].

Two sourced findings jointly imply a third that no study has tested. Persistence models find structural access to monetization infrastructure to be the significant predictor of continuation [[who-reaps-all-the-superchats-a-large-scale-analysis-of-income]] [[vol0123456789]], and that access is systematically worse outside the West, Indonesia and Thailand included [[httpsdoiorg10117720563051241234693]] [[httpsdoiorg10117720563051231180628]]. If both hold, Southeast Asian attrition should exceed Western attrition. No dataset here can test that, and the 51-point intra-regional spread suggests it would not hold uniformly. It is a labelled inference.

## 6. Evidence Quality and Measurement Caveats

The problems here are structural rather than arithmetic: samples that exclude the people the question is about, denominators that shift without notice, and headline statistics whose authority comes from repetition rather than measurement.

### A. Survivorship bias, and its bounded scale

Any sample recruited from active creators cannot observe anyone who already left, and every burnout instrument here is of that type. Institutions concede the constraint: the ILO states there was no sampling base from which a random sample of platform workers could be drawn [[ilo-flagship-report]], and the World Bank found self-identified gig workers the most likely to drop out mid-survey [[the-promise-and-peril]]. The effect is bounded but not calibrated: the clearest paired measurement concerns affiliate-link prevalence, at 36.7% on a dataset restricted to channels above 10,000 subscribers against 3.49% for the same category on a random-prefix sample of roughly 500,000 videos [[characterizing-alternative-monetization-strategies-on]] [[endorsements-on-social-media-an-empirical-study-of]]. That **roughly tenfold gap** shows the mechanism is real and large on a monetization-practice metric; it is not a coefficient for income.

### B. Denominator, reference-period and definitional instability

The Twitch leak's denominator is stated at least six incompatible ways across outlets covering one dataset, and its observation window five ways [[the-unbearable-lightness-of-streaming-superjoost-playlist]] [[many-top-twitch-streamers-dont-make-minimum-wage-and-3-new-takeaways-from-the-tw]]. Reference period alone moves measured platform-work prevalence three- to ninefold: 5.7% of Norway's working-age population over a year, 1.5% over a month [[ilo-brief]]. Threshold changes break cohort comparability outright: YouTube's February 2018 rule change severs comparison with earlier cohorts [[el-komboz-lena-abou-kerkhof-anna-loh-johannes]], and TikTok economics are not comparable across the 16 December 2023 boundary at which the Creativity Program replaced the Creator Fund in four Western markets [[unlocking-even-more-opportunities-for-creators-with-the-creativity-program-beta]].

Sources also differ in unit of analysis (person, persona, channel, account, payroll job), in denominator scope, and in whether income is gross or net. Nearly all of it is gross of the creator's own costs, and the ILO's criteria for a credible platform-earnings figure are met by essentially no published creator income statistic [[ilo-philippines-clarifies-misuse-of-preliminary-platform-work-survey-findings-in]].

The Southeast Asian chain has a survey underneath it and the Western chain does not. The professional count is held flat at 2.0 million from 2022 to 2025 while the total grows by 16.6 million, so every net entrant is an amateur by construction. The definition of professional slides from earning over $100,000 to self-description, and the companion statistic in wide circulation, that 3% of YouTubers take 90% of the wealth, is a unit transformation of a peer-reviewed finding that on average 85% of *views* accrue to 3% of channels [[opus-4-youtube-channels-uploads-and-views-a-statistical-analysis-of-the-past-10]].

The charge against the Indonesian figure narrows accordingly: the 63% rests on a real if thin instrument and is citable with its caveats. The companion 8-million Indonesian creator headcount appears nowhere in the primary report and nowhere in the vendor's own methodology summary [[the-tiktok-effect]] [[tiktoks-socio-economic-impact-in-southeast-asia-a-summary-of-the-report-kadence]]. The failure is the merger: two figures from different sources, years and methods were welded into one statistic at a parliamentary hearing, and then repeated as a national statistic. At that same hearing the official, pressed for Indonesia revenue, stated on record that he did not have the data [[anggota-dpr-cecar-omzet-tiktok-mengaku-belum-siap-data-teknologi]]. Indonesia's statistics agency publishes no creator count at all [[bps-dan-kemenekraf-bersinergi-kreator-lokal-dilibatkan-dalam-sosialisasi-se2026]].

### D. Fabricated apparatus, and aggregates without denominators

Two sources here carry invented scholarly furniture and neither may be cited as evidence. A 2026 trade article attributes a full statistical battery — 62% burnout, 47% considered leaving, n=2,400 full-time creators — to a "Creator Economy Research Institute" that exists in no register, and whose headline figure exactly matches a real n=542 study published three months earlier [[the-creator-burnout-epidemic-why-62-of-full-time-creators-say-theyre-exhausted-t]]. A 2025 Indonesian journal article presents precisely the instrument this question needs — n=60 creators, five cities, a full income-distribution table — while its entire 24-item reference list concerns unrelated physical and medical sciences and its named case-study creators cannot be found under their stated handles [[pengaruh-ekonomi-kreator-digital-terhadap-pendapatan-generasi-z-di-perkotaan-stu]]. Both pass a metadata-only screen; neither passes a reference-list check.

One further figure resists conversion to a per-creator quantity: a widely circulated channel-count composite is described by its own author as manual open-source intelligence assembled from third-party analytics vendors, because YouTube publishes neither figure [[how-many-youtube-channels-are-there-in-2026-tim-queen]].

### E. What becomes measurable next

Three dated developments would change what this question can be answered with:

- A **YouTube channel census** built from 1.21 billion Internet Archive captures, covering at least 106 million unique channels with subscriber histories back to 2006, is public and has not yet carried a survival model — though its coverage bias correlates with subscriber count and would undercount small-channel deaths, where attrition is highest [[tube-census]].
- **Indonesia's 2026 Economic Census** is billed as the first national instrument to enumerate content-creator business actors [[bps-dan-kemenekraf-bersinergi-kreator-lokal-dilibatkan-dalam-sosialisasi-se2026]].
- The **standard due at the 22nd International Conference of Labour Statisticians in 2028** would make national platform-work counts comparable for the first time [[ilo-brief]].

Until one lands, the two highest-value studies here remain unrun: a cohort survival curve over a full channel population, and an exit survey of people who stopped.
