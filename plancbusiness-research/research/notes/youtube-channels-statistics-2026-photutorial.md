YouTube is still the biggest video platform in the world. According to Digital 2026 estimates cited by Sprout Social, YouTube's potential ad reach is now around 2.58 billion monthly active users. Similarweb also ranked YouTube as the top streaming and online TV site, with more than 28 billion visits in April 2026 alone.
The channel side is just as massive, but it's harder to measure. YouTube doesn't publish a complete public census of every channel, so most channel statistics combine YouTube data, public channel pages, Social Blade/vidIQ-style trackers, creator-award estimates, and trend analysis.
## Number of YouTube channels
  * There are roughly **115 million YouTube channels** globally, based on recent creator-stat estimates.
  * More than **10 million channels publish YouTube Shorts daily** , according to reports citing Google CEO Sundar Pichai's 2026 comments.
  * YouTube has about **2.58 billion monthly active users** by ad-reach estimates, but only a small share of users create channels.
  * Channel-count estimates vary because many channels are inactive, empty, auto-generated, brand-owned, or used only for subscriptions/comments.

## YouTube channel earnings
YouTube earnings are harder to estimate than subscribers or views. A channel's revenue depends on niche, geography, video length, ad inventory, sponsorships, merchandise, memberships, licensing, and off-platform businesses.
As a rough rule, a YouTube channel is often valued at about **24 to 36 times monthly profit** , not monthly revenue. A channel making $1,000/month in profit might sell for roughly $24,000 to $36,000, but the multiple can be higher for stable evergreen channels and lower for channels dependent on one personality or trend.
Ad revenue also varies widely. Long-form YouTube channels often earn a few dollars to tens of dollars per 1,000 monetized views, depending on the audience and category. Finance, software, business, and education channels usually earn more than entertainment, gaming, or broad Shorts-heavy channels.
### Highest-earning creator
MrBeast is still the highest-earning YouTube-first creator. Forbes ranked him No. 1 on its 2025 Top Creators list with estimated earnings of **$85 million** and 634 million followers across platforms. Dhar Mann ranked second with **$56 million** , followed by Jake Paul at **$50 million**.
That doesn't mean those numbers came only from YouTube ads. Top creators now run media companies. Their income usually combines AdSense, sponsorships, consumer products, licensing, subscriptions, live events, and platform deals.
