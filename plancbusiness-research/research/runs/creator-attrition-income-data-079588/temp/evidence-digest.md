## Settled ground — assert these without hedging  `[consensus]`

Independence computed from the step 2.6 redundancy audit, which traced every figure to its INSTRUMENT. Outlets recirculating one survey count as one voice.


**[consensus · 6 independent instruments · high]** Creator income distributions are extreme right-skewed power laws in which the mean is several times the median, on every platform where both statistics have been measured.

  - Strauss, Yang & Mazzucato 2026, official Patreon data, n=104,719 sampled Mar 2018/2021/2024: mean is 4-6x median on every platform examined
  - Zhao et al. 2025, n=1,923 VTubers, YouTube, 2017-2023: mean $2,667/month against median $127/month, a ratio of 21
  - Rieder et al. 2023, 36,336,861 YouTube channels against Google's disclosed $8.25bn 2019 payout: per-channel averages span $1,707,446/year (10M+ subscribers) down to $295 (1k-10k) and $0 (sub-1k)
  - Houssard et al. 2023, Scientific Reports, Twitch: Gini 0.57 across the top 10,000, extrapolating to approximately 0.93 across all 9.2 million streamers
  - Twitch October 2021 payout leak: 81 accounts cleared $1M while rank 8,000 cumulated $29,396 over the whole window
  - UK Intellectual Property Office 2021, n=708 UK music creators: 37% earned £5,000 or less and 62% £20,000 or less in 2019, against a small tail above £50,000

**[consensus · 5 independent instruments · high]** A majority of people who attempt content creation earn zero or near-zero from it.

  - Regner 2021, complete Patreon transaction record 2013-2015, n=139,366 campaigns: 115,183 (82.6%) never received a single pledge
  - Rieder et al. 2023: 31,727,531 YouTube channels under 1,000 subscribers receive 0% of channel revenue
  - Zhao et al. 2025: 25% of sampled VTubers received zero Superchats
  - Törhönen et al. 2019, n=377 and n=382 independently: only 45.8% and 46.1% respectively earn ANY income — and this is the optimistic figure, from a survey of active creators
  - Kässi, Lehdonvirta & Stephany 2021: of 163 million registered online-labour accounts, only 2.0% completed ten or more projects or earned $1,000 or more

**[consensus · 5 independent instruments · high]** Samples recruited from currently-active creators systematically overstate income and understate attrition. The direction and approximate scale of this bias are well evidenced; its exact magnitude for income specifically has never been measured.

  - The sampling-frame mechanism is directly measured, though on a different quantity: Hua et al. 2022 report 36.7% affiliate-link prevalence in Howto & Style descriptions on a popularity-gated sample (YouNiverse, channels >=10,000 subscribers) against 3.49% from Mathur, Narayanan & Chetty 2018 on a random-prefix sample of ~500K videos — roughly tenfold, from sampling frame alone. This bounds the mechanism's plausible scale; it is NOT a coefficient for income and must not be applied to income figures as if it were
  - ILO states of its own platform-work instrument that 'there was no sampling base from which a random sample could be drawn'
  - World Bank found self-identified gig workers were the most likely to drop out mid-survey; a footnote reveals permanently-deactivated workers were only reachable because they had moved to another platform
  - Wollborn et al. 2023 concede a selection floor even in their Twitch telemetry panel (one hourly top-500 placement, approximately 21 concurrent viewers)
  - Every burnout instrument in the corpus (Censuswide/BDB n=1,000; Awin n>300; Vibely n=150; Creators 4 Mental Health n=542) recruited exclusively from active creators and therefore cannot observe anyone who already left

**[consensus · 4 independent instruments · high]** No major platform publishes a creator-level earnings distribution. Aggregate payout totals are disclosed without the denominator needed to convert them into per-creator figures.

  - YouTube support and blog documentation discloses eligibility thresholds, payout mechanics and revenue splits, but no median payout, no earnings distribution, and no count of monetized channels
  - YouTube's 'over $100 billion 2022-2025' is stated for creators, artists and media companies combined, with no split and no headcount; its 2 million YPP figure (2021) was disclosed with no payout total
  - Twitch's creator-level distribution became public only through a data breach, not disclosure; the company declined to confirm the leaked tables
  - TikTok Indonesia declined to disclose its Indonesia revenue when directly asked at a DPR Komisi I hearing on 15 July 2025
  - KPMG's $210bn 2024 content-spend figure covers 12 US companies with no creator-count denominator anywhere

**[consensus · 4 independent instruments · high]** Widely-circulated creator-economy statistics frequently have no traceable methodology, and several trace to no original study at all.

  - The Goldman Sachs creator-population figure traces backwards through Zippia (an SEO careers listicle) to SignalFire (a venture-capital blog, c. 2020); Goldman's own exhibit source line names Zippia
  - The '3% of channels take 90% of the wealth' statistic is a transformation error: Bärtl 2018 measured 85% of VIEWS going to 3% of channels
  - A 'Creator Economy Research Institute' is credited with a Q1 2026 study of 2,400 full-time creators and, separately, an audit of 14,200 TikTok creators across 11 countries. No such organisation is findable; its headline 62% exactly matches the real n=542 Creators 4 Mental Health study published three months earlier
  - The frequently-quoted Twitch percentile figures ($120, $28, 0.06%) could not be pinned to any citable source across an entire dedicated fetch batch
  - '73% of creators quit within their first year' is a corrupted restatement of an Awin burnout-frequency figure and measures nobody quitting

**[consensus · 3 independent instruments · high]** Living-wage thresholds used in Western creator research are roughly an order of magnitude above Southeast Asian equivalents, so 'share earning a living wage' is not a transferable quantity.

  - ADBI: developing-Asia living wage of USD 254.28/month (absolute) and USD 414.88/month (moderate), family of four, 48-hour week
  - Western creator studies benchmark against the US federal monthly minimum wage of $1,160, a $15/hour equivalent, $50,000/year, $100,000/year, and the US median household income of $67,521
  - UK IPO benchmarks against the UK minimum wage of £8.21/hour
  - Indonesian provincial UMR levels sit far below all of the above, making a 63%-above-UMR figure non-comparable with any Western living-wage share

**[consensus · 3 independent instruments · high]** Income concentration among creators has increased measurably over the past decade, confirmed independently across three platforms and three research groups.

  - Patreon within-topic Gini rose from 0.30 to 0.60 between 2015 and 2020 (El Sanyoura & Anderson, ICWSM)
  - VTuber between-creator Gini rose from 0.60 to 0.75 between 2018 and 2023 (Zhao et al.)
  - Pareto alpha fell across YouTube, Instagram, Facebook and Twitch between 2018 and 2024 (Strauss, Yang & Mazzucato, official Patreon data n=104,719)
  - _Caveat:_ Houssard et al. 2023 find the Twitch elite tier internally stable (weekly ranking dropout flux 0.2 for the top 100). This is compatible rather than contradictory: the top can be stable while the gap between top and bottom widens.

**[consensus · 4 independent instruments · high]** Southeast Asian creator populations are effectively unmeasured. The region's institutional data covers platform gig work, which is a different population from content creation.

  - ILO: of 133 countries responding to a review, only 40 have ever measured digital platform employment, mostly EU/EFTA, 17 of them within a single Eurostat pilot; no international statistical standard will exist before the 22nd ICLS in 2028
  - Indonesia's BPS publishes no content-creator count; its 'creative economy workforce' series (27.4 million in 2025) is a substantially broader category, and BPS states the 2026 Economic Census will be the first instrument to make content-creator business actors visible
  - Indonesian administrative measurement of adjacent categories is itself unstable: SAKERNAS 2019 counts 12.2 million online sellers while SUSENAS March 2019 counts 5.9 million — same country, same year, a factor of 2.07 apart
  - Indonesia's nationally-representative gig-work data (SAKERNAS 2019) describes a population that is 85% men, 87% urban and 69% in transportation — ride-hailing drivers, not creators
  - The impact.com/Cube Southeast Asia influencer report contains no creator income, attrition, survival or time-to-income data; its only quantified instrument is a demand-side consumer survey (n=2,400)

**[consensus · 3 independent instruments · high]** Reaching a platform's monetization eligibility threshold does not correspond to reaching meaningful income.

  - vidIQ analysis of channels at 1,000-1,100 subscribers: 89% have under 5,000 monthly views, a volume at which ad revenue is negligible regardless of Partner Program status
  - Rieder et al. 2023: channels in the 1,000-10,000 subscriber band average $295/year
  - Twitch's Affiliate gate requires only 25 followers, 4 hours streamed, 4 distinct days and 3 average concurrent viewers within a 30-day window; Twitch's own disclosed aggregate implies approximately $143 per streamer per year
  - YouTube additionally requires a $100 balance before any payment is issued, so eligibility and payment are separated by a further threshold

**[consensus · 2 independent instruments · medium]** Revenue per view varies by a factor of roughly five to six depending on audience geography, independently of creator skill or output.  ⚠️ **attribute rather than assert**

  - Peripheral Creator Cultures study (India n=45 creators, Ireland n=7, Turkey n=9, interviews 2017-2023): 1,000 views at 70% inventory fill yields INR 30 (under USD 1) from an Indian audience against USD 5 from a US audience
  - The same study documents a Turkish channel drawing 10% of its views but 30% of its income from Germany
  - Rieder et al. 2020: Indonesia and Brazil achieve far fewer views than their channel share predicts
  - _Note:_ Two independent instruments rather than three, and the primary source is a qualitative interview study reporting a quantitative figure. Assert with attribution rather than as settled ground.

**Evidence delta:** Side B is decisively stronger. It is administrative and telemetry data (complete transaction records, platform-disclosed payouts, leaked payout tables, a government-commissioned study with published microdata). Side A is entirely self-selected survey, and in Goldman's case not a survey at all but a citation chain terminating in a venture-capital blog post. Side A's own internal disagreement is severe: NeoReach and Linktree, same year and same quantity, report 43% versus 12% above $50k.

**Scope:** PARTIALLY scoped differently — and the scoping is the mechanism. Some of the gap is therefore denominator, not disagreement. It demonstrates that popularity-gated sampling inflates prevalence estimates roughly tenfold on a DIFFERENT quantity; it is legitimate evidence that the mechanism is real and large, but it is NOT a measured coefficient for income or living-wage share and must never be presented as one. No study has directly measured the sampling-frame effect on creator income. The residual disagreement after denominator and threshold are accounted for therefore remains estimated rather than measured.


### [contested] Do creators stop because the money is insufficient, or because intrinsic motivation runs out — and does income predict survival at all?

**Side A — Financial insufficiency is the dominant driver of exit. Creators say so themselves when asked.**

  > Censuswide for Billion Dollar Boy (Jul 2025, n=1,000 creators, 500 US / 500 UK): among creators who suffered burnout, financial instability ranked first by severity at 55%, above creative fatigue, workload and screen time
  > Ivors Academy / Musicians' Union (n=311): 43% said insufficient streaming income caused them to take a job outside music
  > Creators 4 Mental Health / Lupiani Insights (n=542, North America, Nov 2025): 69% report financial insecurity

  Sources: [over-half-of-creators-face-burnout-action-urged-billion-dollar-boy], [music-creators-earnings-in], [in-sweeping-study-89-of-creators-claim-to-lack-access-to-specialized-mental-heal]

**Side B — Income does not predict who keeps going. The variables that actually survive a statistical model of continuation are intrinsic motivation and structural affiliation — not earnings.**

  > Törhönen et al. 2019 (Internet Research, n=377): intrinsic motivation predicts intention to continue at p=0.000, while income and every other extrinsic motivator fail to reach significance (p=0.052)
  > Zhao et al. 2025 (n=1,923 VTubers, Cox proportional-hazards): affiliation status is the significant predictor of exit (HR 1.980 for small-agency, p=0.003), while gender and hours streamed are NOT significant predictors of survival
  > Wollborn et al. 2023 (Twitch difference-in-differences panel): newcomers who reached only affiliate status reverted to pre-pandemic effort within 4-8 weeks, while those reaching partner sustained it — a structural threshold effect, not an earnings gradient

  Sources: [fame-and-fortune-or-just-fun], [who-reaps-all-the-superchats-a-large-scale-analysis-of-income], [vol0123456789]


**Evidence delta:** Side B is methodologically stronger where it counts. Side A is self-reported attribution collected cross-sectionally from people who have NOT quit — it records what active creators believe would make them quit, which is a different quantity from what actually causes exit. Side B contains the only two studies in the corpus that statistically model continuation or survival. However Side B's samples are narrower (one Finnish-led survey, one VTuber population drawn from a fandom wiki), so it is stronger in design and weaker in generality.

**Scope:** GENUINE DISAGREEMENT, and it is the sharpest one in the corpus. The two sides measure different constructs that are routinely reported as if they were the same: stated reasons for burnout among survivors, versus modelled predictors of actual exit. Both cannot be uncritically reported as 'why creators quit'. This is the dialectical core of the quit-reasons deliverable.


### [contested] How many creators stop, and how soon — is the median career two years or nearly nine?

**Side A — Attrition is fast and majority-scale: most creators are gone within two to three years.**

  > Zhao et al. 2025 (n=1,923 VTubers, YouTube, 2017 to Jul 2023): median career 24 months; 1,057 of 1,923 (over 50%) stopped streaming entirely within 3 years; among those who quit, the median was 19 months. By affiliation: 44 months large agency, 28 months independent, 21 months small agency
  > Regner 2021 (complete Patreon record, n=139,366 campaigns): 115,183 (82.6%) never received a single pledge — failure at entry rather than exit
  > Kässi, Lehdonvirta & Stephany 2021: of 163 million registered online-labour accounts, 8.6% ever worked and 2.0% completed ten or more projects or earned $1,000+

  Sources: [who-reaps-all-the-superchats-a-large-scale-analysis-of-income], [vol0123456789-3], [how-many-online-workers-are-there-in-the-open-research-europe]

**Side B — Attrition is slower and less total than the pessimistic figures suggest; a large share of channels persist for years and activity rates are far higher than 8.6%.**

  > creatormap.io: of 97,601 independent YouTube channels tracked 2019-2026, 46.1% went dark, 25.4% are 'fading', 21% still growing, with a median channel lifespan of 8.8 years
  > World Bank (n=503 platforms, traffic-proxy model): mean active share 37%, median 26% — roughly four times the ILO/Online Labour Observatory 8.6% figure

  Sources: [creatormap-youtube-channel-survival-rate-study], [the-promise-and-peril]


**Evidence delta:** Side A is far stronger. Zhao is a peer-reviewed survival analysis with a Cox model; Regner is a complete transaction record, not a sample. Side B's headline figure (creatormap) is a marketing-blog 'study' whose methodology, sampling frame, author and data source require verification and which carries invented-looking precision. The World Bank / ILO apparent conflict is NOT a real contradiction and was resolved during fetching: 36.8% is the highest single-platform value inside Kässi's own observed range of 2.0%-36.8%, so the World Bank traffic-proxy estimate simply sits at the top of that range. Kässi's authors decline to publish a confidence interval precisely because the spread is so wide.

**Scope:** MOSTLY scoped differently, in a way the report must state rather than resolve. 'Median lifespan 8.8 years' counts channel existence; 'median career 24 months' counts active production. A channel can persist as an artifact for years after its creator stopped. Additionally: online-labour marketplace accounts are not content-creator accounts. The deeper finding is that NO peer-reviewed platform-scale cohort telemetry exists for creator survival — three targeted academic sweeps returned nothing — so this fight cannot currently be settled with good evidence, and saying so is itself a required finding.

## Standing rules for the drafter

1. **Never present a survey figure and a telemetry figure as a range.** They describe different populations. State the population with the number.

2. **Every figure carries four fields** — sample size, year, platform, country. A figure that cannot carry all four is annotated as incomplete, not presented as if complete.

3. **Seven outlets reporting 52% is n=1,000, once.** Cite the instrument, never the outlet that repeated it.

4. **Two fabricated sources were caught in this run** — a phantom "Creator Economy Research Institute" credited with two different studies, and a 2025 Indonesian journal article whose reference list is unrelated STEM material. Neither may be cited. Their existence is itself a reportable finding.

5. **Date-label the core telemetry.** Regner is 2013–2015, Rieder is 2019, the Twitch leak is 2019–2021. TikTok economics are not comparable across the 16 December 2023 Creator Fund to Creator Rewards boundary.

6. **Southeast Asia is not one condition.** The recovered nine-country survey spans Thailand at 36% to Laos at 87% on the same question, fielded at the same time by the same instrument.
