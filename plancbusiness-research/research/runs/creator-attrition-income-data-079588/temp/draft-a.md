# Content Creation as an Occupation: A Catalogue of Measured Findings

**Executive summary.** The measurable record on content creation as an occupation is thin, and where it exists it points one way. **On attrition:** no platform publishes a creator survival curve and no peer-reviewed study tracks a cohort to exit at platform scale. The four real measurements measure four different units — a median career of 24 months, over half of 1,923 VTubers stopping entirely within three years (YouTube, 2017–July 2023, global) [[who-reaps-all-the-superchats-a-large-scale-analysis-of-income]]; ~46% of an already-monetized German YouTube cohort gone in about three years (December 2017 to September–November 2020) [[el-komboz-lena-abou-kerkhof-anna-loh-johannes]]; 82.6% of 139,366 Patreon campaigns never receiving a pledge (2013–2015) [[vol0123456789-3]]; 32% churn among everyone who ever started a podcast (n=5,034 US consumers, fielded 2025) [[the-creators-2025-understanding-the-modern-podcast-creator-landscape-sounds-prof]].

**On income:** every dataset where both statistics exist shows a mean several times the median — 4–6× on Patreon across 104,719 creators (March 2018/2021/2024) [[rich-get-richer-platform-attention-and-earnings-inequality]], 6.4× in Regner's complete Patreon record [[vol0123456789-3]], 21× among VTubers [[who-reaps-all-the-superchats-a-large-scale-analysis-of-income]]. **On the living-wage share:** measured payout data puts it between 0.043% and ~1.75% of everyone who registered an account or holds a channel, at US subsistence thresholds; self-selected surveys reporting 12–63% measure a pre-filtered population and must not be averaged with telemetry. **On time to first income:** the quantity is not well posed. What is measurable is a two-gate structure — an eligibility threshold, then a separate US$100 payout floor [[meet-youtubes-revenue-thresholds-for-payment-youtube-help]] — plus the finding that elapsed tenure does not distinguish successful channels from unsuccessful ones (correlations of 0.009 and 0.003 across 36,336,861 channels) [[mapping-youtube-a-quantitative-exploration-of-a-platformed-media-system]]. **On quit reasons:** no instrument anywhere has asked a departed creator why they left. **On geography:** the record is almost entirely US, UK, Nordic, German or global-platform-with-no-country-split; Southeast Asia has one traceable multi-country survey, three thin national studies and zero attrition data.

## 1. Attrition and Survival Rates of Creators and Channels

### A. What has actually been measured

The four-field requirement exposes the problem immediately: these measurements do not share a unit of analysis. Person, persona, campaign, channel and registered account are five denominators, and pooling them yields a number describing nothing.

One row is routinely misread. The Kässi activation rates come from freelancing marketplaces, not content platforms, and rest on activity data for only seven platforms — the authors decline to publish a confidence interval and give an observed range of 2.0%–36.8% instead [[how-many-online-workers-are-there-in-the-open-research-europe]]. The World Bank's competing 37% mean / 26% median active share across 503 platforms therefore sits at the top of that observed range rather than contradicting it, and finds the distribution bimodal: 174 platforms in the 0–20% active band, 180 in the 80–100% band [[the-promise-and-peril]].

### B. The absence, and who holds the missing data

No major platform publishes a creator-level survival or earnings distribution. YouTube discloses eligibility thresholds, revenue splits and aggregate payouts — "over $100 billion to creators, artists, and media companies" across 2022–2025, a pool with no headcount attached — never a distribution or a count of monetized channels [[youtubes-impact-on-the-creator-economy-how-youtube-works]] [[powered-by-americans]]. The infrastructure reason is documented: the YouTube API exposes only current, never historical, subscriber counts, and the leading commercial alternative lists only the top 5,000 channels with at most three years of history [[tube-census]]. The one genuine platform-disclosed cohort figure is Spotify's — eight in ten artists who crossed the US$100,000 royalty threshold in 2022 stayed above it every year through 2025, a cohort of roughly 12,400 — but that is retention *within an earnings tier among the already-successful*, not participation among everyone who started [[takeaways-loud-and-clear]]. YouTube nonetheless operationalises exit internally, disclosing that monetization may be switched off on channels silent for six months or more [[youtube-partner-program-overview-eligibility-computer-youtube-help]]: a continuously applied definition of creator death whose resulting counts are never published.

### C. Closest documented analogues, in unpaid populations

Two literatures have produced the survival methodology this field lacks, on unpaid populations. English Wikipedia good-faith newcomer survival — at least one edit two to six months after the first session — fell from 35.7% (first half of 2004) to 11.3% (second half of 2010), on 100 randomly sampled newcomers per six-month period, 2001–2011 [[researchnewcomer-quality-meta-wiki]]. Debian shows 121 of 216 package maintainers (55.8%) still contributing 6.5 years on, implying a cohort half-life near 7.5 years [[evolution-of-volunteer-participation-in-libre-software-projects]]. Neither transfers as a rate, but both remove "inherently impossible" as an explanation for the creator-economy gap.

### D. Concentration is worsening while the top is stable

These two findings are compatible, and sharper together. Across the whole population, inequality has risen: Patreon within-topic Gini from 0.30 (2015) to 0.60 (December 2020) on the complete pledge record [[quantifying-the-creator-economy-a-large-scale-analysis-of-patreon]]; VTuber between-creator Gini from ~0.60 (2018) to 0.75 (2023) [[who-reaps-all-the-superchats-a-large-scale-analysis-of-income]]; Pareto exponents falling on YouTube (1.86→1.97→1.80), Instagram (2.09→2.18→1.84) and Facebook (2.12→2.07→1.94) between 2018 and 2024 [[rich-get-richer-platform-attention-and-earnings-inequality]]. Within the elite tier, composition is nearly frozen: Twitch weekly ranking flux of 0.13–0.20 resembles word-frequency stability in natural language (English 0.13) rather than social datasets such as GitHub repositories (0.65) [[vol0123456789-2]]. The joint reading, which neither source states alone: incumbency at the top persists while the distance between top and bottom widens, so the expected value of entry falls without the top becoming a lottery redrawn each year.

The Twitch October 2021 payout leak sits apart, and four constraints travel with every figure from it. The *Wall Street Journal* excluded 896,261 zero- or negative-revenue accounts from approximately 4.9 million before computing anything, so its percentiles describe *earning accounts*: 75% under US$120 and 50% under US$28 for 2021 through September, 0.06% above the 2020 US median household income of US$67,521, 5% above US$1,000 [[twitch-streamer-earnings-increase-for-top-gamers-data-from-hack-shows-wsj]]. The unit is the account, not the person — organisations sit inside the top 100. It excludes sponsorships, tips and publisher payments, and the US$100 payout floor censors the low tail. And it discloses no country composition, so every minimum-wage comparison drawn from it assumes US costs for a population of unknown nationality — a caveat the *Washington Post* stated while reporting that 25% of the top 10,000 highest-paid streamers do not clear US minimum wage, the 8,000th-ranked account cumulating US$29,396 since 2019 (≈US$14,698/year against a US$15,080 federal minimum) [[many-top-twitch-streamers-dont-make-minimum-wage-and-3-new-takeaways-from-the-tw]]. Platform denominators: US$889m paid January–September 2021 against US$519m in 2020, 8.1 million active streamers in September 2021, revenue mix 56% subscriptions, 20% Prime, ~9% advertising [[the-unbearable-lightness-of-streaming-superjoost-playlist]].

The arithmetic inside single datasets is the point. Rieder's own table moves 0.043% to 0.43% purely by changing the threshold [[httpsdoiorg10117720563051231180628]]; Regner's own paper moves 0.18% to 1.03% purely by changing the denominator [[vol0123456789-3]]. Those two levers — ~10× and ~5.75×, both on identical underlying data — account for much of the apparent hundredfold telemetry-versus-survey disagreement without any appeal to measurement error.

### C. Vendor surveys, labelled as such

Two self-selected vendor surveys fielded in the same year on the same nominal quantity report answers 3.5× apart. NeoReach/Influencer Marketing Hub found 43% of creators above US$50,000/year and 70% full-time, from an influencer-agency roster [[creator-earnings-breakdown-the-creator-economy-today]]; Linktree found 34% full-time, 12% of full-timers above US$50,000, 46% of self-identified full-timers under US$1,000/year, and 66% part-time (n=9,576 of its own users, 95% completion, fielded 2021, country composition never disclosed) [[pdf-linktree-creatorreport-2022-02f3aa05a27be6fecb3537b13d5ec9depdf]]. Neither measures the creator population; the gap is the size of the recruitment effect. NeoReach discloses no sample size, fielding date or country anywhere, and is cited only as that illustration.

Cross-sectional academic surveys of active creators land closer to telemetry: 45.8% of 377 respondents across 30 countries reported any income (Finland 38.6%, USA 32.3%, fielded 2017) [[fame-and-fortune-or-just-fun]], replicated at 46.1% of 382 in the companion study, where reported income standard deviations exceeded group means by 2.6× to 4.6× [[play-playbour-or-labour-the-relationships-between-perception-of]].

### D. Administrative benchmarks

US federal microdata supplies the only nationally representative earnings measurements. Full-time employed artists had median annual earnings of US$70,170 in 2023 against US$61,170 for all full-time workers, but artists employed **part time** had a median of US$20,380 — 3.4× lower, and statistically indistinguishable from all part-time workers (constant 2023 dollars, American Community Survey) [[artists-and-other-cultural-workers]]. Only 59.8% of US artists worked full-year full-time against 66.0% of all workers, falling to 23.5% for actors and 32.6% for musicians (ACS 2015–2019, >119,000 unweighted) [[office-of-research-analysis]]. The wage Gini for independent artists, writers and performers is 0.582 against a US national 0.474 (ACS 2024, n=172,376) [[independent-artists-writers-and-performers-data-usa]], while the same industry group counts only 61,710 on the employer-reported payroll measure [[independent-artists-writers-and-performers-may-2022-oews-industry-specific-occup]] — the ~2.8× gap is the self-employment share.

The UK Intellectual Property Office study is the strongest government-commissioned instrument in the record: of 708 UK music creators, 37% earned £5,000 or less in 2019, 47% under £10,000, 62% £20,000 or less, 9% above £50,000; among the 197 for whom music was the sole income source, 43% earned £20,000 or less against a minimum-wage-equivalent annual salary of £17,076. Medians ran £51,816 (major label) to £12,944 (self-releasing), and £20,160 (men) to £13,057 (women). Streaming royalties were 6% of average income against 31% for live performance [[music-creators-earnings-in]].

## 3. Time from Starting to First Meaningful Income

### A. The quantity resolves into three separate gates

"Meaningful income" operationalises three ways, and the three are close to decoupled.

| Gate | Threshold | Source type | Platform | Country | Date |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Eligibility | 1,000 subscribers **and** either 4,000 valid public watch hours in 12 months **or** 10 million Shorts views in 90 days; Shorts Feed hours excluded from the 4,000 | Platform-disclosed | YouTube | Global (available countries) | Current as of July 2026 [[youtube-partner-program-overview-eligibility-computer-youtube-help]] |
| Review | Decision "typically in about 1 month"; appeal within 21 days, reapply after 30, then 90 after repeat rejection | Platform-disclosed | YouTube | Global | Current [[youtube-partner-program-overview-eligibility-computer-youtube-help]] |
| Payout | Five sequential thresholds; money moves only at the **US$100 payment threshold** | Platform-disclosed | YouTube | Global; only IDR (Rp1,300,000) and SGD (S$150) listed for Southeast Asia | Current [[meet-youtubes-revenue-thresholds-for-payment-youtube-help]] |
| Income floor | No platform reports a calendar-time-to-income-floor distribution | Audited absence | All | All | — |

Eligibility does not confer participation: YouTube disclosed 2 million Partner Program members in August 2021 [[youtube-says-its-partner-program-now-has-2-million-members-the-verge]] [[new-features-community-updates-for-2022-youtube-blog-youtube-blog]] against a crawl count of 4,415,180 channels above the 1,000-subscriber floor [[mapping-youtube-a-quantitative-exploration-of-a-platformed-media-system]] — roughly 45–50% enrolment among the eligible, and neither figure carries a payout total.

### B. Elapsed tenure does not predict success; output volume does

Across the 36,336,861-channel crawl, correlation between channel age and success indicators is 0.082 (subscribers) and 0.101 (views) within the elite tier, collapsing to 0.009 and 0.003 across the full population; elite channels average 940 published videos against 294 for the next tier down, while average days active are "surprisingly constant between tiers" (2019–2020) [[mapping-youtube-a-quantitative-exploration-of-a-platformed-media-system]]. Wollborn's Twitch difference-in-differences replicates the mechanism at identical tenure: both newcomer cohorts raised streaming time 22.7% at lockdown onset, but newcomers reaching only Affiliate status streamed 15.4% less than new Partners and reverted to pre-COVID levels within roughly 4–8 weeks, while new Partners sustained the increase (n=18,467 streamers, 3,329,042 hourly top-500 observations, August 2019 – July 2020) [[vol0123456789]]. A status threshold separates them, not a duration.

### C. Two figures that bound the entry population

In a random video-level YouTube sample (n=10,016 videos, 9,997 channels, late 2022), hand-coding of 1,000 videos found both coders agreeing visible monetization was present in 0.21% of cases (upper bound 2.98%), the median posting channel had **4 subscribers** against a mean of 55,100, and mean views were 5,868 against a median of 35 [[journal-of-quantitative-description-digital-media-32023-185]]. YouTube's own 2018 disclosure caps how much of the near-zero mass is merely non-attempting accounts: of creators expelled by the February 2018 threshold change — who had already cleared the older 10,000-lifetime-views bar — 99% had earned under US$100 in the preceding year and 90% under US$2.50 in the preceding month [[el-komboz-lena-abou-kerkhof-anna-loh-johannes]]. Narrowing the denominator raises the share; it does not remove the mass. The best-documented floor-crossing figure remains Regner's: roughly 250 of 139,366 registered campaigns (0.18%) reached an income the author calls sufficient to live on [[vol0123456789-3]].

## 4. Documented Reasons Creators Give for Quitting

### A. The population mismatch, stated before any figure

Every instrument here samples creators who have **not** quit and asks them to introspect about burnout or a hypothetical departure. No instrument in the record has asked a departed creator why they left. The closest design — a nationally weighted US survey genuinely reaching a lapsed creator population (6% of a 5,034-respondent base, half the size of the active creator base) — never asks that subgroup its reasons, and its authors list creator-abandonment drivers as an open question [[the-creators-2025-understanding-the-modern-podcast-creator-landscape-sounds-prof]].

### B. Burnout prevalence and stated intention among active creators

| Instrument | Prevalence | Quit intention | n | Fielded | Population | Country | Type |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Censuswide for Billion Dollar Boy [[over-half-of-creators-face-burnout-action-urged-billion-dollar-boy]] | 52% burnout | 37% "actively consider leaving" | 1,000 creators (500 US / 500 UK), plus 1,000 marketers | July 2025 | Active creators, commercial panel | US, UK | Agency-commissioned survey |
| Creators 4 Mental Health / Lupiani [[in-sweeping-study-89-of-creators-claim-to-lack-access-to-specialized-mental-heal]] | 62% burnout; 69% financial insecurity; 89% lack specialised mental-health resources; 10% report work-related suicidal thoughts | Not asked | 542 full- and part-time | Published Nov 2025 | Active creators | North America | Advocacy-adjacent survey |
| Vibely [[90-of-content-creators-deal-with-burnout-vibely-wants-to-change-that-tubefilter]] | 90% burnout | 71% considered quitting | 150 (45.3% at 100k–500k followers) | November 2021 | Active creators | Not stated | Vendor survey, incomplete metadata |

The 52% figure recirculated by at least seven outlets is one instrument, n=1,000, once. Its full methodology — Censuswide as fielding agency, July 2025, 500 per market — is disclosed only in secondary write-ups, not in the sponsor's own release [[over-half-of-creators-face-burnout-action-urged-billion-dollar-boy]].

### C. Stated causes do not agree across instruments

Billion Dollar Boy reports creative fatigue 40%, demanding workloads 31% and screen time 27% as most-cited causes, but financial instability ranked first *by severity* at 55% among those who experienced burnout — most-cited and most-severe are different variables on different denominators [[over-half-of-creators-face-burnout-action-urged-billion-dollar-boy]]. Vibely's top stressor is algorithm/platform change at 65%, "need to make a living" second at 59% [[90-of-content-creators-deal-with-burnout-vibely-wants-to-change-that-tubefilter]]. In a Latin American and Caribbean platform-worker survey (n=1,153, 21 countries, published 2025) no named risk approaches a majority: financial insecurity 13%, internet issues 11%, lack of growth opportunities 10% [[survey-on-workers-on]]. Money as the leading exit driver is the result of one instrument, not a replicated finding.

### D. What actually predicts continuation

The two studies that model continuation behaviour rather than self-reported feeling both find a categorical structural variable doing the work. Zhao's Cox proportional-hazards model returns a hazard ratio of 1.980 for small-agency affiliation (p=0.003) while total stream duration (p=0.207) and gender are not significant, and total Superchat income has no significant main effect (β=−0.136, p=0.111), only a small time interaction (p=0.010) [[who-reaps-all-the-superchats-a-large-scale-analysis-of-income]]. Wollborn's Affiliate/Partner divergence at identical tenure is the second [[vol0123456789]]. Törhönen's structural equation model (n=377, 2017) finds intrinsic motivation predicting intention to continue at p=0.000 while extrinsic motivation as a whole fails at p=0.052 and no individual extrinsic construct reaches significance; income predicts weekly hours (p=0.046) in a model explaining 2.1% of variance in hours [[fame-and-fortune-or-just-fun]].

A quasi-causal test of the income-shock hypothesis points the same way. On the 2017–18 YouTube Adpocalypse, a Patreon panel of 11,371 creators and 131,006 creator-months shows the affected population *growing* rather than shrinking, with multi-homing creators increasing paid content and patron counts relative to Patreon-only controls [[r-a-p-h-a-e-l-a-a-n-d-r-e-s-m-i-c-h-e-l-a-n-g-e-lo-r-oss-i]] — creators with access to an alternate channel reallocated instead of exiting. The regression-discontinuity companion finds the complement for those without it: losing Partner Program eligibility cut uploads by 2.816 per month against a sample mean of 3.248 (~87%) and keyword diversity by 25.38 against a mean of 41.62 (~61%) — a collapse in output, not a measured exit [[el-komboz-lena-abou-kerkhof-anna-loh-johannes]]. In that same German cohort, creators who exited had *higher* average subscribers and watchtime than those who stayed, and a smaller share had lost Partner Program access.

Stating this as an explicit inference with its provenance: the Zhao and Wollborn results (structural threshold predicts persistence) combined with the Andres/Rossi and El-Komboz results (income shocks change allocation and output, not membership) jointly imply that income determines who *can* continue while motivation determines who *does* among those who can. No source states this; it follows from those four.

### E. Non-Western near-miss evidence

The closest exit-reason elicitation anywhere is non-English and samples the wrong side of the transaction: a Dynata survey of lapsed Niconico viewers (screening n=3,328, main survey n=1,356, stratified by sex × age-decade, fielded 24 August – 7 September 2021, Japan) found "creators/streamers moved to other services" the top or joint-top quit reason in every one of six demographic cells, ranging 29% to 42% — but the respondents are viewers, not creators [[why-niconico-in-japan-lost-game-jikkyo-streamers-and-their-audience]]. Nothing comparable exists for Southeast Asia.

The most-cited Southeast Asian influencer sector report contains no creator-side survey at all: its only quantified instrument is a demand-side consumer survey (n=2,400, 400 per country across six markets, June–August 2024) [[role-of-influencer-marketing]]. Institutional coverage confirms the vacuum: of 133 countries responding to an ILO review, only 40 have ever measured digital platform employment, mostly in Europe and Central Asia, and no international statistical standard will exist before the 22nd International Conference of Labour Statisticians in 2028 [[ilo-brief]]. For Asia-Pacific the ILO states plainly that "there is no systematic data collection across the region" on platform workers [[x-opportunities-and-challenges]].

### C. The living-wage threshold does not transfer

The living wage for developing Asia is estimated at US$254.28/month on the absolute poverty line and US$414.88/month on the moderate line, assuming a family of four and a 48-hour week (published April 2019, 2018 World Bank poverty lines) [[adbi-working-paper-series]]. Western creator studies benchmark against a US federal monthly minimum of ~US$1,160, a US$50,000/year bar, or a US$67,521 median household income — a gap of roughly 3× to 22×. "Share earning a living wage" is therefore not a transferable quantity, and the nine-country chart makes the point internally: Thailand at 36% and Laos at 87% came from the same instrument, fielded at the same time, on the same question [[the-tiktok-effect]]. The counterfactual differs too — informal employment exceeds 70% of non-agricultural employment in Indonesia and the Philippines, exceeds 60% in Vietnam and is 38% in Thailand [[adbi-working-paper-series]] — so creator self-employment in Southeast Asia is not the departure from a salaried norm that it is in the US.

### D. A labelled inference, and the study nobody has run

Zhao and Wollborn establish that structural access to monetization infrastructure predicts persistence [[who-reaps-all-the-superchats-a-large-scale-analysis-of-income]] [[vol0123456789]]. Rieder et al. establish that non-Western creators are over-represented among channels with no monetization linking at all and face thinner alternative-monetization infrastructure [[httpsdoiorg10117720563051231180628]]. Those two sourced claims jointly imply that Southeast Asian creator attrition should exceed Western attrition. **No data exists to test this.** It is offered as an inference with its evidentiary status attached, not a finding, and it identifies the highest-value unrun study in the field.

## 6. Evidence Quality and Measurement Caveats

**Survivorship bias is the dominant failure mode.** Any sample recruited from active creators cannot observe anyone who already left, and every burnout instrument in the record is of this type [[over-half-of-creators-face-burnout-action-urged-billion-dollar-boy]] [[in-sweeping-study-89-of-creators-claim-to-lack-access-to-specialized-mental-heal]] [[fame-and-fortune-or-just-fun]]. Institutions concede it: the ILO states there was no sampling base from which a random sample of platform workers could be drawn [[x-opportunities-and-challenges]], and the World Bank found self-identified gig workers the most likely to drop out mid-survey [[the-promise-and-peril]]. The sampling-frame effect has been directly measured only on a different quantity — 36.7% affiliate-link prevalence on a ≥10,000-subscriber dataset against 3.49% on a random-prefix sample, roughly tenfold — which bounds the mechanism's scale but is **not** a coefficient applicable to income.

**Denominator instability is second.** The Twitch leak's denominator is stated at least six incompatible ways across outlets covering one dataset (2.4M / 4.9M / 7M / 8.1M / 8.8M / 9M), and its observation window five ways [[the-unbearable-lightness-of-streaming-superjoost-playlist]] [[many-top-twitch-streamers-dont-make-minimum-wage-and-3-new-takeaways-from-the-tw]]. Reference period alone moves platform-work prevalence by a factor of three to nine — 5.7% of Norway's working-age population over a year, 1.5% over a month [[ilo-brief]].

**Provenance chains, judged by one standard.** The widely-quoted global creator population runs SignalFire (venture-capital blog, c. 2020) → Zippia (SEO listicle, 2023) → Goldman Sachs (2023 and 2025) → global press; Goldman's own exhibit source line names Zippia, its professional count is held flat at 2.0 million while the total grows from ~50 to ~67 million, and its 2025 footnote defines the professional split by self-definition with no income threshold [[with-this-report-we-assess-the-current-state-of-the-creator-economy-including]]. The Indonesian chain resolves the opposite way: the 63% figure, chased to its primary, rests on a real if thin survey [[the-tiktok-effect]], while the "8 million creators" count appears nowhere in that report and was separately attributed to unaudited internal data as of December 2023 [[63-percent-of-tiktok-creators-earn-more-than-minimum-wage]]. The failure is a merger of two figures from different sources, years and methods into one statistic, at the 15 July 2025 hearing where the same official, pressed for Indonesia revenue, answered on record that he did not have it [[jdih-setjen-dpr-ri-jaringan-dokumentasi-dan-informasi-hukum-sekretariat-jenderal]]. Applied uniformly, the standard leaves the Western figure in worse condition than the Indonesian one.

**Two fabricated sources were identified during this work** and neither may be cited as evidence: an organisation credited with two separate studies that has no findable existence, whose headline burnout figure exactly matches a real n=542 study published three months earlier; and a 2025 Indonesian journal article claiming an n=60 creator-income survey whose entire 24-item reference list consists of unrelated STEM papers. Their existence is itself a finding about the field's information environment.

**Comparability rules that follow.** Two comparison classes hold across this record: within-instrument, within-platform time series with a fixed denominator; and dimensionless shape statistics — mean/median ratios, Gini, Pareto alpha, hazard ratios — which are scale- and currency-invariant. Absolute income levels, living-wage shares and attrition rates from different instruments must never be pooled, ranged or averaged. Period labels are load-bearing: Regner is 2013–2015, Rieder is 2019 revenue, the Twitch leak is 2019–2021, and TikTok economics are not comparable across the 16 December 2023 boundary at which the Creativity Program fully replaced the Creator Fund in the US, UK, France and Germany [[unlocking-even-more-opportunities-for-creators-with-the-creativity-program-beta]]. Fee changes must be sized before being called structural: Substack's 10% cut is unchanged, and the only dated change is a payment-processor billing fee moving 0.5% to 0.7% in July 2024 [[how-much-does-substack-cost-substack-inc]].

**What becomes measurable next.** Three instruments in flight change what can be answered by 2030. Indonesia's 2026 Economic Census is described by its own statistical agency as the first to make content-creator business actors visible, which would give Indonesia — not any Western country — the world's first nationally representative creator denominator [[bps-dan-kemenekraf-bersinergi-kreator-lokal-dilibatkan-dalam-sosialisasi-se2026]]. The ILO working group reports an international platform-work standard to the 22nd ICLS in 2028, settling centrally whether content creation sits inside or outside "platform work" [[ilo-brief]]. And a channel census built from 1.21 billion Internet Archive captures, covering at least 106 million unique YouTube channels with subscriber histories back to 2006, is already public [[tube-census]]: nobody has run a survival model on it, but the data-access obstacle that blocked creator survival analysis for a decade no longer applies. The consequential near-term development is therefore not a finding but a denominator — the population base against which every share statistic catalogued here would have to be recomputed.
