# Draft angles — step 10

Format `structured` (target ~3,500 words, middle of the 2,000–5,000 band).
Modality `collect`, register `survey`, citation style `wikilink`.

This topic has sharp, well-documented tensions, but the deliverable is
enumerative rather than argumentative. The angles below are therefore a hybrid:
they differ in *epistemic posture toward the figures* rather than in thesis,
which is what will actually produce three different drafts for a collect-modality
query.

---

## Draft A — Maximum figure density

Report every measurable finding the corpus supports, each carrying its four
required fields (sample size, year, platform, country), organised strictly under
the six required headings. Where the corpus contains a defensible number, print
it; where it contains only an absence, state the absence in one sentence and move
on. This draft optimises for a reader who wants the complete inventory of what has
actually been measured, and it should read as a well-fielded evidence catalogue,
not an argument.

Bias to guard against: uncritical accumulation. Even this draft must label vendor
surveys as vendor surveys and never place a survey figure beside a telemetry
figure as if comparable.

## Draft B — The skeptical audit

Take seriously the strongest version of the position that almost none of the
field's headline figures should be believed, and that an honest report on this
question is mostly a report about absences and provenance failures. Lead with what
the numbers are made of: the SignalFire→Zippia→Goldman chain, the merged Indonesian
8-million/63% statistic, the two fabricated sources caught in this run, the
disqualified creatormap survival study, the fact that seven outlets reporting 52%
is one survey of a thousand people, and the fact that no instrument has ever asked
a departed creator why they left. Then report the small number of figures that
survive audit.

This is the steelman of the minority position, and it should be argued forcefully
rather than hedged — the synthesiser needs a strong version to work against.

## Draft C — Boundary conditions and the comparability crosswalk

Argue that the apparent chaos in the numbers resolves once each figure is placed
against its unit of analysis, denominator, threshold and window — and build the
report around that discipline. This draft's organising device is the matrix plus
the non-comparability crosswalk: the same underlying reality produces 0.043% and
63% depending entirely on scope, and showing exactly how is more useful than
picking one. It should also carry the two opposing measured biases (off-platform
income undercounting versus registration-denominator inflation) and the finding
that Southeast Asia is not one condition — Thailand at 36% against Laos at 87% on
the same instrument.

This draft is where the reader learns what they can legitimately conclude and
what they cannot.
