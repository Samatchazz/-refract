COMPANION STUDY to the same team's HICSS-52 'Play, Playbour or Labour?' paper, drawing on the same 2017 fieldwork: N=377 respondents from 30 countries, distributed via Reddit, Twitter, Facebook, direct messaging on Twitch and YouTube, and an email list of active creators, with a prize draw for a USD 65 product as incentive. The Relationships between Perception of — Toerhoenen, Hassan, Sjoeblom & Hamari (2019), HICSS-52 proceedings pp.2558-2567 — cross-sectional online survey of N=382 video content creators (YouTubers and Twitch/Vidme streamers), fielded 2017, recruited through Facebook groups, subreddits and direct outreach via platform messaging (self-selected sample of ACTIVE creators; survivorship bias understates attrition and overstates income). Geography: Finland 149 (39%), US 122 (31.9%), Other 111 (29.1%) — no Southeast Asian representation. Demographics: 73.6% male, 43.0% aged 18-24, 35.5% students, 33.7% employed full-time, 16.3% unemployed. HEADLINE INCOME FINDING: only 174 of 382 respondents (46.1%) reported ANY income from content creation; 208 (53.9%) reported none. Respondents were split by whether they perceive the activity as work, play, or both, then compared by one-way ANOVA. Work-oriented (n=48): 25.00 h/week producing content, 11.88 h/week promoting on other social media, 34.21 months tenure, mean total income $774.85 (SD $1,999.79). Playbour/'equally work and play' (n=66): 20.74 h/week, 10.91 h/week, 33.95 months, mean income $145.76 (SD $587.66). Play-oriented (n=268): 13.28 h/week, 6.55 h/week, 38.67 months, mean income $179.11 (SD $818.15). Income differences significant (F=7.444, p=0.001; Tukey work-vs-playbour p=0.003, work-vs-play p=0.001); production-hours differences significant (F=11.240, p<0.001). CRITICALLY FOR TIME-TO-INCOME: tenure differences across groups were NOT significant (F=0.801, p=0.405) — how long someone had been creating did not predict earnings, and the longest-tenured group (38.67 months, play-oriented) earned less than the shortest-tenured (34.21 months, work-oriented). Standard deviations exceed means by 2.6x, 4.0x and 4.6x respectively, documenting extreme right-skew in creator earnings. Authors note the 25 h/week production figure excludes promotional, administrative and organisational work, so even 'part-time' creators approach half a standard working week. Well-being was not measured. `evergreen`
