Recruitment via ESOMAR-compliant consumer panel plus industry organisations. 84% identified as white; women under-represented (matching the known gender composition of musicians, so no correction applied). 82% had started their career before 2015, 71% before 2011. ONS median full-time UK salary (April 2020) was GBP 31,461. The authors conclude 'many musicians who gain all their earnings from music were working at around or below the minimum wage.'\n\nMEDIANS BY SUBGROUP: women GBP 13,057 vs men GBP 20,160 (2019). By label relationship: currently signed to a MAJOR label GBP 51,816; previously signed to a major GBP 25,500; signed to an INDEPENDENT GBP 20,250; self-releasing GBP 12,944.\n\nWHERE THE MONEY COMES FROM (average share of music income, pre-pandemic): live performance 31% - by far the largest single source; audio streaming royalties 6%; physical sales 6%; downloads 5%; video streaming 3%. Digital combined is 14%.\n\nSTREAMING CONCENTRATION (October samples 2014-2020, UK): the top 0.1% of TRACKS took more than 40% of all streams in every year; the top 0.4% took more than 65% from 2016; the top 1% took 75-80%; the top 10% took 95-97% (2016-2020).
